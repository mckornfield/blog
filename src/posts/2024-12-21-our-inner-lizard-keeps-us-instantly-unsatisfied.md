I saw a set of lizards scurrying over the Mayan ruins in Uxmal and was reminded, as they sunned themselves, that we share a pretty important part of our brains with them, and it’s a part that lets modern companies effectively prey on us. Let’s heed the warnings of our lizard brain buddies.

### A Tale of the Three Brains

[Simon Sinek understands the way humans think.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4ZoJKF_VuA&vl=en) He might lump Apple in with MLK in the linked talk, but I think his overall understanding of the human psyche is pretty spot on. We’re a layered creature: an automatic, instinctual system at the base, an emotional middle, and a [social/rational outer layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triune_brain).

These three brains equate to the brains of creatures we share ancestors with. The innermost brain often termed our “lizard” brain. While we can lambast the lizard (haha they have those weird eyes that move on their own), the lizard is a species that has [spread everywhere except Antartica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizard). This literal lizard brain paired with a highly adapted and unique skillset is a winning recipe for survival.

The next brain is our mammal brain; lizards and their kind are egg layers (figure it out yourself, kids!) who are adapted for killing and procreating, but not much else (lizards == men?).

Instead, mammals have nurturing qualities and social functions that allow them to cooperate and raise their young, creating a level of interaction that extends beyond the “here’s my genes, good luck” strategy of the lizard.

Lastly we have the primate brain, our big frontal lobes. This is where humans and primates stand apart from dogs or other mammals. This is complex social hierarchies, tool usage, communication techniques. This is “rationality.”

These brains have different names in different contexts, like Freud has the Id, Ego and SuperEgo that map pretty well to them.

I like to think of them in Simon’s definition as the Why -> Lizard, How -> Mammal, What -> Primate brains.

And these brains are not all equal. They were forged in different times and have different influences on our well being. None of these brains evolved during a time of “add to cart” or “social media”, or even older things like a literal slot machine or Playboy magazine.

These brains are used to experiencing things that would be the difference between death and procreation, they are driven by survival and emotion at their core.

Our lizard brains control us more than we think they do, and they are not prepared for our brave new world.

And ultimately, letting our lizard brains determine our actions leaves us feeling worse than ever.

### Which Neurotransmitter Makes You Satisfied?

🎶Four neurotransmitters went out one day, over the hills and far away🎶

(OK I have been hearing too much toddler music recently, forgive me.) [Here’s a breakdown of the neurotransmitters](https://www.parkinsonsnsw.org.au/four-happy-hormones/#:~:text=Endorphins%20Primarily%20helps%20one%20deal,Oxytocin%20Bonding%20%E2%80%93%20Love%20and%20trust) we have in our brains terms of “happiness” or satisfaction

- Dopamine — Do More
- Serotonin — Just Be
- Endorphin — Keep Going
- Oxytocin — Be With Others

I think you can also line these up as a spectrum. In the view of folks like Mr. Sinek, oxytocin sits at one end, forged from hard work and sacrifice in a group setting, whereas dopamine sits on the left side, helping us form habits and repeat behaviors, which can be constructive or *destructive*.

### All Play and No Work Makes Jack a Depressed Boy

Modern companies are essentially built around “dopamine hijack.” These are a list of items that engage the lizard brain really well, but don’t produce any tangible rewards beyond “play” or “entertainment.” In my experience, they are at best, a poor imitation of their equivalents.

- Video Games: Point systems, achievements, rewards
- Social Media: Notifications, bright colors, algorithmically engaging content, virtual slot machines
- Pornography (yes OnlyFans counts): Basic attraction and visually stimulating content
- Food: Extreme amounts of sugar and carbs, bright and catchy packaging
- Gambling: Brightly colored, loud slot machines, enticing rewards, opulent presentation

These are all perversions of higher cost, communal things that humans used to engage with, a list might look like:

- Team sports or board games
- In person socialization
- Sex with a long term partner
- Cooking and eating tasty, satisfying food
- Pursuing purposeful, meaningful and rewarding work

The division comes from the fact that the second category, while taking more effort, and triggering less dopamine (therefore engaging the lizard brain less) instead triggers more oxytocin. Physical activities are more likely to release endorphins, as an added bonus.

That being said, this isn’t an article saying “the first category of things totally sucks”, but rather that we are way more incentivized to do the easier, more dopamine focused activities than their oxytocin heavy equivalents. While each set of items produces dopamine, the second set produce significantly more oxytocin and endorphins.

I wrote about this before, so I won’t repeat everything, but the cost of oxytocin and endorphins are *work* and *grit.* But it is worth the cost.

[A Good Life Embraces Friction](https://mckornfield.medium.com/a-good-life-embraces-friction-3e7376c475f9)

Letting the lizard get what it wants makes a person who doesn’t ultimately move forward, instead letting video games and social media steal their time and attention, pornography and food steal their ability to enjoy the world properly, and gambling to piss away their money.

[And it makes us miserable.](https://stanfordreview.org/young-men-in-crisis/)

### Summary

Lizards have survived and so have we, but we must not forget that we thrived not by embracing a bunch of easy, isolating and ultimately activities, but by suffering with other people.

I mentioned earlier in a joking sense that men are just lizards, and in some sense I think they *more* embody that aspect of our psyche. Other than social media, the above set of dopamine hijack items more negatively affect [men way than their female counterparts.](https://stanfordreview.org/young-men-in-crisis/)

We must remember that, while the lizard is the *why*, the thing keeping us alive, we can’t let it drive the ship. The other parts of our brain, our mammal and primate centers, need to step in and steer us towards tasks that, while more difficult, will ultimately leave us more satisfied.

Conversely, holding the companies to task that prey on us and our lizard brains might be another way to fight back.

Hopefully we can burn the candle at both ends.
