### Big Brother, Little Brother

I joined BBBS a year ago. I probably won’t repeat it, because it takes a ton out of me, even for the short time I spend on it each month. This is not because my Little is tough to be around; he’s a good kid.

Rather the opposite; the people that are supposed to fill my Little’s inner circle are not there for him. His grandmother(s) have had to step in. At his 18th birthday, it was me, his sister, and his grandmother. Me, a perfect stranger as of a year ago, but someone who’s willing to show up. Experiencing this secondhand really takes it out of me.

I am not a knight in shining armor; I’m a person with many of my own flaws, and my experience with my father was much the same as my Little’s. But I wanted to write a short letter to my Little (whose name I won’t use for his privacy). And I hope this letter says something to you if you are a young man or have similar experiences.

### Dear Little

There will never be anything to right the wrongs of your life that will appear out of the blue. Your dad and mom won’t show up newly minted heroes, showering you with riches and opportunities. Instead, I suspect they will try and pull you into the world they occupy, one of taking and not giving.

Please do not let them.

[My own dad is a taker](https://mckornfield.medium.com/a-letter-to-my-dad-be06a7f609d8), which does not make him by any means unique among parents. To the contrary, I think most people are unfortunately selfish. Either by having a brain that cannot conceive of the suffering of others, or just by putting others needs *after* their own.

The world is full of people who put on their own oxygen mask on, then gawk at you as you struggle to put your own. Between the onslaught of screens, promises of fortunes for no effort, absent fatherhood, and the reality of ladder pulls by previous selfish generations, being young right now f — king sucks.

I think there is a false narrative that we’re safer, freer, and richer than ever before; this is a highly localized set of positive statements, to older people of specific geography, ethnicity and taker mindsets.

Young men are adrift, and no one is coming to help them put on their oxygen mask.

I used porn and video games regularly through high school, college and into early adulthood. The salve to the lack of a relationship, lack of anything interesting in my academic or post academic career, was to stuff my brain with dopamine highjacking s — t.

We each carry this hole, made wider or smaller by the amount of love given by our parents and close guardians. Kids need their parents, because figuring out what to do with this hole is a lifelong endeavor.

To my Little, I know that your parents use alcohol and drugs to fill that hole, as did much of my mom’s side of that family. Spoiler alert: it doesn’t work, though it does make the pain distant. We might consider our porn and video games somehow different, but it’s another salve that rots us from the inside out, while not letting us address the real issue.

No one is coming to save you. No amount of school aid, or financial aid or government mandated support can make you a man. Those things just make you a kid for longer.

I definitely cannot make you a man; I am no fairy godmother. And if I could, I wouldn’t. You have to walk your own path.

What changed me as an adult? I found a woman for me and found out that I needed to change to meet her. I can only hope the same for you, but I think you can start to change before that time as well.

Trying to be physically fit, pursue interests that make yourself better, give yourself a healthy creative outlet, and putting yourself in unfamiliar circumstances are all great ways to cross the chasm into manhood.

A few weekends ago we made a film together, and I saw your creative side on display. You have talent and interest; I think you can be a good creator. You probably already are, but to break out of the “100s/1000s of views” as you described it, you need to chuck your Nintendo out the window and pour your whole self into getting better at your craft.

I know you’re spreading yourself into many creative endeavors, and have dreams of all sorts of content and things you can make. At your age, that’s totally acceptable.

But if you want to stand out, you need to put more wood behind fewer arrows. Pour yourself wholly into a few things, and spend less time consuming content. Be a creator, a builder.

The line between a man and a boy is fine. A man makes, a boy takes. My father, and I believe your parents, are more on the taker side than the giving side. They were there for your graduation (as was my own dad), because they get to *take* the win and celebrate (look, *my* son graduated), but they weren’t there for your birthday, because they don’t want to *give* you the love that you deserve. And that sucks.

The only revenge you can take against the takers in your life and your country is to tell them to go f — k themselves: by building your own family and your own brand.

Making money, becoming skilled, and using it to make a better version of yourself.

“[Heroes aren’t born, they’re built.](https://www.reddit.com/r/Avengers/comments/wuuabu/iron_man_heroes_arent_born_theyre_built_comicbook/)” The same with men. The same with you.

And because you have to pull on your own oxygen mask, you’re going to have to work harder than a silver spoon kid like myself.

Don’t let that deter you.

Don’t fall into the trap of filling the hole with things that make you more of a consumer and continuing to corrupt yourself.

Fill it with writing, singing, playing guitar, filming, editing, creating and expressing yourself. Fill it with getting better and better. [Make yourself](https://mckornfield.medium.com/make-yourself-9e54dd831d80).

You got this.

-Matt
