The seven sins are deadly, but not equal. I want to lay out why I think sloth is the *most dangerous*.

The other 6 sins are much more obvious:

*wrath, envy, lust, greed, pride and gluttony*

are all actions i.e., the sin is the result of doing something. Envy might be the most subtle, but it still represents an action of sorts, even if it’s one that results in theft or coveting.

Sloth stands alone; while the other sins are ones of action, sloth is definitionally inaction.

### If everyone were a sloth

There is an actual animal named after the term “sloth,” but they just move quite slowly. True sloth, as the dictionary says, is “reluctance to work or make an effort; laziness.”

The best examples of sloths I’ve read about at a community level are [hippie communes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/russellflannery/2021/04/11/what-happened-to-americas-communes/). The only reason they ever come into existence is some rich person’s seed money, but once the hippies, through inaction, fail to feed themselves and make any money, the communes fall apart.

Sloth in the regular world comes from anyone who takes more than they give.

- A coworker who never seems to get any work done, but spends a lot of time discussing it.
- A spouse or partner who promises but “money is tight” or, “it won’t happen this week babe, sorry”.
- A politician who… I mean finish that any way you want.

Another way to think of Sloth: people doing what benefits themselves only, and doing nothing or the absolute minimum for others. But you could include “their future self” in that list of others.

Resting, playing, or mindlessly scrolling are all forms of sloth when they are replacements for work that would benefit oneself or others (say: exercise, child care, a job).

The worst form of sloth is where people spend their time arguing against doing work, or make a big show about how every thing is standing in their way.

This is modern victim culture, rife with oppressors that, if it weren’t for them, we would all be self actualized superheroes.

Or really?

### The Antidote

Sloth is the shortcut. It’s the pill, the crash course, the excuses.

The opposite of sloth is diligence. The road must be walked, without going around or cutting through.

In my life, if you don’t do your work, sooner or later people will figure it out. You can only be a parasite or free loader in good times, but in bad times you’ll be the first person people are going to cut ties with.

The antidote to this parasitism is “doing the work”. That can take many forms, such as

- Taking care of your own life
- Taking care of your finances
- Taking care of your health
- Taking care of your relationship

### Efficiency vs. Effectiveness

In my life, people who are highly effective, &.prioritize putting a lot of effort into a small number of things, are much less of a problem than people who do many different things but in a highly “efficient” manner.

Efficiency means executing quickly, but not necessarily in a way that benefits anyone else, while effectiveness is about achieving the right outcome, and understanding that it might take more time to do so.

The best anecdote I’ve come across for this is in *The* *Seven Habits of Highly Effective People*. The author/dad recounts trying to get his son to take care of the lawn. After a quick walkthrough of the equipment and responsibilities, *nothing happened* for a few weeks, no lawn cutting, just grass getting longer.

Both father and son felt shame disappointment.

Once the dad got over himself and talked through with his son what was going on, his son needed to be shown *exactly* what to do.

These approaches mirror the efficiency vs. effectiveness. While efficiency saves time in the short term, it doesn’t guarantee success in the long term. When both the student and the teacher act from a place of minimizing their time on a task, they maximize being ineffective, though highly efficient.

### The Danger of Sloth

Part of why sloth is so dangerous is that, only a very small percentage of what humans in a modern economy do result in life or death scenarios. So much of modern jobs are acts of bureaucracy and blame shifting.

When work is left undone, if it is unimportant, nothing will force it to be done. And so sloth can just chill, unperturbed.

The danger of sloth is not that all mothers will turn lazy and let their kids die, or that farmers will ignore their crops and flocks, and we all starve.

The danger is in a slow, downward spiral, where otherwise productive, effective individuals are replaced with blame shifting parasites, whose contribution to the world is a consumption of resources they did nothing to earn.

Sloth has two ultimate conclusions: you are excluded from your community or workplace, or your community or workplace suffers to support you. But compared with the other sins, this cost can be invisible, and appear insignificant.

### Appearance is Reality

The other real danger of sloth is that it is a very subjective sin. Managers as well as ICs can dress up what little work they do in such trappings as to appear productive while ultimately providing little value to company or customers.

Sloth is a sin that hides well among “blockers” or “extenuating circumstances.” In a world with so many things getting in the way of work, being unproductive is an external problem.

But part of your or anyone’s job is to accomplish things despite things getting in their way.

That means talking to people, and valuing effectiveness over efficiency.

The phrase “do fewer things better” is the antithesis of sloth: pruning back you work and personal obligations to a small set that you do very well.

To quote Bruce Lee (someone who was anything but a sloth):

> I fear not the man who has practiced 10,000 kicks once, but I fear the man who has practiced one kick 10,000 times.
