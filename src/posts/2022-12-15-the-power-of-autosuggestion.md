### Clarifying the meaning

In [*Think and Grow Rich*](https://www.amazon.com/Think-Grow-Rich-Landmark-Bestseller/dp/1585424331)*,* the author Napoleon Hill brings up the term “autosuggestion”. Having only heard this term refer to when a computer helps you write something (like a snippet of code, or part of an email), I was a bit put off. This book was written in 1937, so clearly we weren’t talking about the same thing.

But autosuggestion, by this book’s definition, isn’t what a computer helps you do. It’s what YOU help YOU do.

Autosuggestion is what we tell ourselves. And it’s not all good, recently in my life autosuggestion has been pretty debilitating.

### The Dark Side of Autosuggestion

Recently I made a mistake at work; not one that I believe will cost me my job, but probably put a few people on guard. But between the lack of sunlight and my own brain, it really threw me for a loop. I kept telling myself I wasn’t good enough and found it hard to get up for a few days.

What we tell ourselves is unfortunately more important than the real world. We can tell ourselves that we are the greatest competition jump roper that ever lived (weirdly specific), or that we don’t even deserve to exist. It causes people to be the greatest… but also to fall off the face of the earth, or do horrible things.

Funnily enough, what turned me off of this funk was in part starting to take my vitamin D pills again (I’d stopped since it was the summer). Almost immediately, either via placebo or actual chemistry, the autosuggestion was still there but a lot less powerful. Medical interventions can help get you over the dark side. Just basic blood work or a therapist can really help you gain control over those voices.

### Make Autosuggestion Work for You

*Think and Grow Rich* lays out ways to get autosuggestion to work for you. The best way is to write a statement that describes what you want to accomplish, or who you want to be. Then have it in line of sight when you wake up in the morning and when you go to sleep.

Make that goal your mantra, because what we tell ourselves is more important than what the real world thinks.

If you want to make lots of money, maybe write “I will make a million dollars in 5 years.” And maybe it seems absurd at first, but you’ll start to realize that once you make something part of your identity, it’s less absurd. You push yourself in a way that maybe you wouldn’t have if you just kept the current narrative you have of yourself going.

Or you write “I will lose 30 pounds in the next 6 months.” Or whatever you think is an admirable fitness goal. Maybe even better “I will like looking at myself in the mirror in 6 months.”

Write it down, keep it where you can see it, and read it to yourself. Let it motivate you to be the person you say you will be. That you *can* be.

And do whatever you can to keep the negative thoughts at bay.
