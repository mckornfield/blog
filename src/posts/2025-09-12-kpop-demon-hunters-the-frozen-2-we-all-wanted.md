### Frozen and Frozen 2

Frozen was a solid movie. Good story, great songs for kids, amazing visuals.

Frozen 2 was a pretty meh movie. Good songs, lame story, good visuals.

What Frozen 2 failed to capitalize on — namely older kids that still love singing heroines — Netflix’s hit *KPop Demon Hunters* has capitalized on *big time.*

In my mind is the true spiritual successor to the film.

### Rumi is Elsa

Let me provide a vague description for our heroines:

*A young woman with a secret to hide has to go on a journey to discover herself. She spends the story wrestling with her friends/family/society accepting her, while singing great songs and using magic powers.*

“Let it Go” was a girl’s anthem for a while, but it’s simple.

It’s “I have all these problems and secrets, but instead of solving them, I’m going to f — off to this castle.”

Rumi’s “What It Sounds Like” is a song that evokes another step in development. Instead of stepping into her own power and running away, she steps into it to come back and finish the fight.

While Elsa is “betrayed” by a bunch of men she just met at a ball (really, they were just dicks to her), Rumi has to face the manipulation of demon men who trick her into opening up, enough to use the knowledge of her secrets to drive her apart from her friends.

Rumi’s secret isn’t that she’s an ice witch, it’s that she *is* the evil that she is trying to drive from the world.

### Jinu is a super old Hans

In some ways, these two are the same character.

Jinu accomplishes what Hans wants to (sacrificing women/children for power), but ends up spending the rest of his life regretting it. You could argue he’s a 400 year old version of Hans, whose existence is torment.

His motivation is to *forget that he did a terrible thing.*

The only character that we know lusts for power on the demon side is the amorphous *Gwi-Ma*, who we might visualize as less of a character and more of an avatar in the film; a representation of everyone’s negative emotions.

Jinu’s act of sacrifice at the end of the film brings him full circle. He did a bad thing, but that does not mean he is a *bad man*. He is capable of good, of hope.

Hans meanwhile is thrown in jail and in a Frozen short, is shown shoveling poop back home. No redemption there.

### The Battle of the Sexes

The battle of the sexes in Frozen:

- The weasel dude and his goons try to shoot Elsa
- Hans leaves Anna to die
- Elsa almost gets executed by Hans
- Anna punches Hans in the face

It’s a pretty simple back and forth, but also juvenile.

The interactions between the heroes and villains in *KPop Demon Hunters* are more complex (aside from the crying popcorn…).

There is animosity but also attraction between the Hunters and the Demons. In this sense the “battle of the sexes” is less of a deathmatch and more of a dance. The women (Huntrix) are powerful and spawn a fandom built around kicking butt, but the demon men (the Saja Boys) represent attraction and giving into your emotions.

Jinu and Rumi develop a relationship borne out of mutual circumstance (their patterns/demon heritage), and Jinu sacrifices himself, both in his affection for Rumi and I’m assuming to help right his wrongs.

The duet they sing, “Free”, is also a hint at the resolution of their story, and a resolution to the battle of the sexes in some sense. Together they can achieve much more than they can fighting one another.

The villain isn’t a man or a woman, but those negative emotions that cause men and women to fight (or literally in the film, Gwi-Ma).

### The Musical Arc is More Complex

Frozen and Frozen 2’s musical narratives are relatively flat. There isn’t tension in the songs for the most part, they are just about women wanting to be themselves (“Love is an Open Door”, “Let It Go”, “Show Yourself”) and explore the world (“For the First Time in Forever”, “Into the Unknown”).

Good stuff, but “Part of Your World,” “Just Can’t Wait to Be King,” “I’m Wishing”… You get the idea. Disney characters want their circumstances to change, so they sing about it.

KPop Demon Hunters is a film *about* musicians (who are also heroes/demons), so there’s a bit of a meta narrative about music.

“Golden” and “Your Idol” are the best pairings when trying to compare the complexity of the music in Frozen/Frozen 2 to KPop Demon Hunters.

The first is “we’re seizing *our* moment”, the second is “we’re seizing *your* moment.”

Underneath “demons take over the world”, I think the film is showing the two sides of the music industry.

There are artists that produce music and content that serves to help their listeners/fans through hard times or just to improve their lives.

And there are artists that produce music because they want to be filthy rich. And they could care less about their fans.

We can summarize these as the Givers and Takers. In Frozen, the men are very obviously the Takers, but are Elsa and Anna Givers? I think Anna certainly is, but Elsa… she just wants to be a hermit.

The fact that Rumi doesn’t run away to a castle and has to “face her demons” at the end gives the final ballad that much more of a punch.

“What It Sounds Like” is the answer to this tension between giving and taking, telling the truth and lying, consuming your fans or producing something for them.

The song is an acceptance of the fact that her secret, her past, and her patterns, are what she is, but none of those things make her a taker, a *user* of others. That is a choice she refuses to make, and that makes all the difference.
