### What You Say No to Matters More Than Anything

When I was in math class in high school, we had one of the other math teachers substitute in. He was from Haiti, which if you don’t know anything about it, it’s a poor half-of-an-island nation in the carribbean.

The teacher was telling us how he was an official in the government, and one day these men showed up and came with a bag of $10,000 or some such amount of money, intending to bribe him. He refused, which they weren’t to happy about, but they left with the money and he left with his soul.

He said this after the story:

> When you let the corruption in, it’s not that you did a bad thing in that moment. It’s that you can never get the corruption out. You’ll be someone who takes bribes, and more.

### Why DARE is Stupid

In elementary school, there was a [class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_Abuse_Resistance_Education) that exposed us to “all the dangers of drugs” and told us how we’d be hardened criminals etc. etc.

[It doesn’t work.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1448384/)

I think the people who started the organization *might* have had their hearts in the right place (emphasis on might), but if anything, it just exposes kids to the fact that these drugs exist.

When ADULTS tell you that they’re not cool, you think “well… these are probably cool then.” And after a few months you probably forget most of what they said anyway.

“Saying no” is not something you do when you’re in elementary school, or middle school, or high school, and then you’re set for life. This idea that education or morality is a single moment in time, and then that it’s “over” is… dumb.

You have to say **no** every day.

Every day there will be some form of corruption, someone trying to bend you to their plan or their will. And every day you have to say: “[Not today](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/471315-what-do-we-say-to-the-lord-of-death-not).”

### The Power of No

Being a teenager, I said yes to a lot of things that I thought were “fine,” like pornography or hours upon hours of video games, all while holing myself up in my room.

After college, especially once I was in a real relationship, I realized I needed to say “No” to these things, because they made me into something I didn’t want to be, something I *hated*.

By saying no I moved away from “cretin who lives in an office/gaming chair” to “guy with a wife and kid who does races.” The saying no gave me time back to invest in things that made me like myself a bit more.

### Retardmaxxing

(Apologies if the term retard offends you, but that’s what it’s called).

I got into this [series of videos from Elisha Long](https://www.youtube.com/@ElishaLong) where he smokes a cigar and shoots the shit. In one of the videos I listened to he talked about “blowing up your house” and “mixing orange juice with whey.”

It had me laughing but also, had me thinking. He also went on and on about golf, which I had no interest in, but made his point pretty clear.

The idea of “retardmaxxing” is what he’s popularized, i.e., not buying into all this crap being thrown at you every day.

An overcommitment to planning and a lack of being in the moment is what he points out as a real harm to men. Just setting up a time and enjoying it is much more important than being future focused.

He talked about this golf tournament he did. Elisha had a friend send him a photo of the course, but instead of studying it and changing his golf bag out, he just said “f — k it” and showed up at the tournament and played with what he knew. And he said he won, so shows that he was prepared anyhow.

The true danger of not saying no, of trying to ingest or promote or consume all the garbage in the world, while being super anxious and over planning, is that we lose the time to become a decent version of ourselves. My childhood was, for the most part, a waste.

Locked into a screen and academically jumping however high I was asked, instead of just smoking a cigar and doing dumb s — t… I would have at least had a more fun childhood to look back on.

All these things we say yes to (including being anxious) steal our time away, and they also steal our future: we don’t have great memories to look back on, and we let our brains and bodies turn to mush.

### Nomaxxing

I propose that you as an individual are your best self when you “nomax.” This doesn’t mean tell your boss to f- off and sit home and smoke pot.

It’s the opposite:

- Say no to people who are telling you to screw around and consume their products. Buckle down and do something hard, for YOURSELF, not them.
- Say no to an alleviation from friction and pain. Lean into it.
- Say no to extra credit/ extra work that doesn’t help you move forward, say no to dumb busywork. Stay late if it matters, leave early if it doesn’t
- Say no to someone on a podcast telling you how to eat, what to wear, how to be, if you don’t want to do that. Being something you hate will never beat being something you can stand to be.
- Say no to spending your time on stuff that makes you a worse person. You know what I’m talking about
- Say no to [being a kid forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puer_aeternus). Earn some money and provide for others.

When I was applying to college, I had all these interests on my resume, but they were all milquetoast. The guy interviewing me said I had to “learn to package myself.”

It’s been a while but I’ve learned what that means. It means saying no. Picking a few things and being better at them. Sure, do some things that are fun, but turn that s — t down, and turn being a better you up.
