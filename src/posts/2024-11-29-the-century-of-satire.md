## The Phases of Movies

I was listening to a podcast recently that referenced the [Critical Drinker](https://www.youtube.com/@TheCriticalDrinker) and the phases of movies. They were simply:

1. Introduction
2. Growth
3. Maturation
4. Parody

The example covered in the podcast was the Thor movies. While the movies themselves vary in quality, they stick pretty well to these phases.

Consider the latest movies that feature Thor, where he is a bumbling fat version of himself (*Endgame*) or stripped naked and made fun of (*Love and Thunder).* The other phases are a bit more obvious, as we can see Thor go from an arrogant god to a slightly less arrogant superhero.

This is true outside the Avengers and Marvel, it really just depends on how long the series goes on; it inevitably will reach parody. Parody also doesn’t need to be funny (see the *Stars Wars Prequels*), it just has to be an exaggerated imitation of the original, with the *intent* to be comedic (though the reality might be largely unfunny).

It made think of these phases and how they might apply to things besides films. Like the USA.

### Democracies Don’t Last Long

A few searches online will yield the fact that democracies last [a few hundred years](https://www.al.com/opinion/2016/08/eight_stages_of_democracy.html). I think to be fair to democracies, most governments don’t last very long.

Between wars, usurpations and revolutions, most governments are doomed to be wiped from the face of the Earth. Even a few hundred years is a good run, and arguably governments that have longer continuities (China) have eras that are so distinct from one another (e.g., [a Mongolian led dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty)?), it’s hard to think of them as the same government.

But let’s take our baby United States of America through its phases, which I think map pretty well century by century, maybe with a bit of bleeding between the two.

## The Phases of the USA

### Phase 1: Intro (1776–1800)

How to summarize the very pivotal ~25 years of formative US history?

(Maybe just watch [Hamilton](https://hamiltonmusical.com/new-york/)?)

A bunch of elites didn’t want to pay taxes to their mother country.

So they fought a war, set up a non-functional confederation that they rapidly replaced with a more centralized, federated constitutional government. They also carved out a non-hereditary executive and a two party system, at that time a rarity in democracies. And enshrined slavery as a viable institution that they’d have to fight over, ~4 score and 7 years later.

And so the great American experiment began.

### Phase 2: Growth (1800–1900)

The USA pushed West. It took lands from natives by forcing them off the land, fighting them, or giving them diseased blankets. It also annexed some territories detached from the main continent, via Hawaii and Alaska.

The USA also took on the slavery issue and took it to a bloody civil war that cost [over half a million lives](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/civil-war-casualties), and [60,000 limbs](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/amputations-and-civil-war). By the end of that war, the federal government had grown even stronger, exercising more power over the states within its ever expanding control. One could argue the US was reborn, as it became “the United States *is*” instead of “the United States *are.”*

The end of the century saw the USA also pushing out internationally, taking [territory in the Caribbean and Pacific regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War#Making_peace).

This swelled the USA to its maximum land size. Its growth had ended. Immigrants still continued to enter the country in the next century, but the immigrants and descendants of slavery would form the population base for the US in the coming century.

### Phase 3: Maturity (1900–2000)

This was the century of two world wars, with the USA ensuring victory for Britain and friends both times. It also is the century of the supremacy of the US world economy and US military might, with many interventions around the globe, mostly to further US interests, but others in the defense of democracy and freedom.

The USA relinquished a few of its island territories, but its grip on the world economy and its prominence in world affairs reached a higher point than ever before. The dollar became the world reserve, and the US military the world’s police.

### Phase 4: Satire (2000 — Present)

This is where I’ll leave my focus. I can probably expand further on how we’re living in the “century of satire” in its own topic, but it suffices to say it’s not just because Donald Trump won re-election, or that we’ll have a US department named after [a meme cryptocurrency](https://dogecoin.com/). The satire began with the turn of the century.

The first really satirical act of the United States in the 21st century was the War on Terror. In it, we have some major satirical movements, such as a war that was [launched on false intelligence](https://warontherocks.com/2023/03/the-iraq-wars-intelligence-failures-are-still-misunderstood/), [a 2 trillion dollar war whose conclusion left the region in the exact same place](https://watson.brown.edu/costsofwar/figures/2021/human-and-budgetary-costs-date-us-war-afghanistan-2001-2022) (and remember, who we were looking for *wasn’t even there*), and [a set of shadow wars fought in the Middle East and North Africa](https://democrats-foreignaffairs.house.gov/2023/4/meeks-introduces-landmark-2001-aumf-repeal-and-replace-bill#:~:text=Global%20in%20scope%20and%20with,against%20Al%2DQaeda%20in%20Afghanistan.).

I believe strongly in the US military as a (maybe *the*) source of US might, but what a strange way to use it. I think the lens of satire might be the only real answer as to why it was so oddly used.

The USA’s main economic achievements at the beginning of the century were to cause a housing calamity, build a tech empire on the backs of addicting children and adults to [social media](https://www.addictionhelp.com/social-media-addiction/statistics/) and [games](https://gamequitters.com/video-game-addiction-statistics/), while [tracking the hell out of them](https://apnews.com/article/828aefab64d4411bac257a07c1af0ecb), and having [suicidal foreign factory workers build them their phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxconn_suicides).

There’s also our friends the [drug companies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1122833/) and [food companies](https://www.statnews.com/2023/12/14/sanders-blames-food-companies-for-diabetes-epidemic/) who help make us the most well fed and yet most unhealthy people on Earth.

Even more noble pursuits like [trying to warn the world about climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Inconvenient_Truth) while using more [gas per capita than any other nation](https://www.worldometers.info/gas/gas-consumption-by-country/) leave the USA as the premiere hypocrite on CO2 emissions.

The list goes on and on. Politics in many ways has been a proxy war for more powerful interests in this country, but these days it’s literal billionaires running against a non-functional Democratic party apparatus (funded by other billionaires).

It’s hard to find a single thing that the US has done in the 21st century that is not the satirical version of what it did in centuries past.

As this is the last era in a movie series’ existence, I can’t help but wonder if it’s the final era for the USA.

I guess we’ll see if we make it to our tricentennial. 🇺🇸 Good luck USA!
