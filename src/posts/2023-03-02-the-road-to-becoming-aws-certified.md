Not too long ago I decided it would be worth it to me to try and study up and get the [AWS Developer Associate certificate](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/certified-developer-associate/?ch=sec&sec=rmg&d=1). I passed the test last week and have [the badge to prove it](https://www.credly.com/badges/47b3c2cd-85e5-4282-8616-fe1c04b327ef/public_url).

Certifications are great if you want a way to prove that you know how to work with a system, without blah blah blah-ing your vast years of experience. A little badge can go a long way.

So if you’re looking to get certified at the different levels (foundational all the way to specialty), I can lay out how you sign up to get certified, what taking the test is like, and how you’ll want to prepare.

### Signing Up

It’s pretty straight forward to sign up for tests. AWS outsources their test administration to a few companies that offer both in person and remote test taking opportunities.

If you want to sign up to take a certification exam, you can go to [Amazon’s training website](https://www.aws.training/certification?src=devops-pro), log in with an Amazon.com (not AWS) account if you have one, then follow the links to the certification your interested in.

If you’re new to AWS, you’ll want to go with a foundational one. I went with the associate level one since I had some experience but wasn’t sure how difficult it was going to be.

### Preparing for the Test

For prepping, I’d recommend probably three things:

1. Find an expert to learn from
2. Use the AWS provided learning materials
3. Get your hands dirty

**Find an expert to learn from**

For me that was Stephane Maarek. He is a super knowledgable and highly credentialed AWS expert that has a course for basically every AWS certification type. [With almost 2 million people taking his Udemy courses](https://www.udemy.com/user/stephane-maarek/), he’s an obvious choice for an expert to learn from.

His videos have good theoretical and instructional segments, quizzes and he has lots of videos demonstrating how to use parts of AWS in a “hello world” sort of walkthrough. I can’t recommend his lectures enough. He also has one practice exam included with the course, though you can look elsewhere for other practice materials.

I would also recommend committing to a number of minutes/ hours each day. The courses are long (20+ hours) so if you take your time it could be months before you’re ready. Pick an exam date that forces you to get through the material in a few months, so you don’t drag your feet.

**Use the AWS Provided Learning Materials**

The [AWS training site](https://explore.skillbuilder.aws/pages/16/learner-dashboard) is not necessarily any better than Udemy or other sites, but it does get you inside the head of how the AWS test questions will work and what sort of things they’re looking for.

Remember this certification is also a way to try and promote using AWS native services, so the test questions will have a certain preference that follows this mantra, i.e.

- Prefer serverless over servers
- Prefer managed services over self run services
- Use AWS native services instead of 3rd party/ on premise tools
- Use global/ scalable designs over regional/ not scalable ones

The AWS provided materials help drive these bullets home, and also go over how to eliminate wrong answers when taking the test.

**Get Your Hands Dirty**

For every certificate about the foundational level, AWS recommends a year or more of experience with the platform.

It makes sense, because while the test format (which I’ll get to) is just multiple choice, using the platform gives you a better sense on how things are connected.

If you aren’t working at a company that uses AWS or has a reason to use AWS, spend the few dollars a month to try out and use it yourself, maybe for a personal project or [follow an AWS tutorial](https://aws.amazon.com/getting-started/hands-on/host-static-website/). I learn a lot by doing, and so might you, vs. the audiovisual materials I mentioned above.

### Taking the Test

I don’t know how it works to take the test in person (the testing center was far, wah wah), so I’ll just explain how it works for taking it at home.

**Taking the Test at Home**

- You’ll need government issued photo ID and a pretty immaculate desk and room.
- No one can go in or out of the room, and you can’t wear headphones or talk.
- You can’t hand write but the testing tool they make you use lets you use a whiteboard on the computer
- You’ll have to photograph the room and run a piece of software that records you (via webcam), your screen, and closes all other apps while you take the test. Make sure your computer can support that. (They send you emails, just read them and follow them!)

The test formats probably vary but mine was less than 70 questions, and features 4 answer multiple choice or questions where you choose 2 out of the 5 options. Answers will be worded very similarly a lot of the time, so read carefully and use the scratch tool they provide to help narrow choices down.

Remember the AWS mantra (*AWS good, self-managed bad*) and make sure to take a few practice exams before the test.

They give you a good amount of time to take the test, so be sure to re-read your answers or skip questions and come back if you get stuck.

**tl;dr**

- Sign up for a test from the [certifications page](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/) by clicking on the badges
- Find an expert to learn from and timebox your learning from them
- Use the AWS training materials to learn their mantra/ how to reason about the test questions
- Practice using AWS yourself so you’re familiar with the platform
- Make sure your room and computer are all set up if you’re taking the test remotely

Good luck!
