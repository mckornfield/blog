### An abbreviated history of 20–21st century computing competition

- A couple companies design at home computer systems (Apple/Microsoft) and become the tech giants of the future
- The Web reinvigorates the Internet, making it a necessity for the public and businesses, but also launching the “[browser wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browser_wars)”
- An IBM computer can beat a chess grandmaster (**AI steps up to bat**)
- Companies that track and control people’s attention become the next big technology companies (Google, Meta)
- **AI re-enters the fray** as the mechanism by which content/ads are recommended to people across technology platforms. It also can [translate and navigate](https://www.theverge.com/2023/2/8/23589886/google-search-maps-translate-features-updates-live-from-paris-event).
- OpenAI launches ChatGPT, a **Large Language Model** (LLM) that shocks the public conscience
- Microsoft announces a partnership with OpenAI to enhance Bing

### OK so why the history lesson?

Computing has always been an arms race. It was used to fight wars/ win battles in the political sphere as well as the economic sphere.

Today is no different. We’re living at a time where so much of our content is recommended to us by AI that are very good at solving the nearest neighbor problem. They use data that you don’t even know you’re handing out [to predict what content (and of course ads) you’d like to see](https://www.wired.com/story/tiktok-algorithm-mental-health-psychology/).

All of the innovations over the past century (the Internet, semiconductors + GPUs, advanced AI) have led to something more remarkable than a robot that can beat you at chess.

It’s lead to an AI model that people want to *communicate* with.

### The Better Product?

These arms races have been about who has the better *product*.

- A better computer in WWII could break the enigma code, or calculate firing trajectories
- A better browser can give you an edge on tracking consumers
- A better AI can let you [capture more attention](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/chatgpt-chatbot-tiktok-ai-artificial-intelligence/)

But “better” is relative. Most would argue Netscape was the better browser, and that Internet Explorer was not the better product (as we saw in the long term).

Microsoft won the first browser war with [monopolistic practices](https://www.investopedia.com/ask/answers/09/browser-wars-netscape-internet-explorer.asp#:~:text=The%20End%20of%20the%20Battle,of%20the%20personal%20computers%20sold.). “Better positioned” might be more accurate. For the current arms race, Microsoft might once again be “better positioned” by partnering with OpenAI.

### The LLM Wars

Large Language Models are at the forefront of modern technology.

They use the most [sophisticated software libraries](https://blog.tensorflow.org/2022/09/optimizing-tf-xla-and-jax-for-llm-training-on-nvidia-gpus.html), as well as the most [sophisticated hardware](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/2023/01/26/what-are-large-language-models-used-for/), to produce pretty human-like output.

Systems that were originally used to power the displays of computers (GPUs) now provide the constant shelling of the current conflict.

These models are trained on massive amounts of text data (hence “Large” in the name) to predict and produce content that reads like a person wrote it.

We can scoff and say “that’s not human,” but maybe it’s an *archetypal* human. It writes like WE write, not as any one of us writes.

The power of this technology is the same power that all technologies that proceeded it have: its ability to capture human attention/ imagination.

Being able to [run your search engine](https://www.semianalysis.com/p/the-inference-cost-of-search-disruption) with the help of an AI that people are so interested in interacting with is a cause for alarm.

Google has already fired back with their version, Bard, but the battle is just beginning.

The LLMs will start a war on many fronts, not just against Search Engines (or *the* Search Engine), but also will be part of:

- The Rise of Plagiarism.
- Fake(r) news.
- The Death of Copywriting.

These models will fill a very full space with even more content. Writers be warned.

The best thing you can do to survive this technological battle is to get yourself educated or you’ll be left behind.
