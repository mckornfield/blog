### Entropy, as defined in statistical physics

Entropy, as you may have heard it, is part of the second law of thermodynamics. It can be stated many ways, but “entropy stays the same or increases” is the lamest way to describe it.

But what is entropy? How messy your room is?

It’s a little more than that.

When I studied physics, I stumbled my way through statistical mechanics (along with the other folks in the class).

Not only is the math difficult, but the concepts are deceivingly complex. Entropy is one that often is analogized to particles moving in boxes, so balls in a ball pit are what we’ll use.

Imagine you have a ball pit perfectly set up where half the pit is full to the brim with balls, and the other half is empty.

Now imagine I throw 30 kids into the ball pit. What do you imagine things will look like after the kids move around, play, or even just try to get out of the ball pit (“*hey, why did you throw me in here*?”)

The fact that you had a perfectly empty side, and perfectly full side, is what we’d call “low entropy”, and the post-kid apocalypse variant of the ball pit would be “high entropy.”

The state space explanation of this is: how many ways could you rearrange the pit when the balls were only in half of the pit?

If you use the dividing line of the middle of the pit, you can’t swap out any balls on one side for another, because they’re all stuck on one side.

But once a single ball moves over, you could imagine that there’s many different ways you could rearrange that one ball, where you swap one of the balls on the full side for it. All of these different ways represent a “higher entropy” state than the initial.

As the pit fills out, the number of swaps you can make across this middle line increases rapidly (state spaces are also written as W), so much so that in physics we represent the set of states with a function that helps squish things down so they can show up on the same graph (logarithmically).

The guy who set up entropy has it on its gravestone, so it must be true?

So to summarize (maybe I need to retake that statistical mechanics class haha)

- Fewer ways things can be rearranged -> low entropy
- More ways things can be rearranged -> higher entropy

### Why are artists not paid well?

Artists of any sort (music, drawing, digital art) operate in an attention based economy.

To get anyone to listen to your music or view/buy your art, you have to get them to pay attention to it first, and then pay for it!

In an era of not only an enormous amount of human content but an possibly even larger volume of AI generated content, attention is a scarce resource, whereas art itself is plentiful.

The entropic view of this is that attention is mostly a “low entropy” construct, i.e., there are not many states one person’s attention can be in, whereas the things they pay attention to/the media they consume, can be very “high entropy,” e.g., watching a TikTok video or YouTube video with some good/interesting looking person in their 20s-30s talking about something.

There are many, many people that can fill those roles, especially as production quality is not as important as being able to hold attention, but only the most supremely skilled rise up via the algorithm and are rewarded, while many creators fall into the “generic” category.

That’s not to say those people aren’t talented, it’s just that in a “winner take most” system, you (and more importantly, your art) end up being one of the many balls in the ball pit that could easily be swapped out for someone who’s a modicum more or less talented than you.

Only when you break free of this generic, dare I say, “sloppy” high entropy state and move to a less entropic level, can you get enough followers/viewers to produce art that makes you some money.

But I don’t believe getting there is easy, nor does it pay well leading up to that success. And yeah, I have no good advice on how to get out of that high entropy state other than honing your voice and talent. Generic advice for a generic problem (*boo… get off the stage!).*

OK, bear with me here.

Women end up in professions where they are acting as “mothers.”

(Don’t cancel me! Wait!)

Childcare, healthcare (nursing), teaching. These are various forms of “motherhood” as far as I can tell. And professions dominated by women, per the above numbers/graphs.

Is this to say “wow being a mother is easy, anyone can do that!”

No… but back to the ball pit, if you picked a profession out of a hat, let’s say… auto mechanic, what’s the chance you could just swap any man for that? Pretty low right? I’d argue most men *cannot* work as an auto-mechanic, maybe as someone who sucks with anything hands on. There’s over half a million auto-mechanics in the USA, and they’re mostly men (87%).

But I couldn’t swap out all the men for an “auto-mechanic”, and I could barely swap out any of the women, based on that 87% number.

I bet a decent number of men not in the profession, and probably some women, could also work as auto-mechanics, but I think that number of “reserve mechanics” is probably pretty low. If it’s proportionally low for both, we have a proxy for the idea of “lower entropy jobs.”

Back to the motherhood style professions, what if I wanted to hire a school teacher? I’d have at least a few million women to choose from, and in the reserve pool, I bet if you pushed some women through academia, they’d probably do alright in a classroom, since in a lot of ways, we’re just asking them to be moms.

Considering that [~86% of them](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2018/01/18/theyre-waiting-longer-but-u-s-women-today-more-likely-to-have-children-than-a-decade-ago/) are/will be mothers by the time they’re in their 40s, they’d develop the skills to deal with children.

That sort of numbers game, where not only is there a giant talent pool of women, but that arguably many more women (and yes, some men) could fill in, I think accounts for *some* of the financial discrepancy. This is outside of sexism and market demands, how economies work, etc. etc.

Another data point, the elementary school that my daughter will be attending has a lot of labor that is offloaded to PTA members (e.g., volunteer parents). They are providing the functions of teachers for no cost, because… they are just using their parenting/teaching skills that (hopefully) they have as parents to help the school out.

While being a mother is an essential skill, it’s a highly swappable skill, in our entropic view of the world.

### Why are highly educated professionals (lawyers) paid so well?

Sexism

…

Okay that one might be true.

Back on the ball pit metaphor, getting hired as a skilled working usually involves lots of *filters.*

Getting into undergrad and graduate schools are themselves filters, but also being able to maintain a career without dropping out of the workforce to care for your child, punishes a lot of mothers, but also acts as another kind of filter, for those who can’t “handle it.” (*What, they can’t handle two full time jobs? Losers…)*

The flipside of the coin that women work in jobs where they can be easily replaced with women, is that men tend to work jobs where (supposedly) they are *not easily replaced with other men*. Up to the earlier example, I’d be hopeless as an auto-mechanic, and even if you chucked Claude Code at an auto-mechanics forehead, I don’t know if you’d want him writing code full time (maybe soon?).

From an entropic perspective, male dominated fields are relatively thing focused and diversified, whereas women fields are people focused and channeling their inner mother.

If you look at the [comparison of jobs by gender](https://aibm.org/research/jobs-by-gender/), you’ll probably be aware of the gaps between these, let’s list the male dominated ones:

- Construction
- Installation, maintenance, repair
- Extraction
- Architecture + Engineering
- Protective Service
- Technicians
- Transportation and Material Moving

The reality of a *lot* of these jobs is that, even with automation and technology, they are physical, and being a smaller female disadvantages you. Some of these professions don’t necessarily require more brain power, but they require more undistracted time, which I think mothers will find difficult to come upon.

Being able to swap a smaller woman or a mother into these professions where you are driving a truck for days at a time, or working on an oil rig for 6 months, seems like a joke. Even among men, though truck driver is one of the most common jobs, I know very few men who can drive one of these vehicles.

Women can be (and increasingly are) beneficiaries of highly educated professionals who earn more than the average worker because (*gasp)* women are probably *at least as smart as men, if not smarter.* Their burden is the truest burden of all: making and raising children. And it accounts for a [big financial gap](https://www.bankrate.com/banking/savings/motherhood-penalty-study/).

The hope for women is to try and offload more of the childcare to men and to break into high earning professions where they can (lawyers and doctors). However, men are not as swappable into the care giver role just based on the “father gap” (i.e., there are 20% more women who are mothers than those who are fathers, meaning fathers are going among multiple women).

In my anecdotal experience, men tend to perform the childcare work worse than women, to the point where they are not trusted to be with the kids. This leads to a real supply + demand problem (women want more childcare so they can work, men cannot fill it, so childcare industries receive more demand from women who normally would have taken care of their own kids).

### Summary

Entropy is a system for understanding how different state spaces evolve. The US economy is one that, for the longest time, has valued women’s labor as free labor (thanks for doing all the childcare!), but that value has always been one that has been considered “high entropy” (any woman can be a mom!).

Women’s professions historically have lined up with something adjacent to childcare (governesses, teachers, nurses), which considering it had historically been free, those professions were largely underpaid compared with their male equivalents.

Meanwhile, men’s jobs slot into fields that are not nearly as swappable. I cannot go out and fix a car, drive a truck, work on an oil rig, or build a house. And I don’t expect any of those people to easily be able to swap to another profession either, though they might have an easier time than me. We’d consider those professions “lower entropy” in the analogy.

The hope for women is to be able to break in where they can (like most of the scientific and intellectual fields), and maybe replace the men with robots where they can’t? Hahaha
