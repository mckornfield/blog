In a serious discussion about god/gods or religion, it’s good to start with an ice breaker or a joke.

Another one I like: *A priest, rabbit and imam walk into a bar. The rabbit says “I think I’m a typo.”*

So joking aside, my life has been full of agnosticism and atheism when it comes to “god”, but I thought it worth sharing where I believe I come from, and where I’ve landed, and maybe some of it resonates with you (or not, you can let me know!)

I think I’ve gone from no god -> maybe a god -> definitely no god -> two gods. Let’s begin

### No God

As a kid, I grew up not attending church, occasionally attending synagogue, but mostly observing the world as a chaotic place with nothing that really compelled me to believe one way or another.

I think this is largely motivated by not having a *need* to believe (my life was pretty low on the survival based stressors), and the lack of a cultural connection(my parents weren’t very religious), but I’ve decided in my very wise adulthood that I didn’t believe in God because I had a version of God to believe in that didn’t jive with my reality.

The God I was told about was a spiritual being with great powers that existed in ancient times, and still exists in some way today, but his powers of explainability have been overtaken by science etc.

There wasn’t much of a point to God in my view, but I think as a kid with most of his needs met, who spent much of his time on a computer, there wasn’t much time to consider/care about something like God.

And then… puberty.

### Maybe a God?

Multiple times in my life, (going to church briefly in high school, while taking classes in college) I felt a much stronger pull towards religion.

I never felt great about praying or trying to speak to God, but I think a lot more uncertainty and fear crept into my life as I became a teenager and then adult. There seemed to be a comfort in believing in something greater than myself and the world I lived in.

So I had a few moments of agnosticism/almost belief, but then I of course swung to —

### Definitely No God

For most of college and soon after, I was pretty sure God wasn’t a thing. Just… look at the state of the world &lt;insert bad news headline here>, how could there be a God presiding over this? Or Gods?

Or really anything other than the world we see.

I think this came from my “I understand everything about everything” hubris that follows getting internships, a decent job and making decent money.

I was pretty sure I had it all figured out. Then I met my future wife, and she is a believer, and I think our views on things were pretty well aligned. We’ve started to go back to church after moving, and I want to unpack my current view, which is —

### Two Gods

We’ve made it… to my current belief system… but probably not the final one. But it does seem to bring together a few concepts that occurred to me over the course of my life.

**Mother Earth**

I read something recently that crystallized my thinking on all of this [*Dudeville* by Jonah Das](https://backcountrymagazine.com/stories/dudeville-by-j-d-kleinke-evokes-the-glory-days-of-ski-bumming/), good but depressing read. It catalogues a lot of hiking, snowboarding and other outdoor activities done by the main character “Out West” after fleeing a corporate job “Back East.”

When I was ~12 years old I saw the Grand Canyon for the first time. It was cloudy and ended up hailing a bit later (first impressions!), but what I remember seeing staring out at the canyon was something my brain couldn’t quite make sense of.

I’d seen pictures/photos of it before, but being there in person all I thought was “this is a painting.”

I felt an experience that in *Dudeville* is rightly deemed “The Presence”, and for me this was my first God. Mother Earth. The beauty of this planet is something that there is no substitute for, neither in words or in pictures. It must be seen with our own eyes.

A few other experiences have made me feel this God, namely

- Flying in a glider plane
- Hiking in Shenandoah and Harper’s Ferry
- Seeing Santorini for the first time, and hiking from Imerovigli to Oia
- Visiting Canyonlands/Utah national parks
- Many instances of staring out into the ocean
- Seeing my daughter’s birth

I think if you’ve ever been up somewhere very high or seen a place or thing you’ve never seen before, this awe has hopefully come to you and filled you with what can only be described as “serenity.”

What I hate most about using the term “Mother Earth” etc. is that I feel like it conflates me with people I strongly dislike (hippies) or with attitudes that I don’t think capture my beliefs very well (e.g., human beings are a pox on this planet!)

Humans are… of this planet. What we do to it is essentially the planet doing it to itself, in the sense that any biological system can do anything.

But this God to me is the “Great Spirit” god of the Americas. When you witness the natural beauty of the Americas, you can’t help but feel a presence descend upon you. It’s one that makes you feel small, but connected. It is a religious experience.

The other God is —

**The God of My People**

I accept the Hebrew God as what he is: the God of my people (I’m 3/4 Jewish). I can decide not to believe in him, praise the flying spaghetti monster… or I can stop being a dillhole and accept who I am.

My ancestors wandered the desert (and are still wandering), and it’s occurred to me that it’s worth trying to connect to them more by reading and connecting with the text. I read the New Testament during my church phase in high school, and I’ve committed myself to get through more than just Genesis of the Old Testament.

This isn’t the God I think rules the universe, or commands humanity. He’s the God of My People, His relationship with me and my ancestors is symbiotic; he got us through many dark times, and created a covenant with us that requires us to praise him, sacrifice to him, and remember him. We’ve been kicked out of our promise land(s) more than once, and we will have to fight to keep it as long as we live.

My best interpretation of this God is that, whereas the Mother Earth God provides awesome beauty and bounty, the God of My People is the one who helped us navigate human struggles. Most of the trials in the Bible are against other humans and societies.

So that’s the division for me; God #1 (“mother god”) is the god of nature, whereas God #2 is the god of peoples. Some societies have more than one god, but I think the principle still stands.

### Summary

I’m currently stuck on the thought of there being two gods for me. The mother god tells us nothing other than “here is my beauty, beware my danger”. The father god tells us many, many things that we must do both to keep our people alive and keep their story alive. We ignore both of these gods at our peril.
