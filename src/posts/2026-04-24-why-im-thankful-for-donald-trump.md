In church this passed weekend we talked about how often really bad, difficult things in your life can be things you can find meaning from. That you can be thankful for them.

The story of my life after college (2015 and beyond) has been the story, more or less, of Donald Trump’s brand of politics. Which it’s easy to hate, but what’s harder to do is to be thankful of it.

But I’m going to try.

### Donald Trump Shows Me USA Immigration is Completely Broken

A friend of mine made a good analogy on the state of Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE)

*It’s like if everyone was at the mall, parking in the handicap spots for years and no one did anything about it. Then one day, everyone got towed at the exact same time.*

The USA has immigration law. It is difficult to become a US citizen if you don’t have specific skills that would get you an H1B visa, and so millions of able bodied people who seek hire wages have either overstayed a tourist trip or illegally crossed the border.

The USA is also *very dependent* on this illegal labor to perform all sorts of jobs that its own citizens *do not do* (“they took our jerbs!”)*.* But maybe they would if given the opportunity?

I don’t think it’s good that these people are illegally entering the USA.

I also don’t think it’s good to kill or beat up people, but Donald Trump didn’t create a single new law. He’s just enforcing what’s on the books.

If we wanted a working immigration system, our Congress could create one, but they are (as far as I can see) completely incompetent. I’m thankful for Trump for showing me that the laws that we have are not the laws *we should have*, and that we need to change them. Maybe Congress can… do something? Besides insider trading?

### I’ve Always Wondered Why We Didn’t Take Iran On

I never bothered to war game what it would be like to fight Iran, but Saudi Arabia/Israel have been fighting proxy wars with Iran for years now. I know it’s a big country (~100 million people) and a theocracy, and like most of the Middle East, very dependent on oil production. But I didn’t know why we or Israel didn’t take the fight home to them.

And now I do, because of the *Straight of f — king Hormuz*, which I think has been said more in the last month+ than in all of human history proceeding it.

I’m thankful that Trump decided to go full beast mode on Iran, because now I never have to wonder “what if?” The answer is: breaking the world economy.

### The World is Still Extremely Dependent on Oil

Iran and Venezuela are both big petrostates, i.e., they rely on oil production for so much of their significance in the world economy. Trump targeting these places has made me realize things like [Taiwan’s 99% external oil dependence](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202604220019), a place so very important to the world’s ongoing semiconductor production.

But in a time of “Inconvenient Truths” and such other climate change existential threats, as these oil producing countries are targeted, I can see things like the fact that Cuba is weeks/days away from collapse because they rely on a single country’s oil (Venezuela) that has had its leader kidnapped.

I guess I can also be thankful to Putin for this as well, since his war with Ukraine and Trump/Bibi’s war in Iran has made Europe realized [how f — king stupid its energy policy of shutting down nuclear reactors was](https://www.latimes.com/business/story/2026-04-23/nuclear-energy-is-having-global-revival-40-years-after-chernobyl).

### The Democrats are Also Terrible

How does a party fumble the ball so badly that they lose to him twice?

Well, it’s because they’re terrible.

And I don’t mean terrible in the scary sense, I mean incapable of mustering up support or having an agenda that actually makes a meaningful difference in people’s lives.

California is a state that has been run by democrats for years, but it is essentially [the land of the dead](https://mckornfield.medium.com/the-mummification-of-california-4df5bf47b561?postPublishedType=repub). New York (where I was born) is no better.

I hope Mamdani can make a difference in the City, but I just don’t see it. America is capitalist, and raising taxes just makes capitalists what they do best — avoid paying taxes (probably by moving? or hiding money elsewhere?)

Policies that support taxing everyone but old people, a gross misuse of funds, and a crisis of in-affordability are all that democrats are capable of creating.

What happened to being a check against corporate overreach, or protecting American labor? Since when is Donald Trump the paragon of re-shoring jobs? Why is the Democratic answer to everything [taxes that don’t fix the problem](https://www.thecentersquare.com/california/article_07438dfc-103c-42e1-8641-7b435e708e96.html)? California can’t even [build a train for goodness sake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_High-Speed_Rail) (the Wikipedia article is more complete than the rail will ever be).

Since when Democrats decided to be the party that could give a s — t.

We can blame Trump for all of his bad economic policies (of which there are many), but we can also see why he won: he promised (and has unfortunately delivered) on those policies.

I’m thankful that Trump won because (as someone who voted for Kamala) I can say, the Democrats didn’t deserve it.

They need to show the f — k up.

Donald Trump as president has shown me a lot of things that I don’t think I wanted to see.
