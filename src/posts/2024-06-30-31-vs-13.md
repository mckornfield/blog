Last year I hit what I think of as [the last big milestone](https://mckornfield.medium.com/on-turning-30-903afffc1763) before the descent into old age. Though folks live longer than they once did, our bodies I think really do level off in their 30s, before descending.

If you look at the NBA and NFL, [the average age for both](https://www.espn.com/esports/story/_/id/20733853/the-average-age-esports-versus-nfl-nba-mlb-nhl) is ~26, for MLB it’s 29. If you look at the Olympics, that mean number lines up pretty well (27 for men and 26 for women). [99% are younger than 40](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/312632883_Age_of_peak_performance_in_Olympic_sports_A_comparative_research_among_disciplines#:~:text=The%20ages%20ranged%20from%2014.0,27.0%20and%2026.2%20years%2C%20respectively.). I think that lines up with the leveling off I’m describing, which is really about *physical* performance.

But I’m not a professional athlete; my only athletic feats are surviving Spartan races and going for runs/ working on grip and upper body strength. Though I have come a long way from the boy I was.

### A Heavier Me in the Mirror

![Me, circa 2006 (13)](/img/31-vs-13/1_YiyeKUStYCOg1iY4LKrxjQ.png.png)

*Me, circa 2006 (13)*

I was a heavy kid before puberty. I was sedentary and had turned away from all sports activities (didn’t like the sparring in Karate, shied away from soccer and baseball, terrible runner).

I don’t really have much in the way of photos back then, but I have some digitized videos I can pull screenshots from, one of which is above.

I do have to say that fortunately that time in my life was temporary. I lost the glasses, the bodyfat (and unfortunately the hair haha). But I like the way I look in the mirror much better than I once did.

I can still see myself though if I look hard enough, the boy who would become the man. I realize the only difference between he and I is knowing what it takes.

Being healthy or in shape isn’t about time or effort.

You have time and effort to put into things (I just put it into video games mostly). It’s about *direction.* You have to aim and launch yourself on the right trajectory, which is something very hard for a pre-pubescent boy to understand.

I see that beyond skill and talent, what separated me from athletes was how they aimed themselves. They set goals for what they wanted their bodies to be able to do and kept pushing themselves to *be* that.

I wrestled one season in high school and learned a lot about what it meant to be an athlete. I once again shied away (wrestling is definitely tough), but what stuck with me was that mind set of *direction*, of the first battle you win or lose is in your own head. In many ways I am the same person as a 13 year old.

- I have the same genes
- I eat basically the same food (back to meat after many years being vegetarian)
- I have basically the same interests/ cares

But 13 year old Matthew didn’t have the grit to keep himself pointed at a direction, either because he lacked the will himself, or the role model to push him, or whatever it was. There’s no excuse I can really come up with other than what divides 13 year old and 31 year old me is hormones and drive.

13 year old me could barely run a mile. Today I ran a (very sluggish) 5 miles.

![Apple fitness screenshot or it didn’t happen?](/img/31-vs-13/1_gJZvFbURVirzxcFOsnGKAQ.jpeg.jpg)

*Apple fitness screenshot or it didn’t happen?*

I’m proud of being able to get out of bed and push me to do something that I would prefer not to, not because I’m *better* than 13 year old me, but because I’ve set myself on a path where that’s who I am; I’m the person who gets up and runs, so that when I run the 13 mile Spartan race in September, I don’t get my ass handed to me.

### Time is Slower for Me, But Faster for Others

I have a beautiful almost 3 year old daughter that tests me daily. She is definitely in the “threenager” stage, where she’s fighting and crying and tantrum-ing when she doesn’t get her way.

What’s crazy though is that, a year ago, I was basically the same person. Same height, within 5–10 pounds, basically the same fitness level. But my daughter is like another being. She couldn’t walk very steadily a year ago, now she’s full on running away from me. When I used to wrestle with her, it was like a minor annoyance, now I have a 35 pounds to contend with.

My nephew is 13 and he’s changed so much; he had his hair grown long which he just recently chopped off, and he’s slimmed down quite a bit (reminds me of how I slimmed down from 13 -> 15). He’s still got a long way to go though before he becomes a man, but it’s moving much faster than I remember it.

And this is what truly makes me feel old. Not aches or pains, or gray hairs (or no hair!). But seeing others change so fast, that the only way I can get a good sense of how much they’ve changed is flipping through Google Photos.

A year ago I looked basically the same; a year ago my daughter and nephew were completely different people.

My 13 year old self was embroiled in the second most chaotic change a person undergoes, as is my nephew; a time where things change so much that from year to year you are a almost a complete stranger.

My daughter is I think embroiled in the *most* chaotic change, the passing from an ape-like baby/toddler to a walking, talking, potty trained little human.

But change can be good. I came out slimmer and more cognizant of what I needed to do to find companionship and friendship in the world, and my hope is that I can help my nephew and daughter through the chaos onto greener pastures, especially if I can help them set their direction.
