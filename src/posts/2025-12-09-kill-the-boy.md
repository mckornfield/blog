In the latest *A Song of Fire and Ice (Game of Thrones)* book that GRR Martin will probably publish (and probably the last haha), Maester Aemon says to John Snow:

> Kill the boy, Jon Snow. Winter is almost upon us. Kill the boy and let the man be born.

I want to speak a bit to what that meant for me, and I think for other boys who are in the process of becoming men.

### The Internet is Neverland

I spent my childhood and early adulthood online quite a bit. And in the way that I think a lot of boys do, which is engaging with electronic content, some of it [very predatory](/posts/the-turning-inward-of-the-modern-man/).

When I graduated college and started working, I *still* spent a lot of my free time playing games or watching YouTube. I’m not a very social person, but given that I socialized a decent amount at work, the energy I had left I largely spent online.

And if you want to consume consume consume, the internet is the place for it. Never has it been easier to order items, watch videos, play games, talk to people, etc. etc.

I have found the internet is like *Neverland* from the [Peter Pan play/book/film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Pan_%28play_and_novel%29). I remember playing Halo 2 on my X-box as a teenager and it was anything from 10 year olds to 30+ year olds yelling at each other.

As I grew older and played games online, I could see that while new people were still joining this market, many kids who grew up playing games kept playing games into their 20s and 30s as adults.

This is not to point the finger at video games, or any of the more illicit content that boys or young men consume, but rather to point out that, now more than ever, there’s an ease of access to content that keeps males as consumers instead of producers.

But being a man means —

### Getting Good at Something and Producing

If you’re good at your job, you produce good outcomes for your peers, customers, etc.

If you’re good at your craft, you produce decent products or art.

If you’re good at your sport, you beat the other team, or your own record.

If you’re a good friend, you produce lots of friends.

All of these acts take time and share a few traits:

- Sacrifice
- Dedicated time commitments
- Diligence

Early in my time working I switched from a [tech consultant to a product engineer](/posts/paramore-virginia-running-it-consulting/), which meant I put a lot of hours to learn things I knew very little about, in order to catch up to my more appropriately educated peers.

My time for gaming and any other chronically online activities went way down.

I also decided to get more in shape, which meant a lot of time sweating not in my gaming chair, but out on the pavement.

I found that being a man meant being *capable*

- Capable of solving problems
- Capable of earning an income
- Capable of making plans and executing
- Capable of keeping myself in shape
- Capable of doing boring, mundane s — t that is required in adulthood

*Capability* was the cornerstone of my move from adolescence to adulthood.

But the biggest blow to my time in *Neverland* was a relationship.

### Of Kids and Women

Being in a committed relationship with a woman is *at least* a part time job, if not another full time one.

All women are different, so I don’t consider my experience universal. But the most major differences between living on my own and living with her were what some men might call “nagging” or “annoying”, things like:

- “That’s what you’re wearing?”
- “You have to pick up after yourself.”
- “I need you to help me with something.” (At the expense of whatever I was doing)

Early relationship me: “this is some BS!”

Later in the relationship me: “wow I was a doof”

Your partner is not your mom, but they will mom you if you don’t take care of your own s — t. Even though they DO NOT want to.

The best person to help you kill the metaphorical boy is a person who doesn’t want to be your mom, i.e., your partner.

They want you to “be a man”, literally and metaphorically speaking.

That means holding up your half of the sky, both in the housework and chores as well as in the other aspects of a relationship.

The final blow to being a boy is, well… kids themselves. While you get to be part of the fun in their lives, [you set boundaries and safety](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OmhvFzqX_Ic) for them so they can both learn *and* survive.

It’s fun to be able to be a kid’s playmate, but first and foremost you’re [their protector and provider](/posts/mothers-daughters-sisters-or-meat/). Boys don’t have to worry about these sorts of things, but men do.

### Escaping Neverland

The internet is a place where a boy can be a boy forever, but in the real world, with opportunity to earn as well as to make ourselves men, a boy can slough off the dead skin of boyhood and enter manhood.

Along the way he can then become worthy of a partner and, hopefully, kids of his own, to begin the cycle once again.
