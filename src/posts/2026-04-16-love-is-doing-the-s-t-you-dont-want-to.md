### *What’s Love Got to Do With It*

There are many, many songs about love. There are many stories, many movies, many forms of media that try to explain love to people.

There’s arguably a holiday devoted to it ([though we know who](https://corporate.hallmark.com/valentinesslideshow/) really made it what it is).

But in my life, I have seen people really truly express love for each other. And I don’t think what endures is strong emotions, or extreme gestures. What endures is… endurance.

### Why Talking about S — t that Bothers You Matters

The most negative feelings I’ve felt in my relationship is opening up about things that bother me, or things that are really tough to talk about in general. These are things like

- money
- sex
- “our future”

You know, the things no one wants to talk about if they’re bad or a problem. Even on a good day these can be tough to talk about.

And I’ve been near tears or brought my partner to tears during these discussions, but I don’t think that means “the relationship is over,” rather “pay f — ing attention to me or else.”

These talks are painful and gruesome, but I think they are better than their alternatives, where you bottle things up and just leave, or explode and do your best to sever ties.

### Doing Things You Dislike for the Other Person is What Love Is

Have you ever changed a diaper for your kid or someone else’s? Or cleaned up puke? How about doing a chore you hate, like picking up your room, or spending time doing something you loathe with someone you love? Or going out of your way to buy something, run an errand, or do something you don’t like, but know the other person will get a lot out of.

Those are what I think love really is. Those difficult ([crucial?](https://www.amazon.com/Crucial-Conversations-Talking-Stakes-Second/dp/0071771328)) conversations are just one piece of it. What rounds out the whole picture is doing things that, under other circumstances, you would not choose to do.

Taking care of kids is truly a labor of love; getting up in the middle of the night, cleaning poop off of them, you name it. It’s not something you like, but you know that it benefits the other person not to be covered in poop, or to get their midnight bottle.

Relationships with fully grown humans are much tougher, but boil down to the same thing: the part that makes it *love* is that you do something you wouldn’t want to do.

Sacrifice is another word. Grace as well.

Personally I hate spending money on eating out or anything that isn’t random s — t I like or basic needs, but my wife doesn’t feel the same. I have to accept that my way of spending money is not *the only way,* and more importantly, if I spend money a way she wants, that’s love, whereas if it’s a way *I want,* well, that’s self care, but it ain’t love.

The next time you’re doing something or planning to do something that you hate, remember that if it’s for someone you love, that’s how you’re showing you really love the person.
