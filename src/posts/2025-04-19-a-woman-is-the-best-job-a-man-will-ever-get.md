### Before and After

Before I was in a serious relationship with a woman, I was not the best version of myself.

- In crummy shape
- Spent time [hiding in the virtual world](https://mckornfield.medium.com/the-turning-inward-of-the-modern-man-85839377507c)
- Depressed and self hating

After being in a serious relationship, my life is much more complicated, but I am doing much better on all the above metrics. I think the reason is pretty simple: a woman is the greatest gift to a (heterosexual) man.

And a relationship with her is not something on the side; it’s a full time job. We should treat it like so.

### You Gotta Work For It

What boys forget as they become men is that, because women are something you have to earn, we aren’t *owed* a woman. It’s the other way around, we have to become *deserving* of her.

There are two lies we tell ourselves when women don’t miraculously land in our laps:

1. You don’t deserve any woman because you are *worthless*
2. The only way to get a woman to like you is to *trick them*

These lies fester within us.

The more time we spend holding onto rejection, or living in fear of it, the more our self worth spirals downward, until we become *worthless* men who have to *manipulate* women into liking us.

But there is an alternative —

### The Jobs Analogy

My life in a committed relationship is much better than it was alone.

Getting there was a true struggle; I had to change who I was and make concessions about what a relationship took to create.

- I had to make a good impression (interview well)
- I had to put *effort* in.
- I had to concede that I could be much better

### The Interview

Men might think of the first date or first meeting as the interview. I think it’s a bit fuzzier than that. A woman is constantly sizing you up, even before you’re on a date with her.

There are some basic things that men have to improve (cleanliness) as well as the classic “getting your s\*\*t together.” If you wouldn’t show up to an interview without a shower and a haircut, why would you show yourself to a woman that way?

Interviewing with a woman boils down to a small number of things, that I think line up really well with a regular job interview

- Does he present himself well? (Appearance, cleanliness)
- Is he smart/clever? (Funny, shows interest in me)
- Can I see myself being with the guy? (Approachable, trustworthy)

**Aim for “No”**

Unfortunately, with women as with jobs, it’s really hard to get one if you don’t already have one.

Companies and women view you the same way:

Unemployed/Single: Well who could want this guy?

Employed/In a Relationship: Well at least someone wants this guy?

Know that the interview process is tough, especially for the first serious relationship. But take rejection as a sign that you’re trying, and as feedback on how you can do better, not as a sign of your self worth.

Getting into a serious relationship with a woman is like taking your first job. You are going to have to *work* to get it started, literally throw yourself at the thing.

Some useful advice on getting this first serious relationship going are:

1. Ask them out in person
2. Goal is to get a no, not a non-answer
3. Come across as, if anything, *not boring*

### The Job

Why are relationships hard? I think you give up part of yourself. The element that makes for a good relationship is *sacrifice.* I think the same is true about a job.

In a job, you exchange your time for money. But what you *sacrifice* is hours you could spend working elsewhere, or the hobbies or desires you’d rather focus on. Instead, you have to do plenty of things you don’t want to do.

That being said, your job should not be a one way street; if you’re working and not getting the pay/respect you deserve, you should make it clear that if things don’t change, you’re going to start looking elsewhere.

My experience with relationships is that they are *work*. There are easy parts when you’re with the right person, but unless you’re dating a clone of yourself, there will be a lot of times where the gears don’t mesh. In those situations you’ll have to decide if you want to give part of yourself up, or push the other person to give part of themself up.

In my experience, you’ll do both the sacrificing and the asking for sacrifice, and both things suck but are necessary.

I’ll give a couple examples from my own life:

1. My wife wanted to go back to school, which I opposed for more than one reason (making less money, have to spend money/time going to school). But I went along with her decision, albeit with a lot of grumbling, because I understand that it’s short term pain for long term gain (her doing a job she’ll be happier doing).
2. My wife moved out to live with me in Virginia early on when we were dating. It created a rough dynamic since she was in a new place and I was going through a stressful job transition, but it allowed our relationship to take place in person, instead of the long distance bit. I hadn’t lived with a roommate since college so there were all sorts of transitions/concessions I had to make, but we made it work and were able to grow together.

The difference with a relationship vs. a job is, and this might be obvious, a deep relationship leads somewhere a job usually doesn’t: a legacy.

### The Calling

[Scott Galloway](https://medium.com/u/a76508074bec) ends a lot of his podcasts/talks/articles with a reminder of what *this* is all about (i.e., existence). He calls it “The Whole Shooting Match.”

People that find companionship with the opposite sex are driven to create more people. Yes, *kids*.

We stop doing that, [there’s not going to be more people for much longer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ufmu1WD2TSk).

A woman who is in a committed relationship with a man ultimately gives him the opportunity, the privilege and the pain/pleasure of being a father. There are plenty of men who foul up or shirk this responsibility, but they do it at *their peril.*

To use Scott’s sort of terminology: The whole damn point of the whole damn thing is to keep this cycle going, with the hope of improvement for the lives of your progeny. That doesn’t happen with a man trapped in his a room, staring at a screen, OR by a man who drops his sperm off and says “see ya.”

It happens with a man who gets up and gets to work; who isn’t afraid to hear the word *no* and *put the work in* to make the relationship work.

Eventually you *will* find a woman if you keep trying to improve yourself and the presentation of yourself, and if you put the work in, you’ll be able to get to the most wonderful and terrifying thing ever, being a parent.

Also you don’t get to sleep. It sucks. But babies are cute.
