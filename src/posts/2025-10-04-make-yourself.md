### 1999

In 1999 I was 6 years old. A formative time for me, but also a formative time for the Internet, which was about to experience the largest economic bubble tech had created (so far?).

It was also the year a song/album that would become part of my college workout routine was released. [*Make Yourself*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RxPYT-qVyFg)*.* The album is one of my favorites, but it has a bit of a push and pull within it.

There’s the eponymous “Make Yourself”, which talks about mastering your destiny, and “Nowhere Fast”, which talks about being very stuck. There are other great songs on there (“Drive” is probably the one most people have heard off of it.)

Let’s discuss.

### 2012 — The Gym

I got very into this album not when I was six (I think I still liked Barney back then?), but when I was around 19, in 2012. Back then I was a Pandora guy, not Spotify shill like I am now. I had 30 Seconds to Mars, Incubus and Chevelle in a Pandora-driven loop.

But when I went to the gym, I had *Make Yourself* loaded onto an iPod Shuffle.

![Photo by Gio on Unsplash](/img/make-yourself/0_PzqX8VvvNfsF48BV.jpg)

*Photo by [Gio](https://unsplash.com/@mikavas?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)*

Yes… this little brick had *Make Yourself* on it. And I’d just move into the first song, “Privilege” (appropriate, as a kid attending Georgetown, and using the school gym).

Then, as I was either running on the tiny track, running on a treadmill or starting a weight workout, “Nowhere Fast” would kick on. And that was pretty appropriate, as I was literally not moving anywhere fast.

And while I was exercising and going to a good school, I was *lost* in many ways during my time at college, especially my sophomore year of college. I spent a lot of time [playing video games and turning inward](https://mckornfield.medium.com/the-turning-inward-of-the-modern-man-85839377507c). I felt like this was a song about *me.* The chorus:

> Will I ever get to where I’m going?  
> If I do, will I know when I’m there?  
> If the wind blew me in the right direction, yeah  
> Would I even care?

> I would.

Describes I think most young men pretty well. A veneer of indifference, ignorance of the right path, and most importantly, feelings of longing and desire underneath.

If I made it through most of the album, I’d get to “Make Yourself”, whose chorus is much more authoritative than “Nowhere Fast.”

> You should make amends with you  
> If only for better health, better health  
> But if you really want to live  
> Why not try and make yourself?  
> Make yourself

The gym is where people go to make themselves. In school, people try to make *you* (fill you with their preconceptions of the world, make you pass their tests, etc.). Ideally they’re *coaching* not *lecturing*, but I seldom found that to be the case.

The gym is a different place. Even with personal trainers pushing you or crazy cross-fit coaches, the only moves you do in the gym are the ones you make yourself, and by doing those moves, you *remake* yourself.

Make yourself stronger, fitter, more elastic, more explosive.

### 2016 — Work and a Real Relationship

I read a lot of self help books coming out of college, especially ones about relationships. Mostly because I had no clue what I was doing, but using Tinder made me feel like I was going *Nowhere Fast* once again. But the books like *Mars and Venus on a Date* did sort of help me figure out what was going wrong.

But I took a leap of faith, as did my now wife, and decided to try out a real relationship by moving in together (since we were talking to each other long distance). I also changed roles at work around the same time, so I was putting in some fun hours at work.

So much of what I did during that time was repetitive and painful (making mistakes in my relationship and at work), but damn it really did make me into a different person.

I had to [*Kill the Boy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_the_Boy)in more than one way (less illicit content/video games, better executive function and time management, more time working on my relationship and my work persona).

I’m not saying I came out a superhero, but I wasn’t the Matt of my college years anymore.

I was someone who knew what it took to work through problems in software (mostly banging your head against the keyboard and/or getting help from the right people) and in my relationship (talking things out and not doing dumb s — t). I spent that time *making myself* into a man.

### 2022 — Off to the races

In 2022 I did [my first Spartan race](https://mckornfield.medium.com/running-your-first-spartan-race-84eb7b2f283f), and I didn’t really *succeed* as much as *survive.* But as I did my burpees in place, or ran up and down some stairs carrying heavy s — t, I was going “Nowhere Fast”, and I did indeed “Make Myself”.

Since then I’ve done a Trifecta one year (5K, 10K and 21K Spartan races), and run a half marathon and full marathon. Prepping for the races has been itself a part time job (lots of pull-ups and running… marathon training is *a lot* of running).

But the races gave me a really good reason to show up in my workouts. Instead of going to the gym or the track to be fit, I went to the gym to *make myself* into a racer.

### Voting for Who You Want to Be

I view the habits I’ve built up over time as a vote for the person I want to be. Every good (or bad) workout, every thoughtful gesture for my wife or my daughter, every commitment to get something done for work, that’s a vote to make myself into a fit, supportive and successful person.

My emphasis here has been on fitness, but relationships and work/careers function much the same: time spent working on making yourself into the person you want to be is done minute by minute. Just like building a block tower or anything, it’s built up of small parts that are frustrating to assemble.

Let’s also remember that there alternatives to working on yourself. If you refuse to work on yourself, maybe you let other people form who you are:

> If you let ’em make you, they’ll make you papier-mâché  
> At a distance, you’re strong until the wind comes, then you crumble and blow away

Passively consuming media, regurgitating ideas, following without questioning. You might appear strong until you have to think or act for yourself. If you don’t make yourself, someone else will.

Or (arguably) worse, you could make yourself into something *worse*

> So when I make me, I won’t be papier-mâché  
> And if I fuck me, I’ll fuck me in my own way

This means “consuming illicit media” (watching porn) or other forms of eschewing connection, and instead doing things that make you feel good (hedonism/eudaimonia).

Every moment spent doing something is a vote for the person you will make, and the person with the most votes wins.

I’m not always a happy person, but when I look in the mirror, I see a person who has spent their adulthood trying to make themselves. And that makes me something more than happy: it makes me proud to be who I am.

I hope the same goes for you.
