Politicians don’t take hits but they practice an awkward [kayfabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayfabe)

I listened to a [handful of podcasts](https://radiolab.org/episodes/montreal-screwjob) recently describing the dynamics behind professional wrestling. I’d also watched some bouts myself when I was younger, and started to think about how the staged elements of professional wrestling, *kayfabe*, line up with the US political system.

I’d go so far as to say the US political system is like the WWE, but worse.

**tl;dr**: Our political system is a pro-wrestling good guy vs. bad guy match, the results might as well be fake, and we the people take all the hits.

## The Bad Guys

In professional wrestling, it’s always clear that one of the wrestlers is the “good guy” (or gal) and [someone is the bad guy](https://www.digitaltrends.com/movies/wwe-evil-john-cena-peacock-trailer/#:~:text=Examine%20the%20ultimate%20rogues%20in,Table%27%20himself%2C%20Roman%20Reigns!). When they come out to the ring, there’s a constant stream of BOOOOOO’s from the crowd, the music has a darker tone.

You feel like you’re supposed to not like that person, watching at home or watching rinside.

Heck, Donald Trump himself has been in WWE matches (kind of blurring the lines here aren’t we?). There’s a villain if you need one.

If you tune into Fox News or MSNBC, or just listen to any politician speak, you can find examples of them calling out the bad guy ([Biden](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3gPEIW71MNc) or [Trump](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sGZD7-El4xE)). The bad guy is constantly “destroying America.”

In any story, it’s necessary to have a bad guy. That means *someone’s* the hero (hopefully *you*). It makes for an archetypal story, which is why it’s a part of pro-wrestling, American politics and much of the rest of our culture.

American Politics benefits from this bad guy/good guy setup because it is not nuanced. The system is a duopoly, where both sides share power back and forth, taking turns painting each other as the bad guy. The good guys “win elections,” in order to prevent the dystopias that are:

- Republicans are going to maintain a rich, white, male, Christian power hierarchy extending back generations, that kicks all other groups to the curb or puts them in prison.
- Democrats are going to topple that hierarchy and burn it to the ground (*f — your Target!*), replacing it with a “kumbaya” world where everyone is paid a universal basic income to do whatever they love.

Both visions presented are hyperbolic, *bad guy* views of the world to the other sides. They get people to the polls, but more importantly, they get people to fork over cash.

The reality is, the Democrats and Republicans are the bad guys. They want to stay in power, [rake in dark money](https://www.opensecrets.org/dark-money/basics), while appearing like they have your best interests at heart. They’ll work together to maintain the corporate dystopia we’ve all come to know and *love*.

The real constituency they are fighting for is “We the Corporations.”

In the WWE, the bad guys usually lose their matches, but the bad guys are always winning in US Politics. The “bad guys” in politics are just a plot device to get people fired up and to keep them engaged.

And are these good guy vs. bad guy matches really “fair?”

## The Results Aren’t Pre-determined, But Might As Well Be

In WWE, the match results are pre-determined. Things are choreographed if need be, like difficult stunts that could hurt the wrestlers. But the wrestlers act like it’s a real match with real stakes on the line (and as far as their bodies are concerned, that’s true). That’s the *faycabe* of it all.

In US politics, you know one of two people are going to win: it’ll be a Democrat or a Republican, with a [sprinkling of independents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-party_and_independent_members_of_the_United_States_Congress) who were part of one of the two parties at some point.

If you’re running in a city, you’re probably going to win as the Democrat.

If you’re running in a rural county, chances are you’ll win as a Republican.

We live in a time of enhanced [urban-rural divide](https://citymonitor.ai/government/the-urban-rural-divide-only-deepened-in-the-2020-us-election). And usually the places that swing the other way are [obviously gerrymandered](https://thefulcrum.us/worst-gerrymandering-districts-example).

When it comes to national elections we’ve been living in the era of [pendulum politics](https://ischoolonline.berkeley.edu/political-pendulum/) in the US, where yesterday’s loser is tomorrow’s winner. The old bad guy becomes the new good guy.

So when we point to the results of wrestling as fake or predetermined, but talk about how our elections are legitimate, it’s once again more theater. We basically know who’s going to win the elections based on:

- Where the election is
- Who was last in power

We only have two options and they both stink. At least the WWE results are made up by a set of writers. There’s hopefully something more interesting there, from a storytelling perspective.

At least our politicians lose sometimes, yeah? Taking some hits?

## Taking Hits

In professional wrestling, folks get hurt all the time. They’re less professional wrestlers and more professional stuntmen/actors at this point in their career.

Taking flying hits off the turnbuckle is no joke; we’re talking 200+ pounds of muscle falling from decent heights. [And it hurts](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2610966-this-fake-stuff-hurts-like-hell-pro-wrestlings-painful-toll-on-the-body). These professionals put their bodies on the line as part of their career.

For politicians at the top, losing a race is *nothing*. Hillary is still a multi-millionaire after her 2012 defeat. Trump is still a billionaire after his defeat in 2020. There are no consequences for politicians when they lose their matches.

The real losers are Americans who get super invested in a system that just uses them for dollars and free labor (i.e., campaign finance and campaigning). Even if you supported the winning side, you still have the same system that [crushes the middle class](https://www.pewtrusts.org/en/trust/archive/fall-2022/how-the-american-middle-class-has-changed-in-the-past-five-decades#:~:text=The%20middle%20class%2C%20once%20the,Center%20analysis%20of%20government%20data.) and makes sure rich people and corporations are taken care of.

Who takes the hits in the political arena? 🛎🛎🛎 We the People.
