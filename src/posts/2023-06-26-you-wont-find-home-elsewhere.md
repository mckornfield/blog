Traveling abroad always reminds me of who I am and am not

![](/img/you-wont-find-home-elsewhere/1_gCNzXKqokDGiCKu1hLKFeA_2x.jpeg.jpg)

Tokyo is an amazing city. It’s got a rock solid public transportation system, it’s unbelievably clean, the people are super respectful, and there’s a surprising amount of greenery throughout.

I look around though and see people dressed in a fairly western way, but I do stick out like a sore thumb, for example:

- No men wear hats here
- The only men wearing shorts are boys or dads (which I am but don’t have kiddo here haha)
- I don’t understand the language and culture, and never will 100%

I clearly am not nor ever will be Japanese. But who am I?

### No Roots

I was born outside New York, lived in the DC area for a while, and now live around Portland OR.

I’ve travelled to many places at this point, mostly in Europe and East Asia.

Living in and seeing all those places I thought would give me a sense of being worldly or well travelled. Instead I can’t help but feel lost, where I have no anchor to a community or group that will last more than a few years.

My family is much the same, spread over the continental US. My wife’s family is mostly in the PNW but still separated by a 9 hour drive from her parents and a few siblings.

I have no roots. All the ties I’ve formed are tacit and temporary. But it doesn’t make me sad, it just makes me wonder what others feel when they travel.

Home is not going to be here or anywhere else than where my family is from. It’s great to be able to admire and experience another people’s culture, but the only things I can take back with me are memories and trinkets. I can’t take back a sense of belonging or transform who I am. Maybe I can appreciate those I have a bit more, seeing where the venn diagram of my life intersects with Japanese culture.

I saw a tree that was 1000 years old yesterday, and a mountain much older than that. While I may not have roots, I can admire those that literally and figuratively have deep roots, and seek to build my own.
