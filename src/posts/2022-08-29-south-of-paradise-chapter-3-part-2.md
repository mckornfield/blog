### Continuing where we left off in [part one](https://mckornfield.medium.com/south-of-paradise-chapter-3-part-1-3763ae157eec?source=user_profile---------0----------------------------)…

*We were extra careful about leaving a trail, taking careful steps to avoid twigs and thick pieces of underbrush, and doing our best to walk on bare ground. Wanda agreed that avoidance was our best plan. Distance is the key to survival, the Vulture’s mantra. We dearly hoped our overseer would deal with the Carrion, but even he/she might avoid a fight when it came to three roamers. We could still hear the leg bones cracking in between their jaws as we moved through the moonlight.*

After moving a few miles away, and attempting to cover our scent with a bit of mud and water by a small stream, we felt it was time to rest. We leaned up against a pine and I took the first watch, as Wanda drifted off to sleep for a few hours. Now that we were in such a dangerous place, we would need to keep watch every night. The watch was uneventful, but I did jump a few times at the sounds of nocturnal creatures tittering and moving throughout the forest. I shook Wanda awake since I was about to collapse, and I fell to a clump as she got to her feet. “Thanks Leon.” she mumbled as she slumped into a restive but alert pose. My sleep was driven by exhaustion but tormented by fear, so that when she shook me awake to take another watch, I felt worse than before.

I was going to shake Wanda awake to take watch when the dawn cut out from behind the trees. Enormous shadows covered the ground, as darkness was turned to day and the trees bent ever so slightly towards the morning sun. I gave Wanda a gentle shake and we broke our fast quickly before performing another survey. We swept out in a circular motion, as we had the day before, keeping one another in sight as we tried to find a water source. We had spoken very little since we had entered the forest, but I still felt a strong bond of communication with her, one that went beyond words.

We had moved out very far from one another, so much so that we were just specks in one another’s eyes. She stopped moving and threw up her arm in signal. I quickly moved to her side, and we looked down onto a moving stream she was kneeling over. The stream wound quietly through the rocks on the forest floor, bobbing and weaving as a dancer across a rocky dance floor. It extended down from a mountain covered in trees, and made its way towards the tree line back where we had entered. I gestured forward, up the mountain, and Wanda nodded silently, as we threaded through the trees and rocks on our way up.

After a few hours of walking, the gradually thickening stream reconnected with a clear, quick moving river, about thirty feet across. Wanda and I took a break to quench our thirst and wash ourselves off a bit; cleansing the grime we had covered ourselves in the previous days.

When she first spoke, I was surprised; it had been a while since she’d said a word to me: “Look for tracks,” she commanded, and pointed to the opposite side of the river. I handed her what little gear and food I had before wading across the river. The current was slow but forceful: dangerous if one was not careful, and the water teemed with small fish and other creatures that nibbled at my legs and boots. When I reached the other side, I shook myself off a bit and nodded at Wanda. She took my pack, made sure it was securely shut, and tossed it over to me. We set off on opposite sides looking for tracks, scanning the two sides of the river.

The river wound slowly back and forth, as we walked slowly for about two miles before I spotted a faint mark. I looked at Wanda and she looked back immediately, as though sensing my glance. I pointed to the depressions at my feet, which she squinted at for half a second before readying her bag to toss it over. I caught it and placed it down gently. She made her way over with haste, and shook herself off after she reemerged out. She reached down to the depressions and ran her finger across, getting a feel for how old they might have been. To me they had seemed only a couple days old and she concurred, without speaking or nodding. Our bond had grown so strong now, I could feel the thoughts zipping through her head as she thought what to do next.

*Into the trees we go*. I thought. She turned towards me and cocked her head, and when I smiled at her she knew that she wasn’t hearing things.

*Agreed*. I heard back from her. Although maybe heard isn’t the right word. I *felt* her agreement would be a better way to put it.

After a brief snack, consisting of some berries and stale bread we had left over, we followed the faded tracks as best we could. After a short time they faded into the underbrush, and we decided to walk about 2000 paces more before doing a sweep outward to try and find any more sign of the deer.

Part way through those 2000 paces, we heard a rustle of leaves and branches being snapped off in a distant direction.

Some awkward movements followed that rustling, as something bounded into a tree and made a *smack* sound that came across as a *thump* from where we stood, followed by the panicked crunch of undergrowth. Wanda and I immediately sought a tree and scampered up it, not looking down until we were about 20 feet up. Then we heard a noise that was truly unexpected: the sound of lots of claws scratching the underbrush.

*Oh no*. Wanda and I thought simultaneously. A bounding deer shot by like an arrow, making almost no sound, as a pack of seven gray wolves flew by in pursuit, the alpha male in the lead.

After they had passed by, Wanda and I held onto the tree for a short time. The wolves had passed by in an instant, but it was a beautiful and terrifying image. With purposeful grace, powerful and well-proportioned muscles, and a pack mentality, those creatures were nature’s precursor to the Vultures. They were also deadly killers, and in that sense they reflected our enemy.

My master’s words drifted into my head *“When I say two as one, I refer not just to two beings with one purpose, I refer to two purposes in one being. There is a good wolf and a bad wolf, but each serves its purpose. By feeding your two wolves, and using each when you need to, you understand the meaning of two as one.”*

Wanda was deep in thought when I turned to her, her eyes gazing blankly in the direction that the predator and prey had fled. I let out a low whistle to get her attention, which she responded to by scrambling down the tree.

She found a taller tree and climbed up it, catching sight of the animals off in the distance. I stayed at the bottom of the tree and looked up. She looked down and shouted “Are we allowed help with our task?” She gave a crooked smile and clambered back down. As soon as she reached the floor, she quickened her breath and settled into a dead run, picking a path to cut off the wolves.

I followed her as fast as I could, feeling she had some stroke of genius. I held my pack tight so it wouldn’t bounce as I took off after her.

Branches sped at me, tickling my face and arms as I shoved them out of the way. Her figure was much more graceful than mine, as she moved between trees and danced over roots, the distance increased between us. I was focused on keeping my breathing stable as I stumbled about every obstacle on the forest floor. The sound of the wolves was hard to pick out as the wind rushed by my ears, but very faintly I could feel the sound of paws digging at the earth as the powerful beasts maneuvered over the dirt and underbrush.

As the wind rushed by our ears, another sound came into focus, as well as a fetid smell. I noticed it before Wanda did, as she was very intent on following the wolves (and maybe I had the better nose?). I wanted to shout to her to be careful, but my eye caught some hulking figures speeding into view on the right. My head turned almost of its own accord, to track their movement. This was the first major mistake I’d ever made while outside the Nest, though certainly not the last. A low-hanging branch met my forehead with a –

CRACK!

I fell back, rolling to stop from landing on the back of my head, and almost got the wind knocked out of me as I landed on a bush jutting out from the earth. The pain blinded me for about a half a second, before I snapped back to focus and tried to scream after Wanda. The black shapes moved with superhuman speed after her, and one of them had broken off and was heading towards me. I wheezed for breath.

My mind scrambled for what to do next. I was light-headed and struggling to get up. My mind grabbed onto what seemed like the only possible choice. An arrow formed in my head, an arrow of *thought*. I pulled taut the cords of my mind and loosed a shot in the direction of Wanda, who was fast disappearing behind the trees.

I felt it connect with weak accuracy, just barely delivering the message; she told me later that it was only a whisper:

“*Behind you…”*

But just as she was about to disappear from my view, she started doubling back and away from the Carrion that were pursuing her, zigzagging between the trees. Relief filled my mind as air filled my lungs. I shot up faster than a well-watered sprout and sped off in her direction. The sounds and smell of death enveloped me.

Wanda and I cut through the forest to meet up once again, the bumbling creatures behind us taking two huge strides for every one of ours. As we met again she mouthed a word to me and I felt it pulse within my mind in an exasperated tone. *Knife.*

I concentrated on keeping my feet moving as quickly as possible and slung my pack around, pulling the knife out by its blade and holding it out in my bouncing arm towards her. She seized the knife and with a half second’s thought, slid the blade along her arm hand and made a shallow cut. Biting back the pain, she wiped the knife off on the back of her arm and flung the knife into a nearby tree. We lost sight of the creatures for a moment when we had circled around a line of bushes. She took that opportunity to speed away from me, wiping her bleeding arm all over the trees, the shrubs, everything within her reach. I followed after the knife and pulled it out of the bark while sliding behind the tree.

The Carrion slowed their pace a bit, seeing the smears of red and sniffing after the enticing smell. There were three of them standing around the blood smeared flora; though it was hard to tell if they were the same three we had seen the night before. I backed away slowly, realizing my smell would be easy to pick up, since I was close and covered in sweat. At the sound of Wanda’s feet crunching the underbrush a distance away, two of them sped off, but the third one lingered for a moment. Just as it was about to take off, I banged the blade of the knife against the tree bark. It stopped moving for a moment, shifting its blank gaze back towards me.

I pulled back my arm and hefted the blade with all of the force I could manage. It was balanced oddly, because it was not a throwing knife, but it buried itself in the creature’s neck all the same. (Good thing I’d practiced!)

It cried out, in annoyance, not agony, and it came towards me with a strong yet clumsy step. I scanned the ground for a large branch, and once I found it, readied myself in a low stance.

Little blood oozed from the knife wound; the carrion’s blood had pooled and dried after its heart stopped beating. I relaxed my pose a bit as he stumbled over to me; I breathed deeply and slowly to lower my heart rate. As he moved just outside of my reach, it stuck its grasping hands out and leant forward. I dodged to the side and struck with all my strength below his legs. He tumbled forward and landed straight onto the blade, as it plunged deeper into his throat, his limbs flailing about in confusion. A tear opened across his neck that I took advantage of. I wrapped the stick around his head and put my heel to his back. Pulling with all my strength, his head tore off with the sound of rotten meat torn asunder by a pair of dogs. I kicked aside his head and picked up the blade, wiping the dried flesh off on a bush. With the branch and knife in hand I sped after Wanda.

\*\*\*

I couldn’t find her. I followed the trail of blood and broken branches and trampled underbrush; eventually the forest opened up into a small clearing. The trail from there was much harder to follow, as it appeared the wolves and deer had been through there recently as well. Her wound has most likely clotted by then and the trails of the different creatures crisscrossed as I exited the clearing, with no indication of blood and no clear way to distinguish the footprints/pawprints left in the forest floor.

Then something strange happened. The trail wound in a circle. I followed it as best I could, but somehow found myself back in the same clearing again. I thought hard for a moment, leaning against a tree and staring at the plethora of marks and movements imprinted in the ground in front of me. I thought about why Wanda had wanted to go after the wolves in the first place. Did she think we could take the kill away from them? I doubted it: that brief viewing we had of them showed we were outnumbered and outmatched in strength.

Though maybe not in cunning.

I sat there puzzling for a few moments, before I realized what she had done. She had intended to use the carrion to scare away the wolves, or even to fight with them. She had smeared her blood around to create distance, but wanted them as much as she wanted the wolves. As I moved back along the trail, I tried reaching out to her with my mind, feeling the corners and shadows for her presence.

I sensed something about halfway through the loop, and moved towards a small grotto, hidden about a quarter mile off the sets of tracks by trees and shade. Inside I found Wanda with the dead deer curled up by her.

Wanda was badly injured, having lost blood from a wolf bite on her arm, from a long scratch across her chest, and the self-inflicted wound on her arm. I took my water and tore pieces of cloth off my shirt and cleaned the wounds as best I could. I then wrapped the wounds with some dry pieces of cloth. At that point I had very little shirt left. I looked the deer over, and saw that one of the legs had been torn off, and blood was still oozing from a wound in the creature’s neck, probably a bite from one of the wolves that had slain the animal.

I stroked her forehead affectionately, trying to stir her to consciousness, but to no avail. I knew that our current location was not the safest place, and thought of my best course of action. I decided to put the deer in the rear part of the cave, and covered it with branches and pine needles to dampen the smell. Then I slung Wanda over my shoulder and moved us out of the cave, but kept it within my line of sight, moving somewhere where I’d have room to defend us.

The sun had set just moments after I moved her. I leaned her against a tree and slumped down next to her, overcome by fatigue. My eyes started to close, but I forced them open and busied myself trying to rouse Wanda. I reached out to her with my mind, trying to feel anything. All I came into contact with was the chaotic miasma of dreams, flowing through her mind. I pulled myself back and kept my watch as best as I could. Not ten minutes went by before a figure came into view in front of the cave. My heart skipped a beat, and I readied my branch and knife as I came over. But in the rising moonlight, I saw the outline of a spade sticking over the figure’s shoulder, and what I realized was the overseer crept towards me.

“Show me.” He said.

We went into the cave and I pointed towards the pile of twigs and pines, staying at the entrance to the cave so I could keep an eye on Wanda. The overseer cleared away the cover, scrutinizing the creature in the darkness. Replacing the leaves, he turned towards me and said, “Go rest.”

I went over to Wanda and took up the same position next to her, and watched as the overseer moved up above the hill and buried the saber at his waist in the ground, while laying his spade gently by his side.

In the morning, the corpses of a few carrion lay strewn about the cave, all of them cleanly decapitated. The overseer met the awe in my eyes with a cold look.

“Help me.” He said, turning in towards the cave. Wanda began to stir, and I gripped her shoulder affectionately. Scared for a moment, her eyes flickered open, but then they lazily moved back shut at the realization that it was me.

“They fought over her… While they fought I snuck her away… the parts of her that were left.”

We field dressed the deer, leaving the organs outside the cave and taking the hunks of meat as best we could carry them. We took the head back as our proof.

With the kill on our shoulders, the three of us made our descent back to the Nest. As we left the forest, and made our way back to the open plain, the sun set once more. The howling of wolves echoed out: defiant against the cold moon, the prowling undead, and the thieves that had stolen their kill.
