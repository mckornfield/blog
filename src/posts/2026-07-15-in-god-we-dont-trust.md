I’ve spent the year reading ye olde Bible, and feel like I’m encountering a bit of a paradox with the way the US is (and has?) been operating with respect to the God portrayed in the book.

### The part of the 10 commandments that keeps sticking with me

If you read the ten commandments summarized somewhere, you’ll see something like “thou shalt not make false idols.” But the 10 commandments are not presented this way in the text.

Some are one line, and some are much longer.

This specific commandment (false idols) shows up like so in the New International Version:

> *4. You shall not make for yourself an image in the form of anything in heaven above or on the earth beneath or in the waters below. 5 You shall not bow down to them or worship them; for I, the LORD your God, am a jealous God, punishing the children for the sin of the parents to the third and fourth generation of those who hate me, 6 but showing love to a thousand generations of those who love me and keep my commandments*

This keeps sticking with me because in the modern world, one full of people of the book (Christians, Muslims and Jews), this commandment seems to have extra weight, and yet might be the most ignored of our time.

The punishing the children part I take metaphorically to mean you will ruin your children and bring ruin on them by performing idol worship. But let’s draw some lines here so we understand what my view is of idol worship and why it is so dangerous.

### What God isn’t

God isn’t a picture. He isn’t even the word God. The Jews go to good lengths to distance the word from the entity, using YHWH as a way to write a pointer/indicator to God, but not the text itself. In the NIV edition of the Bible, this YHWH is shown as LORD.

(While reading this I kept thinking “Why is Moses yelling at me, come on dude, chill out.”).

Step inside a cathedral and you’ll see various forms of holy figures depicted in a way that make the second commandment seem like advice more than a *commandment*.

More important than any religious iconography, God isn’t a tool that can be deployed to support an agenda. This is where I feel some cognitive dissonance.

The inclusion of “In God We Trust” on our dollars is an awful combination of idolatry (for the dollar) combined with making God a tool to use in the fight against Communism (its reason for being put on the dollar and [being made the country’s motto in 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_God_We_Trust#:~:text=The%20following%20year%2C%20Democratic,foundations%20of%20our%20freedom%22)). Though it has [a longer history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_God_We_Trust#:~:text=In%20a%20letter,ignominy%20of%20heathenism%22), that is the reason it is on the US dollar.

God isn’t a picture or words deployed to serve an end.

These things are idolatry. And he certainly isn’t gold or printed paper ([as the bible makes painfully clear](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2032&version=NIV)).

Growing up, a friend of mine would very purposefully omit “under god” from the pledge of allegiance, because he had a secular view of America and believed the pledge/USA had gone too far by including those words.

I think I agree with him now, but for a different reason.

### What God is (to me)

The Bible speaks of God (or the Lord/YHWH/Elohim) in many ways and forms, but given that I’m almost done with my old testament read through, I think I have a good idea of what God is *to me*.

God is the highest good. There’s a reason there’s a “capital G” in it, and not “god.” The unifying concept of Judaism is the oneness of God, and the fact that his greatest followers are the ones who obey him and are rewarded with “a land of milk and honey.”

If you follow what he commands and calls you to do, you and your descendants will prosper. And if you ignore Him, miss the mark (sin), and turn away, then you and your descendants will fall. The prophets use a lot of evocative imagery (a lot of falling to the sword) to describe this fall.

When bad things happen to the Israelites, in the Bible, it is because they are turning away from God and worshipping other things in their lives, like gold or other gods. When Israel/Judah do well, it’s because they honor their God and do what he commands.

God isn’t a priest, rabbi, or imam. He isn’t a holy structure or scripture. And he is certainly not the US dollar.

God is the highest good, the thing that sustains people who follow him.

### 🎶Money, it’s a gas🎶

Money is an engine, but not one that acts for the highest good. It is not something we should trust as a moral compass, but rather as a *fuel*.

That fuel can make us drug traffickers, human traffickers, and weapons traffickers. It can bring about destruction, rape, suffering and death.

It can also make us feed and clothe people, bring prosperity where there was none, and create a truly better world, in the moral *and* material sense.

The thing about having money, printed with a statement about trusting God, is a mockery of the idolatry that’s lambasted in the Bible.

### Why we need God more than ever

Money, now more than ever, has many, many forms it can take in our economy, from derivatives, to crypto, to real estate holdings, to funds that represent physical gold. There are technologies that are coming online that are either the end of humanity or total vaporware (though the reality is probably somewhere in the middle). And we have leadership within our country (and in other countries) that has profound idolatry for the dollar and the markets, while forgetting what this (human existence, striving, and thriving) is all for.

[Gerontocratic murderers and rapists](https://dissentmagazine.org/online_articles/the-epstein-class/) have control of our systems, including the money system. And as long as we trust in them and their tools, we are not trusting in God, at least in the one of the Old Testament. We are trusting in a golden god.

We don’t need to trust in God as the dollar and its commanders tell us to, we need to trust in God as our children and future generations would want us to: we need to do the things that will let us and our descendants prosper.

And I don’t speak this as a devout Christian, or Jew, or Muslim, or someone of any faith, I speak it as someone who believes that morality is divorced from capitalism (though on balance, it’s probably not very moral).

There are evil capitalists and there are good capitalists. But doing good is hard.

The world of our future is one that we will, like every generation before us, have to fight for.

And we *will not* do it by trusting in corrupted and morally reprehensible leaders and fallen institutions.

We *will not* do it by trusting solely in ourselves, in our rationality, or our judgment.

We must trust in our ability to seek to “do good” in the world, and ourselves, that allows us to prosper for a thousand generations. We must take up the hard work of fixing what is broken (*tikkun olam*), by us and by those who came before us.

### What good might look like

Repairing doesn’t always mean fixing the things that happened in your life. Sometimes the repairing means doing good things for other people that are wrongs that don’t right your wrongs, but whose wrongs rhyme with yours.

I grew up feeling like I never had the male role model for me. My dad, who was definitely like me ([and as I’ve gotten older we’ve gotten more alike](/posts/mind-body-soul/)) was not the role model I thought I needed, mostly because we didn’t share the same living space (yay divorce).

I had my step dad as more of a role model, but our interests weren’t always the same (darn genetics). I hope to give back to the world like my step dad gave me the opportunity to have a role model, volunteering with Big Brothers Big Sisters, and finding ways to be mentor or mentor-like for my cousins/nephews, and heck, my own daughter.

For my daughter I want to build the stable home life I didn’t have, meaning one where every other weekend I had to assume a new facade. My daughter should only need to be one version of herself and deal with one home environment.

To me this is what it means to trust in God. To find a way to repair or make good on something that others have passed over, or that mirror foibles and failures within my own life. And as I am coming to find, it often is perpendicular to money or gain for myself. Often the work that I do to try and repair the world I find myself in is work that takes my time, sure, some of the money I have, but most importantly, it means changing who I am, so that I am not a mindless, scrolling, gambling, money toting idolater. It means doing hard work, connecting with real people, and trying to do things that I am not good at (hello, writing), but hope to be better at.

Thanks for reading, and I hope you can find a way to create goodness in the world. I don’t know if I can, but I’m trying.
