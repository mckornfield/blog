### No… Not Avatar the Last Airbender

Interestingly the show uses the order “Air, Water, Earth, Fire”, but the order and the reasoning behind it I heard was from the [Meditation App](https://medium.com/@robfin1/the-way-a-meditation-app-review-2ae7eb28279f) I’ve been using (yes I know, how cliche…)

But this order is something different than “the four elements” of which all the world is made of.

— Technically air is multiple gases, fire is a chemical reaction, water is a molecule, and Earth is either a planet or a bunch of elements/molecules mixed together —

But let’s put the physics aside for the moment and instead consider these elements as: *that which sustain us*. And they do so on different timescales.

### Air — Minutes

Try going underwater for more than a few minutes, and if you’re not a fish, or equipped with scuba gear, you’ll be reminded of your desperate need for air.

We need as much to power our cells with oxygen as we need to expel the poisonous CO2 that builds up in our bloodstream.

No human can go more than a few minutes (5, 10? [25 if you use oxygen before](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2021/5/freediver-holds-breath-for-almost-25-minutes-breaking-record-660285)?) without breathing. Air is the essential element. Deprive yourself of it for a short time and you pass out, and a bit longer and you pass from this earth. Speaking of earth… oh wait no it’s not earth it’s —

### Fire — Hours

Interestingly fire, as a chemical reaction, is more apt of an element to our survival than thinking of it as “the red one” in [Pokemon rock paper scissors](https://pokemondb.net/type/fire).

Fire for the ancients and us represents *heat*. Human beings must be warm, but not *too* warm, meaning our chemical reactions have to occupy a relatively narrow range of temperature (98.6 F or 37 C) in order to work properly.

Going outside of this band significantly, like with very cold water can mean death [within an hour](https://www.emedicinehealth.com/how_long_does_it_take_to_die_from_hypothermia/article_em.htm), or even just in a chilly environment, a few hours.

People can also die of extreme heat, but more often than not that’s because of the lack of our next element —

### Water — Days

You can go a few days without *drinking water*.

Granted, those will be terrible days, and if it’s hotter this can be down into the hours range, but the general timeline is a few days. It’s odd to think about because it’s pretty rare these days to die of thirst, but [after about 3 days](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/325174#how-long-can-you-live-without-water), most of your body’s major functions will grind to halt without water.

Interestingly most of our water loss is related to our other two elements (we breathe a lot of water out, if you’ve ever breathed in cold air you can see the vapor, and we sweat a lot of water out as well.)

So in the event of lots of breathing and lots of heat, you’ll need lots of water. That’s not to say water alone is enough, especially if we sweat, bringing us to —

### Earth — Weeks

It does seem extreme, but a human being can live a few weeks without “earth”, or food, as the last.

Arguably it’s even up to [a few months](https://www.verywellhealth.com/how-long-live-without-food-1132033), but I think that’s probably dependent on your level of exertion as well as your level of body fat before the fast.

As mentioned with the sweating, we lose water but *also* we lose salt that is necessary for our muscles to work properly, and that salt is relatively rare in drinkable water.

The things we eat are “of the earth” — either grown directly out of the earth, or other things that eat the plants, or lastly other predators.

Earth is not necessarily more essential than the other elements, but it certainly is less abundant, in terms of finding things that you can actually eat (although these days we have too much in the way of cheap, crummy food).

### Summary

The four elements might be thought of as the four sustenances:

- Air — Breath
- Fire — Bodily Warmth
- Water — Drinkable fluid
- Earth — Edible food
