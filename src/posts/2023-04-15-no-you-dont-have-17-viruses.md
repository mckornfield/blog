## Nothing is free in the tech world

Looking at your phone, you might peruse something like Twitter, or Facebok. You didn’t pay for those apps unless you have the nice blue checkmark, so they’re “free!”

Or download a Solitaire app or any other game from the App Store/ Google Play Store and you’ll freely enjoy some nice game time.

But the people that make those apps get paid somehow don’t they? Meta has been losing money for a while now but they’re still pulling in billions right?

Well if you didn’t pay for something online, then YOU are the product, as you may have heard.

- Facebook and Google track you and sell ads worth billions
- All those free apps from the app store come riddled with ads

If you don’t want to be tracked; you can “[ask not to track](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT212025)” on iOS, or go with alternative browsers (i.e. Brave/Safari instead of Chrome) or alternative search engines (DuckDuckGo or Bing haha). For Facebook I think you can just delete it? Hahaha!

The other sorts of free apps let you do one of two things: pay for a version of the app that doesn’t have ads, or continue to sit through ads until your eyes glaze over…

Let’s talk about those ads for a moment.

## How to deal with ads that are almost phishing

You might see an ad pop up on one of those “free” apps that looks like:

> Your phone has (17) viruses!

And then links to an app you can download to clean your phone off.

No no no… that app and the ads it producesthe most dangerous thing on your phone… they’ll lead you to waste even more time and possibly even money downloading other useless apps.

Or maybe you’ll see an ad like:

> Your phone is out of storage!

Well, don’t believe anything your free game app tells you.

You can check your storage following [these instructions for iOS](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT201656#:~:text=Use%20your%20device%20to%20check,of%20storage%20each%20one%20uses.) or [these instructions for a Samsung phone](https://www.samsung.com/uk/support/mobile-devices/how-can-i-check-how-much-storage-space-i-have-left/) if you’re really worried.

### How to avoid those ads

Delete the app, or pay to remove ads. Don’t settle for free apps with so many ads, they are a waste of your time and an unnecessary rise in your blood-pressure.

## Good old regular phishing

Just like the alarmist phone ads, you might see something in a text message like

> Your Amazon account has been suspended. Log in in here: bit.ly/abc123

These messages can look pretty convincing, using the right fonts and everything if they’re sent via email. Google has a [pretty good quiz](https://phishingquiz.withgoogle.com/) to help you spot *phishing* attacks, but think of these attacks as something that checks a lot of these boxes:

- They have very urgent/alarming instructions
- They require you to respond/ take action immediately
- The email sender/phone numbers are not something you recognize
- The URLs they require you to go to look very sketchy (bit.ly is an example of URL shortener that can hide an attacker’s URL)
- They require you to enter confidential information to a site that IS NOT the actual site in question (e.g. it looks like, but is not Amazon)
- They ask for you to do strange things like pay with gift cards or cash
- They ask you to download an app/ share your screen (never do this!!!)

Phishing is a [multi-million dollar industry](https://aag-it.com/the-latest-phishing-statistics/#:~:text=With%20an%20average%20of%20%24136,emails%20per%20100%20internet%20users.), so don’t let yourself get caught up in this craziness.

### How to avoid those attacks

Phishing is all about scaring you into action, while also asking you to place trust in people that are ultimately not trust-worthy or related to the company.

If you receive an alarming email, ask a friend/ tech savvy person in your life if it’s real. Don’t forward it to them unless they ask you, but call or text them about it and have them come check it out. Above all: don’t panic and react right away. Almost all legitimate companies will give you some sort of warning, whereas phishers are trying to get you to *react*.

### In Summary

Your phone probably doesn’t have any viruses, you just have free apps with really annoying ads. Also *phone a friend* when it comes to any sort of alarming or scary messages and show it to them, so they can check for you if it’s REAL or just phishing. If you don’t have any tech savvy friends, then now’s a good time to try and make some!
