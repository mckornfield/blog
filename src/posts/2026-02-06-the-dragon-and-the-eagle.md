An overdue travel blog.

### 2011

In 2011, I visited China for the first and only time.

I had the privilege of an aunt that worked for the BGI (Beijing Genomics Institute) who was in the middle of launching a journal, [GigaScience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GigaScience).

I was there a month but we traveled a good amount, and I really got to see what is a truly massive nation, not just in size, but in spirit and detail.

It’s hard for me to remember what I was like back then, but then I look at photos of me being goofy and think… well OK I still am *that* person.

![Making a fool of myself at the Forbidden City in 2011 (photo by my Aunt)](/img/the-dragon-and-the-eagle/0_lfiyAXEpGFPE_VJP.jpg)

*Making a fool of myself at the Forbidden City in 2011 (photo by my Aunt)*

My impression of China, looking at it backwards 14 years and all sorts of information I’ve consumed since then, but I want to speak to something that I think we as Americans (United Statesians?) seem to forget when we talk about China. Not about them as our adversary, but as other people.

### Shenzhen/ Hong Kong

Most of my time was spent in Shenzhen, an area famous for its transformation from fishing village to [tech assembly mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longhua_Subdistrict,_Shenzhen) in the span of a few decades. It struck me as a massive, sprawling city, and one that I just went back and forth between a few sections (a former factory turned science research center, and our hotel).

Listening to [Apple in China](https://www.amazon.com/Apple-China-Capture-Greatest-Company/dp/1668053373), there is an odd, symbiotic but at times adversarial relationship between American companies and Chinese manufacturing, but one that has led to dominance on the part of both countries on the world stage.

Put another way: have you bought a Russian designed computer, built in Korea? Or a British designed phone, made in Japan?

“Designed in the USA, Made in China” is the story of modern technology.

Across the water (and our initial entrypoint via plane) is Hong Kong, which is one of the coolest cities I’ve ever visited.

Beautiful mountains, on an island, shrouded in mist, a gorgeous skyline and ferry ride, pretty sweet public transit, in just about as diverse a city as I’ve ever been to.

I remember hearing a lot of Mandarin, Cantonese, and other languages, as well as Australian accents while we were betting on horses at the horse track (no, I did not win, but had some awesome fried rice).

I know a lot of political things have taken place in Hong Kong, and more than picking a side or trying to make a political statement, it just makes me sad that such an awesome city has had to go through such trying times. I hope for the best for the people of Hong Kong.

### Xi An

Ah this is where I met the pomegranate flower up above. It has the absolutely massive tomb of the terracotta warriors. Pits and pits of unearthed soldiers and horses guarding the tomb of the first emperor.

There’s nothing quite like it in the USA. There are many ways that the native americans have left their mark, but there isn’t anything like the terracotta warriors.

Supposedly they were found by a farmer who was digging a well, and would [still sign tourist books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Zhifa) near the site.

I really was (am?) a dork

### Beijing

The north capital is a massive city. It’s so big that at some point if it manages to grow into Tianjin/Hebei, it’ll be [the largest city on Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jing-Jin-Ji) by more than one metric.

![City as far as the eye can see. That poor backpack had been through a lot (photo by my Aunt)](/img/the-dragon-and-the-eagle/0_G3jFe9JGAWlbeRb7.jpg)

*City as far as the eye can see. That poor backpack had been through a lot (photo by my Aunt)*

But Beijing is much more than a lot of roads and a lot of buildings. It’s also the seat of power of the world’s largest country. It’s the yang (or the yin?) to Washington D.C.’s yin, where I lived for about 10 years. The cities are not really like each other at all, other than containing massively influential central governments.

Beijing has many great restaurants that we ate at, including the one below where I have a coconut in hand. But the Bejing Kaoya (peking duck) was probably the best I had/ will ever had in term of being cooked and prepared just right.

China when I went had very particular things about their food, which you might recall some if you’ve ever seen *A Christmas Story*

- They always leave the heads on the animals (to show freshness!)
- They love family style and lazy Susans
- Chopsticks and spoons only!
- For some reason, no napkins, and they will offer you tissues if you ask (this I never understood)

Beijing was (and I hope still is) a city with quite a lot to do and see. An hour or so away was a section of the Great Wall, which was pretty cool to see… though it was odd to take a chairlift up and slide down. Oh China…

### The Dragon and the Eagle

If you line China and the USA up, they can look geographically similar, and you can multiply the US population by 4 and get China, but other than that, they’re like two different planets.

One has a 4000 year history on display in its land and people, and the other was the result of a flood of European immigrants followed by immigrants from everywhere else (including China) helping to create a land of opportunity at all costs.

The world has a lot of time it can spend focusing on the differences between us and viewing us as enemies and adversaries, but it’s sad to think that will mean removing some of the richness of the world that I had the privilege to see.

Cheers, and 谢谢!
