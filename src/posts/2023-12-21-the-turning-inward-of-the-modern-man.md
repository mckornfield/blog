### Man in the Machine

The modern man has many advantages over his 60,000 year old ancestor. He’s tamed fire to the point of clogging up the atmosphere and bodies of water. He has caged the lion and the elephant. He can soar through the air, or go to the bottom of the sea.

But as modern man’s capabilities have grown, so too have his vices. Fighting the dragon and saving the princess have become virtual acts, that can be played out day in and day out with low cost. Machines, made by men (and originally [programmed by women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_computing)), lock men into vapid lives, where instead of becoming men, they are permanent children.

Allow me to elaborate.

### Your Princess is in Another Castle

Video games have captured people’s attention from their early days. The first Pong machine broke down from being [jammed full of too many quarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pong#Development_and_history). With Nintendo’s rise to power Mario and Zelda became household names.

Nintendo worked with Illumination this year to release a Mario Brothers movie. It gave me nostalgia for the games I spent my childhood playing, and I’m sure plenty of GenXers, Millenials and some GenZers felt that same nostalgia as well, for the sounds and sights.

One of the phrases from the game, when you defeated an earlier boss, was “Our Princess is in Another Castle.” But in a sense, you are the main character, so you could see it as *your* princess. Your goal.

I think this captures the genius of video games pretty well.

You achieve something, but are immediately tasked with more work to do. You get that dopamine hit, from twitching your thumbs and not blinking your eyes for a few hours, and you’re rewarded with more opportunities for dopamine hits, at the cost of more hours and twitchy thumbs (and definitely more cash).

*Please take all my quarters pong machine!*

World of Warcraft… or as my wife’s friend once referred to it, “the other woman” (hahaha), might have epitomized these dopamine hits. I played the game through my teens, and it was an unbelievable time suck. Days spent playing a character to get them to a max level, then more days (weeks, months) of play just to get the best armor and weapons on the character. Or start another character and try a different play style!

*Your Princess is in Another Castle.*

This is a game where you have to pay per month, and you had to pay for each expansion. “World of WarCrack” was another name I’d heard for it. Accurate.

Like any drug, video games have a good reason to exist. People want to be on teams, feel camaraderie, and accomplish satisfying goals. But going out and doing things can be difficult, especially if you’re unhealthy or an anxious person. The lower impedance is to sit in front of a screen and serve up some rewarding dopamine hits.

This isn’t to lampoon video games, but I know plenty of people that bury themselves in games (and I know I did) instead of forging friendships or *relationships* in the physical world*.* Like any drug, video games can be easily abused. It’s anecdotal, but I know plenty more men who have buried themselves in video games as an escape, to their detriment.

It’s easy to simply turn your brain off and chase the princess in another castle, speaking of which —

### Your “***Princess” is in Another Castle***

Many men don’t see women as equals. They view women as objects/prizes to be won. These are men like our former president ([you know which one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_Access_Hollywood_tape)) or any man that only looks at women like he’d look at a trophy.

Seeing women like this is *wrong*. Women are people. They’re the people that made us and helped us grow. But it’s easy to forget that while we’re thinking with our other head.

Pornography is one of the most powerful tools to help men in their quest for objectification. It’s something you can do privately to attain something that usually comes at a significant price (seeing a woman naked and beyond).

Sex is so taboo in many cultures, especially America. America is fraught with being closeted about sex, [banning sex education](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/graphic-many-states-that-restrict-or-ban-abortion-dont-teach-kids-about-sex-and-pregnancy/) and also [banning abortion](https://www.guttmacher.org/state-policy/explore/state-policies-later-abortions) (I mean, if you’re going to make things difficult, you might as well go for expert difficulty).

But the accessibility of pornography, especially online, compared with the difficulty of finding a real partner, is a no brainer for young boys.

Put another way, a coworker of mine said “if you had a 500 page manual on how to find boobs and gave it to a teenage boy, he’d have that thing memorized.”

The danger of pornography, outside of the fact that it is potentially harmful to women, children and men involved in it, is that it gives men something for nothing. It lets them treat women as a means to their end, and reduces/eliminates the need for men to seek women out in the real world. It also provides such a volume and variety that can’t match up to the reality.

And these things work just like with video games. Strong dopamine hits that leave you wanting more. They’re made for you. They trap you in the machine.

### Men Outside of the Machine

The book [*Wild at Heart*](https://www.amazon.com/Wild-Heart-Revised-Updated-Discovering/dp/1400200393)does this subject of what men want better justice than I will. The takeaways from the book are:

- Men want to be powerful and feel powerful
- Men want to go on adventures
- Men want to pursue meaningful things

Pornography and video games easily hijack men, because they offer a facsimile of the reward, without the risk. Get the woman, get the trophy, get the *princess*, all while not risking life and limb.

While that’s the power of these things, it’s also their weakness. Spending time on screens and being (physically and) socially isolated is a quick path to depression. [Men desperately need women](https://medium.com/@cathyreisenwitz/men-need-women-more-than-women-need-men-6b170f74b9da), and not women performing sexual acts on a screen or in a magazine (“magazine, what’s that?” — GenZ).

Men need them because it’s what men are built for. To paraphrase a few million years of biology: if women are built to make and care for babies, then men are built to become worthy of a woman who’s willing to have those babies.

Being worthy doesn’t necessarily mean fulfilling a woman’s every desire (no man can fulfill a woman completely), but it means trying, damn it.

I turned inward for a good portion of my life, and I wish I hadn’t. Having a woman I love in my life is not something I’d ever trade for pornography. Going for runs, using weights and [participating in Spartan races](https://mckornfield.medium.com/running-your-first-spartan-race-84eb7b2f283f) blows away any video game raids I’d done back in the day. These things take a lot more work, but they make me feel like a man instead of a boy.

They’re what I was made for, not what was made for me.

If you’re reading this, and you’re a man who’s turned inward, I hope you turn outward, and show the world all the good you can do.

I’ll end with a quote from someone who captured what being a man should mean:

> We choose to go to the Moon in this decade and do the other things, not because they are easy, but because they are hard; because that goal will serve to organize and measure the best of our energies and skills, because that challenge is one that we are willing to accept, one we are unwilling to postpone, and one we intend to win, and the others, too. — JFK’s Address at Rice University
