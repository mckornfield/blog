How music is a direct link to my past

### “I’m not so naïve, My sorry eyes can see”

Back in 2015 when I graduated from college, my step dad helped move me down to a one bedroom apartment in Virginia (~$1550 a month, woo). I had an IT consulting job in one hand, a physics degree in the other, and little idea what the future held in store for me.

But I also had a few things to ground me. I moved a mix of my college junk, my old room’s furnishings, and some of my step grandma’s furniture into the apartment, so it was familiar yet foreign.

![There’s some dinghy bachelor apartment lighting for you! July 2015](/img/paramore-virginia-running-it-consulting/0_5JgQrJLTfhxbtxW8.jpg)

*There’s some dinghy bachelor apartment lighting for you! July 2015*

I was also, geographically speaking, within walking distance of the school I’d just graduated from (Georgetown). So I was in a new but very familiar place.

I had a bike sitting in the apartment, a desk full of gaming junk, and a job that would suck up all my future time, starting in a few weeks.

But the best piece of familiarity that I still cling to from that time is *Paramore.* I had listened to the band off-and-on but I became **obsessed** that year**.**

I have a music listening habit where I eschew playlists and only listen to “albums”, these magical things that use to come in sleeves that you had to either flip over or start over by moving a needle. If I only got partway through, I’d start over for my next listen.

The [*Riot!*](https://open.spotify.com/album/1PBdA9OY4DwquzRgEmsgkO) and [*Brand New Eyes*](https://open.spotify.com/album/27UqZoE1kV6sIV6uQcI28A) albums were the soundtrack to my life.

I’d get out of my Arlington apartment, hop on my red kick scooter, and kick myself to the DC metro, where I’d wait (sometimes a long time) to ride out to Reston, VA (~15 miles away), then kick propel myself into my IT life, all while Hayley Williams was (beautifully) shouting in my ear.

![It was basically the Sriracha one, but less awesome](/img/paramore-virginia-running-it-consulting/0_e4rBhyukeE2QAihJ.jpg)

*It was basically the Sriracha one, but less awesome*

I learned a ton about myself in this time, because that’s who I had to rely on.

I had great coworkers, but while they were super supportive in the role and helping me be a better worker bee, we were all being paid to do a job.

When it came to emotional and moral support, *I was* *it*.

That was something I’d learn as well.

### “I drowned out all my sense with the sound of its beating”

![This was next to where I worked. Wooo standing desks](/img/paramore-virginia-running-it-consulting/0_aoPab9DYDu0nqcMr.jpg)

*This was next to where I worked. Wooo standing desks*

I spent the next year of my life in a cubicle staring at a computer screen, like [millions of other IT consultants](https://www.statista.com/statistics/831076/it-consulting-in-the-us-number-of-employees/) in the US.

The work was at times interesting (I was doing programming kinds of stuff, woo SQL!),

at times very boring, (I have tested this 5 times and it is still broken Matt, fix it!)

at times scintillating, (Ah that time where someone was sharing their screen and called another person on the call mean names, that was *bad)*

but it was also a time where I was pretty darn *lonely*.

![Lonely IT guy. With Acne and a bit chubby. Banana for Scale.](/img/paramore-virginia-running-it-consulting/0_XHQJgaKbNxeztdzI.jpg)

*Lonely IT guy. With Acne and a bit chubby. Banana for Scale.*

I had my one bedroom apartment, a computer at home, and a computer at work for companionship. I spent plenty of time with my coworkers but we were *working*, not playing.

So I did what I had done for a long time, which was *crush crush crush* on the women I was around. And they were smart, pretty and *very* uninterested in derailing their days old career by, as my wife would call it “pissing in their pool.” (i.e. dating a coworker)

And I cannot blame them at all. But I had all this single guy **angst** that was driving me nuts. So what did I do?

Well… I did actually try some dates that didn’t work out (cheers to you Matt, you terrible dater), but I did find solace in my own two feet…

### “We’ve learned to ***run*** from anything uncomfortable”

Running and lots of walking were the greatest gifts I gave to myself in my early 20s.

Spending hours on a screen, hunched over (even if standing), then kicking around on a scooter and sitting/standing on the metro, I contorted myself into all sorts of neck pain and flabbiness.

But pull on a pair of running shoes, blast *Riot!* loud on earbuds enough to make myself deaf, and off I went for a paltry mile or two (did I mention I was a terrible runner to start? haha)

*Paramore* was a great expression of angst, potential and longing that I felt, but it was also an anchor. I can play the same album right now and get pulled back into that apartment, that feeling of loneliness, and all that potential to be a different person.

I put many hours in walking and running around that apartment, running from “anything uncomfortable.” But that walking and running also helped me feel more than some dude on a computer. I was also a hairless ape, running through forests and alongside rivers. I’ll never get over the view from Arlington looking down into DC, seeing Lincoln’s temple stark against the trees.

![Yes I took this photo, hence all the random stuff in the way](/img/paramore-virginia-running-it-consulting/0_w90THFw4aq2pUMF2.jpg)

*Yes I took this photo, hence all the random stuff in the way*

I’d also just walk to get stuff. I walked to the grocery store. I walked from a party in the north part of Georgetown back to my apartment because, well, I just wanted to walk (it was only 3–5 miles but that’s a long distance in car happy America).

My feet, Paramore and the green and of the DMV area unfolding before me.

If you’re just starting to be a real adult, it’s great to look for something new to cement the time in your mind. Even if that time doesn’t go that great (lol @ Matt dating), it’s fun to think back on.

Or if you’re nostalgic for your past, pop on an album and pick up a habit from back then, and you’ll have access to the best time machine there is: your own mind.

And when it came to running, I did eventually run *towards* something, my beautiful wife and daughter.

It did take me a bit of time to figure that out as well, and not without a lot of self induced pain.

But I am a stubborn person who learns by miles, not steps.

I’ll end with a quote from “Miracle” off of *Riot!*

> I’ve gone for too long  
> Living like I’m not alive  
> So I’m gonna start over tonight  
> Beginning with you and  
> I don’t want to run from  
> Anything uncomfortable  
> I just want, no  
> I just need this pain to end right here

I did keep up the running, lost some flab and gained a beard.
