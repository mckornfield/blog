### The Problem of God

When I was in college, I took a class called “The Problem of God.” It was a survey of different gods/religions and how people had tackled the many existential problems of god and religion: what is he (she, they) like? What do they want from us? Why do we exist? etc etc.

The class didn’t necessarily give an answer to the Problem of God, but presented various philosophers/theologians proposals. But I heard a new proposal recently; when I listened to the book [*Sapiens* by Yuval Noah Harari](https://www.amazon.com/Sapiens-Humankind-Yuval-Noah-Harari/dp/0062316095), I came across a section that brought back memories of the class, and specifically the problem of *evil.*

The book has a section that outlines different forms of why evil would exist with an omnipotent god:

- God gives beings free will, so they can be evil
- Older religions like Manichaeism (think Lord of Light vs. the Darkness in Game of Thrones), where there is a Good God and an Evil God
- Polytheism, i.e. different Gods might have agendas that fall more into the “evil” bucket than others

The last idea the book presents, and one that no culture that we know of has promulgated, is: what if there’s only one God, but he’s evil?

### The God of Evil

A God of Evil sounds like a horrible thing, doesn’t it? Who would *want* to live in such a world? But digging a bit deeper, it may be more plausible than you think. Maybe you don’t have a choice.

In the story [*I Have No Mouth and I Must Scream*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Have_No_Mouth,_and_I_Must_Scream)*,* the Earth has been taken over by an all powerful, sentient and *evil* computer that tortures the few remaining inhabitants.

This god-like computer, AM, is a God of Evil, capable of keeping his subjects alive indefinitely, having control over all of the Earth, just so that he can subject them to further torture. And the reason AM took control? He took over the other AI and destroyed almost all of humanity, once he discovered he could. Evil is powerful because it plays by its own rules.

Of course the survivors do their best to escape, but that’s practically impossible when faced with a God, isn’t it? God makes the rules, you just have to play by them.

So back to plausibility, if we take the idea of [simulation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation_hypothesis) (i.e. that we’re living in a simulated reality instead of a “real” one) and combine it with the fact that evil is, generally speaking, more powerful than good, we might find it more likely than not that we live in a world (simulation) run by a God of Evil.

### Evil is Powerful

A man or woman who is willing to murder, to lie, to commit violence or violent sexual acts occupies a level of power beyond those who live by a moral code or a set of rules.

In the same way that a tiger is more *powerful* than a rabbit, the tiger has more capabilities. If a rabbit wants something, but someone bigger is in the way, it probably has to run away. The tiger can make a choice to run, to climb, or to kill.

An evil person has more ways to play out a situation than a good person does. For example, if an evil person is told they can’t get something they want, they *can attempt to* take it and kill anyone who gets in their way. Being evil gives you a leg up on the social order.

The corollary to being wantonly evil is that, eventually, you’ll piss of the wrong person and they’ll kill *you*.

So the folks that recognize the power of evil *and* are parsimonious about dispensing it are immensely powerful. Hitler didn’t kill his way to being chancellor, he made plenty of “friends” before he started to architect his killings (and he didn’t even do them himself).

So if we up-level this Evil power to the divine, we could see a power consolidation play out, where the God who doesn’t play by any rules can become the one and only God. The God of Evil bides his time, but finds a way to dethrone the other Gods. (Think [Hades in the Hercules movie.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2Ar_Fvid5fI))

And what might the gospel be of this all powerful, God of Evil? I believe it would be:

### The Goal of Evil is the Spread of Suffering

Religions contend with the problem of suffering through many lenses. Suffering in Christianity lets us feel God’s mercy and grace, suffering in the Old Testament is more of a penance for sin, and Buddhists think suffering is part of reality, one that we should seek to rise above.

But if we were to try to characterize evil from first principles, we might say that beyond obtaining power, evil’s goal is the promotion of suffering. Murder, sexual violence, any sort of criminal act we might deem as *evil* fits in the bucket of causing a significant amount of suffering to one or more persons. By not playing by anyone’s rules, Evil makes its own rules, mostly at another’s expense.

In the world of the God of Evil, suffering is what gives him strength. He hopes for the suffering of all creatures, or a great suffering inflicted on a small few.

- The bodily suffering of childbirth? Great for the God of Evil.
- The loss of a loved one? One of the greatest gifts you can give to the God of Evil
- A violent, thoughtless act? Not the best, but the God of Evil approves.
- A premeditated, bloody, cathartic act? It’s what the God of Evil wishes.
- Genocide? Well, now the God of Evil is listening.

While religions of the world must contend with suffering as part of the journey towards enlightenment or salvation, suffering is the *destination* of the God of Evil. This is not about the journey for this god.

Every broken dream, every moment of fear or of longing, feelings of abandonment, these are all gems collected in the God of Evil’s war chest.

In a world where it’s easy to turn a screen on and witness suffering, one can’t help but feel a God of Evil has humanity in his grip. The four horsemen are his angels, spreading his wishes over the Earth.

Any other arguments about why there may be only a God of Evil? How about the fact that —

### Evil is Venerated

It’s easier to recall history’s greatest killers than its life-givers (sorry women, we still love you though).

We know the names of top generals, committers of genocide, and even smaller time makers of evil, like mass murderers or fictional ones (Darth Vader!).

In certain cases we might not remember the names, but we remember events, like school shootings, or bombings or terrorist attacks, that leave their marks on our societies. Acts of obeisance to the God of Evil.

From the perspective of our survival, fear driven brains, it makes sense to remember and teach about evil people and evil acts, so that we can be prepared to fight against them should folks like them come again.

But in the world of the God of Evil, remembering these people is like honoring a saint. They’re to be celebrated; they’ve done exactly what the Evil God wants, which is cause the most suffering for the most people.

Now that evil makes sense in our world, what about things that don’t?

### The Problem of Good/Creation

So our last dilemma to cover is the problem of Goodness and Creation. Since suffering makes perfect sense in the world of the God of Evil, now we have to wrestle with why there is *good* in the world, why things *exist* and are *beautiful*, just etc.

For a rock to fall down a hill, something must put it at the top first.

In the world of the God of Evil, suffering doesn’t happen in a vacuum ([although in space no one can hear you scream?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OjfRhwn-4fw)). The purpose of good, of anything we’d want to be attached to, is so that we can roll down the hill, and lose all the goodness we have. [Think of Job](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Job).

There are more people alive now than ever. For the God of Evil, that means more suffering than ever. More people affected by storms, forest fires, droughts, famines, plagues, pestilence, war and death. More followers who can commit acts of evil. The God of Evil barely has to drop a storm in East Asia and he’s affected the lives of billions of people.

### Entropy

The last vote for the God of Evil is that he echoes the second law of thermodynamics: Systems over time tend to be more disorderly.

From the human perspective, we usually interpret this as waste, or messiness. But entropy has a (literally) darker side to it, which is the total annihilation of everything we know, namely:

- All the people we love
- All the things we’ve built
- The sun swelling to a red giant (bye bye Earth) then wasting away to a white dwarf. (We’ve got a few billion years for that one though.)

The God of Evil sets the direction of the universe to suit his needs. If energy could be created from nothing, if entropy didn’t *tend* to increase to a maximum, it would be hard for the God of Evil to get what he wants after all.

But since he makes the rules of the universe, despite our best efforts, our striving and our suffering will still lead to a dark nothing.

### Rage Against the Dying of the Light

In this world ruled by a God of Evil, it might seem like opposing gravity to be good, to be happy, however transiently. But that’s what humanity has proved itself capable of.

- Defying gravity literally: we can fling ourselves and objects hard enough against the pull of the Earth to break free.
- We can ameliorate suffering with art, so that we feel closer instead of pushed away by the weight of the world.
- We can heal mental and physical wounds, and learn to live with the suffering of the world.
- We can, as Dylan Thomas wrote, “[Rage, rage against the dying of the light](https://poets.org/poem/do-not-go-gentle-good-night).”

The God of Evil might always get what he wants, but we’ll make him work for it. And if we have to find a meaning to our suffering that goes beyond him, I have no doubt that *we can*.
