*This is actually Tysons Corner, not DC, but whatever*

I saw some National Guard in the train station patrolling with weapons… not my favorite.

I saw some old friends, a bit older than they were when I last saw them, and farther along on the family trajectory side, but still sharp and still full of energy. Both stressed out by the new state of technology, but also willing to try and cease it as a new opportunity.

I saw buildings that have been stood up and polished off outside our nation’s capital, bright and shiny in the Spring sun.

I saw a place that I used to call home, no longer my home.

A building I interned in in college is still there, now with an American flag spread across it. It makes me think.

Most importantly to me, in the background of all of this movement, I saw a new cohort of young people.

New graduates, new college students, new professionals clinging to metal bars as they stare at their phones or stare out the window of metal boxes careening down the rails. The same metro stations with the same signs, but with new inhabitants. The same outlines but with new colors.

People of many colors and creeds, all aligned in their pursuit of the dollar and their pursuit of a life among the bureaucracy and bullshit that is our capital.

Drinking, laughing, talking very seriously about their very serious business plans. The next set of movers and shakers. Of lovers and heart breakers. Ah DC, I’ve missed you.

I love you DC, I know you’ll bring all these people here what you brought me: the promise of a new future.
