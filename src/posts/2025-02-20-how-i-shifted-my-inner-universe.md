A former coworker said that working remote is like “being in your spaceship.” I think that’s accurate: you stare at screens, communing with the outside, and only venturing forth for resupplies, but otherwise you’re just floating in the universe.

I’ve felt over the past few years a decent shift in who I am, and I think it’s worth sharing a few analogies that take into account the fact that I’m floating through an inner universe of my own design.

### It’s All Relative

Einstein’s genius is many: [he wrote four papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annus_mirabilis_papers) that changed our understanding of the world, and made momentous contributions to quantum mechanics. He also changed how we think about our universe at the largest scale.

The term *relativity* is important to Einstein’s work. In *special relativity*, he explains how the speed of light is the universe’s speed limit, but that [weird things can happen as you get close to that speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_relativity#Relativity_of_simultaneity).

For this analogy, his contribution to our understanding of gravity on spacetime is relevant, i.e., [*general relativity*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity).

If you’ve never heard this analogy, Einstein conceived of the universe not as large bodies that *pull* objects in, but instead, warp the space (and time) around them. It’s the Earth, or Sun, or any massive object, as a *disturbance* in the fabric of the universe.

![The Earth bending Spacetime (Source Wikipedia )](/img/how-i-shifted-my-inner-universe/0_0gE5dyEsRnFVm0Lj.png.png)

*The Earth bending Spacetime (Source [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity#/media/File:Spacetime_lattice_analogy.svg))*

When any object, including light, gets close to these massive objects, their paths are bent. That means that unless they have enough power to resist, they’re pulled in. We’re not “pulled” to the Earth, so much as *bent towards it.*

### The Light Cones of Our Lives

One representation of special relativity is all the paths you can take through spacetime. I always thought this was a great visual for our lives as well: this is all the *literal* speeds and directions you can take within spacetime.

![A lightcone ( Wikipedia )](/img/how-i-shifted-my-inner-universe/0_PdnCLQqOddHohJ6k.png.png)

*A lightcone ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_cone#/media/File:World_line.svg))*

The edges of the cone are the speed of light (remember, nothing can go [faster than lightspeed](https://starwarsexplained.quora.com/If-the-Millennium-Falcon-can-fly-5-past-light-speed-and-it-made-the-Kessel-Run-in-under-12-parsecs-Star-Wars-IV-A-N)). The middle line of the cone is unmoving, staying perfectly fixed in space (but moving forward in time).

These cones define the boundaries of how you can move.

The paths our lives take also exist within a metaphorical universe, bounded by many factors that make up our own light cones. (Maybe *life cones?*)

Where you are born, who you surround yourself with, what you study, what you work on, what you consume these are the cones that define the path your life will take. And there are hard and fast limits to these cones, based on the experiences you have and the genes you’re given.

I think our “best life” requires being active participants in our own lives. The universe will do its best to push onto a mundane track, but at worst, turn us towards early destruction.

We can do better.

### Tilting Light Cones

In special relativity, these light cones might seem immovable. You can travel *at most* this fast (right near the speed of light), or travel as slow as possible (i.e., at rest). So what’s there to change? General relativity to the rescue.

Back to that picture of the Earth bending spacetime, you notice how the fabric of spacetime is bent? That means the light cones around the Earth are also tipped, ever so slightly, in the direction of the Earth.

More massive objects can tilt these cones more than slightly. They can turn them so that any object (including light) only has one path to go.

![Tipped light cones ( Wikipedia )](/img/how-i-shifted-my-inner-universe/0_MKXCWOEWQydTU5YP.png.png)

*Tipped light cones ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_cone#/media/File:Light_cones_near_black_hole.svg))*

Our universe is full of different types of massive objects, which I think bear strong analogies to objects in our lives that (metaphorically) tip our light cones and suck us.

- **Planets:** These are smaller pulls on us, like an advertisement or a conversation with a stranger.
- **Stars:** Bright and massive, these pull on us harder, like an upcoming race or a test, directing us steadily to where we need to go
- **Black Holes:** As in the diagram above, these leave us no choice about where we can go, *if* we get close enough

### Tilting My Light Cones

For me, there were two times I shifted my universe in the last ten years of my life.

1. Formed a strong relationship with a woman
2. Got in the best shape I’ve been in

Both of them meant shifting my life *away* from black holes that dominated my youth and childhood (video games, pornography, self hatred).

Given that I do nothing, and put nothing else in the path of my universe, myself (and any teenage/adult male) will be pulled in.

To perform that shift, I had to populate my universe with stars that were closer to me, stars that tilted me away from.

![An inner universe (Photo by the Author)](/img/how-i-shifted-my-inner-universe/1_MAAEV2UUiguLvclNT-UE7w.png.png)

*An inner universe (Photo by the Author)*

The difficult part is not doing things, or even, in my opinion, forming habits. I think that all comes as a result. The hard part is reimagining who you are, so that the path you take aims you towards the stars and not the black holes.

We don’t just need to aim for the stars in our inner universe, we have to put them there. Sometimes it involves others (my cousin motivated me to sign up for the race, and the steps in my relationship have always involved my significant other).

### Tilting Your Light Cones

It might help, as an exercise, to see it in your mind’s eye, or write out, the things that are drawing you towards the type of person you don’t want to be. To visualize your inner universe as a set of planets, stars and black holes, in your mind or on a piece of paper.

The star is, confusingly, not who you want to be, or who you will become, but a specific goal that pushes (or tilts) you to be someone else.

- If you want to be more fit, don’t join a gym, sign up for a race.
- If you want to be smarter, don’t just read, aim for a certification or degree.
- If you want to be better at relationships, get out into the real world and give people a real shot

Placing those stars in your inner universe will tilt you towards the planets, the smaller but closer objects that will tilt you even more towards the stars (the training, the reading, the connection).

I think the easy part for most of us is imagining “who we want to be”, but at least for me, that doesn’t really drive any change. Those thoughts can serve to make the “self hatred” black hole grow larger, knowing what we *want* to be but failing to get there.

Take the idea of “wishing upon a star”. Instead of wishing for something to happen, we should wish a star into our inner universe, to tilt our spaceship onto a path towards where we want to go.
