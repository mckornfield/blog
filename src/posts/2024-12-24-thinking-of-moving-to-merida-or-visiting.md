Some thoughts on this colonial Mexican city

![](/img/thinking-of-moving-to-merida-or-visiting/1_YUWUOPzAJFdvxV_ID9r1rg_2x.jpeg.jpg)

I spent a week in Mérida, Mexico this winter and I got a good taste of the good and the bad while being down here. I thought I’d share in case you’re planning to come down here.

### Definitely Worth the Visit

Mérida as a city and the Yucatán as a whole are worth your visit, no matter what. It’s a region that’s relatively flat, with year-round warm coastal waters, and full of interesting culture and wildlife.

You can visit plenty of remarkable sites in the region such as:

- Cenotes — sinkholes filled with crystal clear water
- Mayan Ruins — Chichen Itza is the most famous but there are others
- Haciendas — beautiful Mexican ranches
- El Centro — The city center, with parks, plazas and churches
- La Playa — Only a one hour bus ride from Progreso, a sleepy beach town

The weather is in the mid 70s/80s (or high 20s if you’re European) most of the winter months, so it’s an ideal time to visit between November and April.

### Reasons You Might Want to Move Here

A short list of reasons to move to Mérida (and part of what makes visiting good as well)

- The Cost — Most expensive restaurants are middle to low cost compared with the US or Europe (example: two lunch items, a margarita and a dessert in a touristy restaurant were ~$40 USD.
- The Weather — Gorgeous dry winter weather, though I’ll get to the flipside of that in a moment
- The Food — For the most part, it’s better. The fruit is fresher, the quality is higher, and there’s less of a culture of “throw as much sugar and fat on it as possible”
- The People — Everyone in the area was super kind to us, even as the silly tourists we were. My daughter was having a meltdown and a random lady came over, consoled my daughter, and gave her some crackers.
- The Safety — Mérida itself is a remarkably safe city in México, more than many cities in the US even.
- The Beach Access — Mérida is an hour drive from Progreso, a sleepy beach town, and only 3 or so hours from Playa del Carmen or Cancún. There’s an easily accessible bus from the city center that goes to Progreso.

### …And Reasons You Might Not

- The Language — While more touristy areas know English, knowing Spanish seemed to be pretty necessary here (I can speak it fine)
- The Summer Weather — Apparently it’s HOT, HUMID and HURRICANES in the summer, so much so that people don’t go outside (we talked with a local couple that has been here since February)
- The Expat Community — You’ll find that the community of English speakers is small/concentrated, which might be a problem if you don’t get along (we saw this at the Mérida English Library)
- The Logistics — Getting the right to live here, or some form of employment here that pays well enough, is no simple manner. The rules for living in Mexico without a visa from the US have a six month limit per year, but it does have a form of a golden visa
- The Stomach Pain — While the food is good, the water can be problematic to drink. We had a reverse osmosis setup in our room, but still felt ill at one point in the trip.
- The Internet — While it was OK in the places we used, it wasn’t anything special, and was relatively slow compared to the internet I have in the states (was like 10–20 Mbps). Read that there is Starlink and Fiber but I didn’t see anyone who had those.

Hopefully this helps you think about at least visiting, if not
