### I Talk a Lot, He Doesn’t Speak

The heartfelt story my mom always relates about my brother is that, for one of my birthdays, I was super excited. When she asked why, I said “Jason will finally speak!”

Almost 28 years later and that wish hasn’t come true. My brother has learned a lot of things as he’s grown up, thanks to his speech therapist and many other professionals, but as an Autistic adult ([PDD-NOS](https://www.autismspeaks.org/pervasive-developmental-disorder-pdd-nos#:~:text=What%20is%20PDD%2DNOS%3F,5%20diagnostic%20manual%20in%202013.)) my brother can do very little with spoken words.

It’s ironic to me that he doesn’t speak, considering how full of words I am.

I was constantly being hushed in school by my teachers, and my wife and friends have let me know multiple times that I have a lot to say.

He is not *silent* though, he does make a lot of vocalizations and sounds, but he does not speak in the traditional sense.

When I see him in person, and hear the sounds he makes, he reminds me of a shadow.

### It’s in the Eyes

It’s easy to distance myself from this shadow, since he’s practically non-verbal and still lives at home with my mom.

He goes to a day program and I work a full time job. I have a child and a wife. He does not. Our lives are on different trajectories…

But we *look* like brothers. We are brothers.

When I look into his eyes I see my own eyes. Though with a shade of blue. His hair is a blonder, curlier take on my black hair.

We hold ourselves the same way.

We have similar mannerisms (apparently we both chew SUPER loudly).

We’re about the same height and build (though the medicine he’s on give him a lot of extra weight).

If there’s one thing that’s truly remarkable about your siblings, it’s how similar they are to you, no matter the developmental knowledge gap.

In another existence I could be the mute one and Jason could be the pioneering younger brother. “There but for the grace of God go I.”

### He Represents a Lot of Sadness in my Life

I’ll always be sad when I think of my brother. He has had so much in the way of opportunity, thanks to how successful my mom is. But the world isn’t made for people with mental problems.

He’s been forced out of a pool by security guards. He’s been made fun of and put in a dress by his own father. His college savings fund stolen from by the same, though he didn’t need it (still wrong). It’s even worse to me that he’s been stolen from like an undeserving animal.

And I’m sure other bad things have happened that he has been unable to communicate effectively to me or his mother. But mute men tell no tales.

He’s dealt with things I’ve never had to deal with, due to his communication challenges. And I feel sorry that I didn’t stand up for him in times where I could have. I have failed my only brother.

I don’t think I can really communicate how sorry I am. Though as long as I live I’ll do my best to help take care of him when I’m needed.

Thinking of him; *seeing him,* brings me a deep sadness.

### The Grace of God

Despite this sadness, I think Jason has made this world a better place.

He definitely made my mother the woman she is today; strong, confident, well informed, very successful and the patience of a saint.

All children with mental disorders put intense pressure on their families, and I bet many of them break their parents and/or their relationships and end up in institutions. But my mother remains unbroken. The world is better because she is an individual that works very hard and makes the world a better place, to be the sole provider for her adult Autistic son.

Jason has made me more humble. He has also shown the true character of people in my life, by how they interact with him. I don’t think I have learned the patience that my mother has from him, but he is the mirror of who I could be. I could be locked in my own head, but for the grace of God.

If ever there were a spiritual force in my life, it would be the mirror that is my brother. I am grateful that I have the brain that I have, and accept the sorrow I feel for the talkative brother I might have had, but most likely never will.

I am grateful, sad and humbled to be able to look into that mirror.
