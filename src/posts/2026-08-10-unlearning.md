### On Religion

Growing up, I moved in and out of religious functions. I had a boring, seasonal temple visit with my grandparents (Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur), I attended a more interesting christian church while I was in high school, and then in college I joined the Secular Student Alliance (and had many good friends in it).

I’d been told by many different sources how religion is a twisted tool, how it’s a power over people, how it’s an incarnation of the patriarchy, opiate of the masses, etc.

But once again I find myself involved in a religious community, getting more involved in a church here after moving to San Diego.

In many ways things are the same (I think a lifetime of not believing in god makes one… not believe in god, womp womp)

Though I feel a bit more *compelled* than before.

There’s something completely missing in the other aspects of my life; my work (especially) and my parents and grandparents can fill me up in certain ways, but I think there’s a hole left by them (or even *made* by them) that I find religion is a decent thing to fill that up.

In a sermon on healing I listened to, the pastor told the story ofthe [pools of Bethesda](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%205&version=NKJV).

A man who has been sick many years complains to Jesus that he can’t get into the pool because people are always cutting him off. Jesus says “Rise, take up your bed and walk.” And sure, the man is healed.

The sermon got to the heart of this meaning (not *holy spirit activate!)*, which is that we cannot heal ourselves with external things, but only with something *internal*.

And for religious people, that thing is God, or the Holy Spirit.

I started meditating a year ago, then fell off it, but during church sermons, when we bow our heads to pray, I feel the same energy. (I’m also dragging myself back into meditation, since I think it’s good for me, even if it’s boring.)

Religion is touted as this evil, binding, anti-science thing, and those are good *bad* examples (of which there are many). The church I go to is a small, dedicated community of people who are trying to help with the suffering that its members face, share in joys, and show them how to live better lives.

I’m stereotypically leaning into being more religious as I age, not just for me, but because I want my daughter to have that muscle of belief and hope that for me is all but atrophied. I don’t want to dump the anti-religious nonsense that I ingested/produced throughout my childhood on my own kid. Religion can be a very good thing.

After Sunday school she was singing the songs we sang in the class. Small thing but, I love to hear her sing, and I am grateful that she has an opportunity to learn about religion in a good environment.

To my current self, religion/god is another lens by which to view the world, one that I threw the baby out with the bathwater, and I know I still struggle to find my tribe. But people who don’t believe these words have power instead put their power in other things. And those other things are dangerous.

### On Wealth

Wealth has been a central part of my life; my mom has always been a hard worker and very financially savvy. Halfway through my childhood she came into her own as an earner.

As an adult, I’ve made a decent amount of money, worked hard and got lucky career-wise, but I find myself increasingly dissatisfied with the whole enterprise of “wealth creation” and “wealth management”.

Wealth is also a big part of why [I don’t talk to my dad anymore](https://mckornfield.medium.com/a-letter-to-my-dad-be06a7f609d8). Not a lot of money in question, but enough to take our not great relationship and sink it to its grave.

*Taking* wealth is one way to achieve it. It’s the scarcity mindset behind unaffordable housing in zoned neighborhoods, actual theft, and the casino economy we’re living in.

Casinos make money because they *take it from you*. Wall Street makes money by charging fees (taking it from you). Vampire capitalism is ascendent.

But it doesn’t have to be that way.

Recently my greatest use of wealth has been to give it to friends or family, not because I have to, but more the opposite, because I *don’t* have to, because it *doesn’t* benefit me.

My mom has always taught me to be generous with money, by herself being generous. (Ex: she’s always given Christmas presents to a family in Louisiana with a kid like my brother.) It’s something that I’ve come around to, that the power of money is not the *taking* but the *giving*.

The money engine operates on cashflows and loans, but the real goodness in this world is in giving things *and not expecting anything in return*.

The opposite, which is what we have as a society, is that “[*The rich rule over the poor, and the borrower is slave to the lender*](https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Proverbs%2022%3A7)*.”*

There are two types of wealth in this world, a wealth of abundance (more and cheaper goods, higher efficiency, better tools) and a wealth of scarcity (limited university seats, unaffordable homes, long wait times/high costs for medical care).

The wealth of the former is receding farther and farther into America’s past (and China’s present), whereas the wealth of American scarcity is in its heyday.

We can say America is rich, but its richness is in its scarcity, not its abundance.

### On Sexuality

I had many friends in college who were gay. I have an LGBTQ+ sibling. I consider myself non-judgmental/supportive of their views.

But when does non-judgment also become acquiesence?

In the (practically non-existent) homophobic/transphobic corner of politics, we have people who deny that other people feel/think a certain way. I remember receiving Joe Kent’s transgender surgery ads in the mail and thinking “wtf why would you send this to me.”

In the ally corner, I find a completely chaotic world. This is the place where, for the longest time, I thought people who were against gay marriage where immoral, and if only enough equality was given to LGBTQ+ folks, they’d be in a better place.

But the gay men I knew/know had incredibly dangerous hookup lifestyles, ones that I rarely saw paralleled (though I believe they exist) in heterosexual couples.

There was more drama (the sort that gets you killed/in jail) among a small cohort of gay men than the entire heterosexual populace when I was in school.

I don’t think the answer is to channel our inner Leviticus. Reading this strikes me as a bridge too far.

> “If a man has sexual relations with a man as one does with a woman, both of them have done what is detestable. They are to be put to death; their blood will be on their own heads.”

Yeah… nope.

I think it comes down to the gas pedal and the brake.

Women are the brake in a relationship (they say no), men are the gas (they try to make you say yes.) When you get two gas pedals together, you get some extreme outcomes. STDs/STIs, the sorts of drama I’ve mentioned, and ultimately, none of it results in more children.

I don’t think these lifestyles are evil, and I don’t think because people are made a certain way, that they should be punished. But blanket acceptance of behaviors and lifestyles, especially dangerous ones, is something I struggle to understand. I don’t think it serves anyone either.

### On America

The War in Iran has reignited what might be the most difficult tension of my life and even of my parent’s, between Goliath (America) and David (Iran).

This is not to paint them as good/bad moral entities, but rather to demonstrate that one has trillions of dollars in military might and bases around the world, and the other has used terrorism, hostages and oil to bring the former to its knees.

Through my life the US has been “playing in the sandbox”, i.e., sending men and women to die and to kill many other men and women for reasons that are hard to capture ([especially when they are completely fabricated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_and_weapons_of_mass_destruction)).

As a nation, I think America suffers from many afflictions (psychosis, money idolatry, militarism, diabetes and obesity). The US exports these afflictions to the rest of the world, via smartphones and smart bombs.

As I grew up I think my views came across as anti-American, and so did the last few paragraphs. But back to the sexuality piece: you can challenge someone or something without hating them or wishing them evil.

There is good that America has done for the world: the development of child saving vaccines, the development of information technology and the Internet, and the establishment of an international trade and monetary policy.

In recent history, we’re doing our best to claw back that good. As much by DOGE as by impotent policies that don’t move the needle.

Saying that we’re great and *being* great are not the same thing. I don’t think we’re the good guys, nor do I think we’re the *only* bad guys, but I know we’re not blameless victims or shining paladins.

The most I can say, I’m not proud of what we’ve done in recent memory, and hope that we can do better.

The thing about America is that I’ve been told by people that if only the Democrats/Republicans had complete control for a period of time, we’d be in such a better spot.

I find myself increasingly thinking “neither of these groups has any interest in making the lives of regular people, or America, *or* the rest of the world, better. Only their own lives.”

Oligarchy, gerontocracy (“old-igarchy”? [as I heard here](https://www.amazon.com/Gerontocracy-America-Hoarding-Power-Wealth_and/dp/0374607648)) is the real driving force of our nation: one where a hollowed out corpse of a nation is puppetted by an increasingly senile, rich and incapable group.

We can aim blame up and down the stack of people inside and outside of the American system.

But the system is working very well for those that it serves.

For the rest, it’s not doing so hot. That includes Americans. And that includes the rest of the world.

America has so much potential, but it’s being redirected in a way that I find myself disillusioned with it not just as a country, but as my home.

### On Other Countries

I might look to other countries with rose colored glasses, but I see a lot of people who flee/leave their own countries to come to America. So… it can’t be all that great where people are coming from?

A core problem with this is that a lot of the countries the US demonizes as sending in their refugees have problems *because* of the US.

&lt;spiderman pointing meme>

I don’t just mean the classic propping up of dictators, I mean the more nefarious network of human trafficking, drugs and all the other badness that has its root as the US as its main consumer and/or producer of these maladies.

It’s hard for me to have stood up for 12+ years, pledged allegiance to a flag, and then spend my whole adulthood thinking, as the planet heats up, and nation is at nation’s throat, and we spend our time here bickering about it all —

*What the f — k did I learn?*

I learned some things that may have been true, but are no longer true.

Or are only partly true.

I look to other countries not as inspiration, not as hope, but as something else, as both the source and the recipient of the suffering America inflicts.

America is a nation of immigrants. To say America is causing all the world’s problems is to once again point at the armies of immigrants that have come and formed this nation.

When I have traveled or read about our country or other countries, I get just a teeny bit more perspective on the evils that exist within and without. Other nations do have things that they do better than America, and things that they do worse. And learning about them means unlearning about how “America is the safest place” or “America is the most dangerous place” to live.

### In Closing

The greatest mechanism I have to unlearn is to adopt other perspectives, when the ones that I have, have stopped serving me. And I think, though it might come off as weird, I’d like to end this with a prayer.

*God, give me the strength to better understand myself and others. Give me compassion to understand where people come from, and why they believe what they believe about god, money, sexuality, and the United States. Give me the grace to learn what I need to, and unlearn that which shackles me to a lesser version of myself.*

Thanks for reading.
