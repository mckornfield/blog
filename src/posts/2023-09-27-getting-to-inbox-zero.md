Some tricks to help reduce your email noise

![the beauty that is “inbox zero”](/img/getting-to-inbox-zero/1_fKw_dsSKw36gPtXcsPdd3w.png.png)

*the beauty that is “inbox zero”*

My old boss introduced me to Inbox Zero (i.e. keeping a clean inbox). For him, a person who received 100s of emails a day, it was an effort to keep his inbox clean, but one that he used so that he never missed a useful piece of information. I’ll share what he and others taught me to manage our inboxes.

### Archive, Never Delete!

He said the number one rule is to “archive, never delete!” You don’t want to accidentally delete an email that you actually needed, since getting them back is not exactly a straightforward process. And storage is cheap, so why save the pennies deleting emails when you can just archive them?

Archiving moves them out of your inbox, but lets you search for them later, especially if you remember to…

### Crack Out the Labels

![Labels are free!](/img/getting-to-inbox-zero/1_VvfE5K5ygG2Z4FS6wLiOUw.png.png)

*Labels are free!*

This is pretty basic, but I label emails that I’m going to look at later, like travel itineraries, or making sure returns I sent went through. When I no longer care about the email, I’ll go back in to the label view and drop the label.

But you might say “inbox zero is impossible, I get too many emails!” To that I say —

### Unsubscribe Religiously

![That “unsubscribe” link, gotta love it](/img/getting-to-inbox-zero/1_SMTKJo_Umg3gBVCTo4VjMQ.png.png)

*That “unsubscribe” link, gotta love it*

Most emails have an unsubscribe button at the bottom of them (or with Gmail, sometimes it’s right next to the sender, as shown above). If you get an email that you don’t want, it’s time to unsubscribe!

If it’s not a phishing email (i.e. the URL/domain are trustworthy), then simply click the link and follow the instructions to unsubscribe. I find that it works 90% of the time, though there might be some delay in the unsubscribe going through.

For the 10% of emails that unsubscribe doesn’t work for, or that you can’t unsubscribe from at all…

### When You Can’t Unsubscribe… Send to Spam/ Create a Filter

My default for emails I don’t want but can’t unsubscribe from is simply to click the 3 dots next to the email and click “Block —.” They end up in my spam folder, which I glance at every so often, highlight them all (assuming there wasn’t anything mistakenly put there), then delete them forever (not even worth Archiving in my opinion).

For some categories of emails, you might want to still *receive* them, but don’t want them to show up in your inbox. Email filters are the way to go; they are accessible in Gmail in your settings. Here’s an example of how you might label an email, but archive it and mark it as read so it doesn’t even appear in your inbox.

*Filtering in Gmail, woot*

Now that you’ve stopped the unwanted email onslaught to your inbox, are there better ways to process those emails? Well why don’t you try…

### Keyboard Shortcuts!

![Don’t touch the mouse!](/img/getting-to-inbox-zero/1_2uAlbP3bdZs-nYFRXnS7dw.png.png)

*Don’t touch the mouse!*

Where I used to work, one of my coworkers was *religious* about his usage of keyboard shortcuts. So much so that he would wag his finger at you if you used your mouse too much. He used shortcuts for everything: the browser, his email, writing code. No mouse required; the man was a keyboard shortcut *expert.*

I still use the mouse a bunch, but I aspire to be as good as him! The secret to trying to be like my coworker?

Being able to call up the keyboard shortcut list!

On Gmail, you can press “?” (i.e. Shift+/) and it will call up the keyboard shortcut window. My favorites:

- g , i (go to your inbox)
- Up/Down arrow keys (move between messages in your inbox)
- Enter (select a message in your inbox)
- j , k (move between emails)
- e (archive your email)
- l (add a label!)
- \* + a -> e(select all, then archive)

Now, you’re flying through your inbox, but you receive an email that’s going to take a bit of work to handle. What do you do? I personally —

### Use a Todo List (Tasks/Todoist) or Star Emails

I’m a big fan of todo lists. They give you a way to prioritize work and also see how much you really have on your plate, all in one place. I try to follow the concepts from [*Getting Things Done*](https://gettingthingsdone.com/)*,* where all my tasks that take over 5 minutes to do go through my “in tray.”

So if I have an email that is going to take me more than 5 minutes to respond, I have two options:

1. Add it to my todo list (urgent/important but I need to schedule)
2. Come back to it later (star it!)

You can use whatever todo list tool you like; I use Todoist and it has a [chrome extension](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/todoist-for-gmail/clgenfnodoocmhnlnpknojdbjjnmecff) that works with Gmail, but also I just use the keyboard shortcut for the mac app (ctrl+cmd+a) to add a todo item and copy the email in.

For the starring, I have a weekly todo item to go through my starred emails and process them. Generally they’re some sort of reading material that it takes me time to get through, and sometimes I’ll just add the reading material as a todo item to spread out the work.

---

That’s what I do for inbox zero! I think the main benefit is psychological; it feels pretty great to have an empty inbox, and I know that whatever is coming in is most likely worth my time, since I’ve curated what lands in my inbox through unsubscribing/filtering. Let me know if you have any other tricks you use for inbox zero!
