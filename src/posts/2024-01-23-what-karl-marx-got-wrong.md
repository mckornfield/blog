### Not Real Communism

Almost all arguments against capitalism and for communism, despite the massive failures of communist regimes, come down to “that’s not real communism.” I don’t really have the years of academic experience to cut down *Das Kapital* point by point, but I think I have some examples I’ve come across in my life that might persuade you. Karl got some things wrong, like not understanding —

### The Matthew Principle + Pareto

> Whoever has will be given more, and they will have an abundance. Whoever does not have, even what they have will be taken from them. — Matthew 13:12

The Bible is a rich document, but this little line is really the only rebuttal you need for a communist society.

Not simply people, but *creatures* follow this distribution, the [Pareto Distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_distribution). It can be summarized as: “80% of outputs come from 20% of inputs.”

For many systems, the 80/20 part is a rough approximation, but it essentially captures the Matthew Principle.

[Think of wealth in the US](https://www.statista.com/statistics/203961/wealth-distribution-for-the-us/). The top 1% have 16.6% of the wealth (16/1) and the top 10 percent have 53% of the wealth (53/10). Once you reach the 50–90% range, you have another 30% of wealth (83/50).

While not exactly 80/20, in some ways it’s “better” (i.e. there is a relatively flat middle part), and in other way’s worse (concentration at the top 1% is truly nuts, almost 20% of all wealth).

*Those who have will be given more.*

There’s more than one reason for this (tax evasion?!) but the main reason is: wealth begets wealth. If you have money/assets, and you don’t put them into a large pile and set them on fire (crypto?) then the value of your assets will grow. Meanwhile, folks at the bottom tend to simply have debt and depreciating assets (wooo cars).

But there’s not a place you can go, even in a communist country, where you don’t see the effects of a Pareto distribution. Just [look at Cuba](https://wid.world/country/cuba/), the numbers are:

- Top 1% -> 14.9% of the wealth
- Top 10% -> 45% of the wealth
- Bottom 50% -> 12.5% of the wealth

These are just numbers, so maybe a visual will help. Imagine Cuba is represented by a group of 100 people at a party. The top 1% is just one person. They come first in line and take a pretty sizable slice of cake and walk away. Then the top 10% come by, 9 more people, and they cut two more decent size pieces of cake.

The remaining 90 people get to split a little more than half of the cake, taking smaller and smaller pieces.

And the way the line is, by the time person number 51 makes it to the cake, there’s essentially one “slice” left. But they can only take a small sliver, since it’s divided among the other 49 people behind him.

That’s a Pareto party, and to be honest, it kind of sucks. It’d be great if we could just seize the means of production and divide the cake evenly, right?

### Don’t Make the Professors Become Farmers

The greatest way to cause mass starvations is to try to re-engineer a food production system from the top. Just ask:

- [The Ukranians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_famine_of_1930%E2%80%931933)
- [The Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Leap_Forward)
- [The Cambodians](https://cambodialpj.org/article/justice-and-starvation-in-cambodia-the-khmer-rouge-famine/#:~:text=During%20this%20time,in%20modern%20history.)

When I was in high school, a woman whose parents fled Cambodia and the Khmer Rouge came to speak. There were plenty of atrocities going on, but the thing that stuck with me is “making various types of professionals become farmers.”

Her parents were very smart people, able to come to the US and do well for themselves in Texas. But the Khmer Rouge, like every (that’s not real) communist government, sought to undo Pareto and make everyone into a farmer (while also fighting a war and kicking out foreign aid).

The reality is, farming is a specialized, difficult profession, just like being a professor or any high level professional. The [US farming population is about 1–2%](https://www.fb.org/newsroom/fast-facts) but can feed our country and beyond. This is another extreme Pareto case, one that is important to consider.

Asking any professional to come out from behind their normal workstation and get to farming will not make this system more productive. It’s highly mechanized and industrialized, and the people that work in it are the right people for the job.

A real danger of communist thought is that anyone can do anything. The Pareto based reality qualifies this: anyone can do anything *badly*, and individuals can do a very specific set of things very well.

Instead of one giant cake divided unevenly, you get a much smaller cake that everyone gets a crumb from.

### Is Capitalism Oppressive?

The last part I want to argue against from my experience is the idea of capitalism as an oppressive force, of the worker/proletariat as someone squished underneath the foot of the rich bourgeoisie.

In my experience, people take as much advantage of the system as the system does of them. I’ve worked with people who skate by, while ogling women or simply writing useless emails.

I’ve seen people who have really gotten screwed by a system, and I know very well that people have screwed the system ([yay COVID unemployment fraud](https://www.gao.gov/blog/more-fraud-has-been-found-federal-covid-funding-how-much-was-lost-under-unemployment-insurance-programs#:~:text=In%20our%20new%20report%2C%20we,paid%20out%20during%20the%20pandemic.)).

Nothing I’ve read has really pointed to capitalism as the core issue for oppression in either direction. [Francisco’s money speech](https://www.capitalismmagazine.com/2002/08/franciscos-money-speech/) I think points out that money is tied to survival, and any association between money and evil is spurious.

Survival is the key point: existence is brutal. People can live crummy lives and be at the whim of the dollar, but the alternatives are not great (like living in the woods on your own, or trying to barter in a homeless camp, or [living in the world’s scariest country](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/article/how-did-the-north-korean-famine-happen)).

I’ve never heard of a democracy going through a mass starvation, but I’ve heard of one for every communist country ([yes Cuba too!](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/millions-of-cubans-facing-starvation-hunger-is-fuelling-an-exodus-of-desperate-refugees-writes-phil-davison-from-havana-1417691.html)). If you want to talk about being ground underfoot, I can’t think of a better way than starving to death.

To assume that workers will wake up and “seize the means of production” is to make the same vapid assertion that a person who is *not* a farmer can just become a farmer by sheer willpower. Interest and talent are what make people good at something, not years of being forced to do it.

Making a product people want, running a successful business, [these are things that very few people can accomplish](https://www.investopedia.com/financial-edge/1010/top-6-reasons-new-businesses-fail.aspx#:~:text=Data%20from%20the%20BLS%20shows,to%2015%20years%20or%20more.). A communist would have you think that the person hand assembling phones can just run the company the next day. The reality is that no, they really can’t. (And vice versa, the CEOs can’t all just hop down and hand assemble the phones). A functioning world is built on the backs of experts, not laypeople or amateurs.

### Summary

- The world is dominated by slanted ownership/production, and it’s hard to change that
- Trying to make non-farmers farm is a good way to starve
- Capitalism is a tool that *can* be used to oppress people, but so is every form of government.
