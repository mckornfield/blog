### My First Game Making Attempt

I tried making something with a friend of mine in [Unreal Engine](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US), an open source game making tool.

It was pretty fun but the learning curve felt pretty steep to me. After doing a succinct Udemy tutorial and messing around for hours with some physics in the game, I felt a bit overwhelmed by the amount of things to learn.

Consider I wasn’t even doing any coding (it was all using Unreal’s blueprint editor), there was a lot to work on and a lot to figure out. It seemed like it was going to take a good amount of time to get anything going. And then there’s things like multiplayer, distributing the game, etc.

Fast forward in time a bit, and my nephew is super into Roblox (well he has been for forever), and it’s played by around [200 million other people](https://backlinko.com/roblox-users) every month. If you don’t know what Roblox is; it’s a game and platform where folks create and play games of all shapes and sizes, from shooting games, to survival horrors, to pet simulators. All of it is built around a blocky core look and feel that looks like that [Kanye music video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cwQgjq0mCdE).

So when my nephew said we should build a game, I thought, why the heck not? Let’s *make* something instead of consuming something.

### Why Roblox Studios over Unreal or Unity?

Here is a short list of reasons why I think it’s better to create something with Roblox Studio over the other open source game engines:

- You have a dedicated game and player base you can roll out to with a simple “Publish” action
- Lots of assets are easily shared and available for use in Roblox Studios, like boats, planes, characters, landscapes
- It’s easy to find help on the [Roblox forums](https://devforum.roblox.com/) when you’re trying to script/ make something
- [The documentation isn’t too bad](https://create.roblox.com/docs)
- Scripting is in Lua, a relatively simple language to understand ([compared with C++ or C#](https://www.reddit.com/r/gamedev/comments/n8h7k6/c_vs_c_vs_lua/))
- My nephew likes Roblox :) and I don’t think it’s so bad either

You don’t have as much freedom on many aspects of the development, but that can be a good thing. If you’ve ever heard of [choice paralysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_paralysis%27), those open source engines are more prone to it, in my opinion. There’s a lot more freedom on how characters can look, as well as environments, or how you can script/code different events. Roblox Studios has a pretty limited set of ways to do things, though people do make some darn good looking games.

### How do I get started?

![Photo by Todd Quackenbush on Unsplash](/img/want-to-build-something-with-your-kid-try-roblox-studios/0_HEtrjkewHIogACwD.jpg)

*Photo by [Todd Quackenbush](https://unsplash.com/@toddquackenbush?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)*

Just jump in! Well… I think there’s really a handful of things you’re going to want to do besides just install Roblox Studios and get going.

1. Do some simple tutorials, either offered by [Roblox themselves](https://github.com/kikito/lua_missions) or from people’s [YouTube channels](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aSwGwX7HbOE). You can do them as part of your first game!
2. (Optional) If you’re going to get into scripting, it might be good to learn some [Lua](https://github.com/kikito/lua_missions). People that develop in Roblox Studios are usually divided into “Builders” (game assets) or “Scripters” (scripted elements).
3. Find a willing compatriot, be it a nephew, niece, son or daughter, or heck someone of any age. But it’s a great way to build something with kids who are already interested in playing games. And building is more fun with others (at least for me).
4. Come up with WHAT you want to build, and lay out some simple tasks you and a friend/ family member can accomplish quickly.
5. Publish your game and try it out!

You probably won’t make any money etc. from making these games to start, but you’ll learn a ton from making them.

Ideally you can get better at making the games and can collaborate/ work with folks on games that do actually make some sort of profit (Roblox is free to play but has all sorts of in game monetization opportunities). More importantly you can learn useful skills, like programming, time management, project management, and collaboration. All while spending time with a family member.
