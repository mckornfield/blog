This is some [possibly controversial advice](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/03/29/couples-who-pool-their-money-are-more-likely-to-stay-together.html), but since we’ve been married my wife and I have had separate bank accounts, and I think it’s worked out pretty well.

I’ll share with you why I think it’s actually a GOOD idea to keep things separate.

### Should Married People Police Each Other’s Spending?

Heck no.

I didn’t get married so that I could question why my wife took out a certain amount of money from our bank account. Or what clothes she bought with our credit card.

The problem is that certain personality types (mine especially) are highly skeptical. That means I’ll be skeptical of transactions that don’t come from my own brain, no matter what they are. (Meanwhile, I buy a bunch of random junk off of Amazon, hypocrisy!)

The only way for me to not question someone’s transaction is to have no part in it. So my wife has her own credit card and her own bank account. If she spends money using either of those things, I don’t see it and I’m not allowed to see it. As it should be.

But my wife stays home and takes care of our 1.5 year old, so how does she have money to pay for things? Well…

### Childcare is Not Free

So I pay her.

I pay her every week what I would pay someone if we had a full time nanny. She’s also on my health insurance, and I don’t expect her to pay for anything room and board related, so I’m usually not too worried about her financially.

Some folks we know have pejoratively called what I pay her an “allowance.” But I don’t pay her to do house chores. She definitely does more than I do around the home, but I still will do my share of cleaning. I pay her for childcare, which is both not free and not something you’d simply give someone an allowance to do. It’s a full time job.

To round it out, I have a handful of credit cards I give her for purchases related to our kid or us in general. I don’t expect her to pay for those sorts of things out of her own pocket off of her nanny’s salary.

Those are “we” purchases. And of course my skeptical self is present for those types of purchases, but a lot of times they are grocery transactions, and we share an account so I can see the receipts easily.

### In Summary

If you have problems with questioning your spouse’s purchases, maybe it’s time to split your finances. Since you’re married, this stuff is [technically an illusion](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/marriage-property-ownership-who-owns-what-29841.html), but a powerful one.

And [childcare ain’t ever free](https://www.ted.com/talks/jeannie_suk_gersen_how_understanding_divorce_can_help_your_marriage?language=en), so if you’re not paying your significant other to do it, you’re not making it clear what the value is of the work that they’re doing.
