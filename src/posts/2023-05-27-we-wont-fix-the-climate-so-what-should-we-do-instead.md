### The World is Ending!

Doomsday is nothing new. It’s written into many religious works, like the Christian New Testament. One of the best Marvel films is a highly entertaining reimagining of the Norse myth of Ragnarok, the end of the world (or as it plays out in the film, the end of Asgard).

These days the people that say the world is ending are not religious folks, or people wearing sandwich board signs about repenting.

Instead they are environmentalists and the UN, food companies and billionaires, who say “we have to solve the climate crisis, or else…”

The evidence is incredibly strong for [Anthropogenic Global Warming](https://climate.nasa.gov/evidence/). The simplest evidence is just the pictures we can take of the polar ice caps from space. At the same time of year, just rolled forward a few decades.

![Where da ice at? (from Wikipedia )](/img/we-wont-fix-the-climate-so-what-should-we-do-instead/1_a0lp6TTZTdMY5uKuYHuR5g.png.png)

*Where da ice at? (from [Wikipedia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Arctic_Sea_Ice_Minimum_Comparison.png))*

Plenty of other things point to the evidence, but this article isn’t going to disprove Climate Change (doing so would be an exercise in cherry picking data). It’s to ask, what are we doing to stop it?

Really, nothing. And we should stop deluding ourselves that we are going to fix this, and instead prepare ourselves for the *Brave New World* we’ll soon be living in.

### Things That Directly Cause Climate Change

The climate is a big, complex system. Our planet is just under 3/4 covered in Ocean water, and that large system contributes heavily to the climate and ecosystem we experience. There’s really only one thing that causes climate change:

- Putting stuff into the air, that wasn’t part of the normal cycle

Methane, CO2, NO2, and all our chemical friends, floating up into the atmosphere. Stupid chemistry, ruining our temperate summers.

When we talk about trying to avoid temperature increases, whether in UN climate change reports or in US Democratic talking points, we really center pretty heavily around “decarbonization”. I think in some ways decarbonization is pretty good (especially for the US, with a heavy reliance on foreign carbon sources), but making it the single issue is *silly.*

If the USA, or China, or any nation, were to overnight replace every single ICE car with an EV, they’d see a pretty marked rise in electricity use. And in some places (like California), electricity is already in very high demand and priced very high. Renewable resources, while not the bogeyman that the oil companies make them out to be, come with plenty of their own costs (mostly land).

Just subtracting carbon is not going to save the world (or what we’re really talking about, the people living on it).

### Things that Indirectly Cause Climate Change

If putting stuff into the air is the direct cause, it might help to ask *why* we put so much *stuff* into the atmosphere. We talked about the natural boogeyman, cars, but there are plenty of other categories, like agriculture, industry and just powering your house. These things are comparably energy intensive in the US.

![(from C2ES )](/img/we-wont-fix-the-climate-so-what-should-we-do-instead/1_y_ZPZwgyPPAIn-qn7uTV6w.png.png)

*(from [C2ES](https://www.c2es.org/content/u-s-emissions/))*

But all these industries are doing the same thing, right? They’re emitting those dreaded greenhouse gases.

They’re not just pumping things into the atmosphere for fun. These are billion or trillion dollar industries we’re talking about.

- Electric Power -> People need electricity at night, or when it’s not windy (and apparently Nuclear power is too scary)
- Industry -> Making things takes a lot of resources (duh)
- Agriculture -> Industrial farming, a ton of mechanization to maintain vast amounts of cattle and crops
- Transportation -> Almost one car for every person in America (and commercial airlines are the only efficient way to get around)
- Commercial/Residential -> Americans live in highly suburban, separated environments, with massive, half the time unoccupied office buildings

All of these bullets really comes down to one indirect cause:

- We put stuff in the atmosphere **to achieve lifestyles/ consumption patterns that we want, or society wants us to have**

### Why We Won’t Fix the Climate Crisis

We’re essentially on a snowball of human consumption.

As a thought experiment: Let’s say tomorrow, no one went out and bought anything besides food from the grocery store, or items to make their own food.

People didn’t fly on planes, they stopped going to work if they had to drive their ICE cars to get there. They deleted their Amazon accounts, and went on a super budget of “only what I need to survive.” They stopped running their heating and cooling in their house, or just pitched a tent (let’s all become homeless?). People threw their smartphones on the ground and stomped on them, and just started living their best “survival life.”

What you’d probably see, very quickly, is society disintegrate, the NASDAQ go to zero, and people die off pretty quickly from infected wounds and some of the classic medicine 1.0 killers (ahem cholera). Not great.

Some parts of the world are this technologically averse, and they are also lowest on the consumption totem pole (i.e. they aren’t taking the planet for everything she’s got).

Our core problem is, that people aren’t going to give up this lifestyle.

They can work a job where they’re not worried for their life, food is guaranteed, they’re comfortable and protected from the elements and predators. And increasingly this “western” lifestyle is being lived in Africa and Asia, large population centers that until very recently didn’t have access to this high consumption/ high output technology. Are we going to say “please don’t buy all of this stuff that helps promote our nice lifestyle?” Most likely not.

It comes down to two reasons why we won’t fix this climate crisis:

- The climate crisis is built on a culture of high (over?) consumption
- That culture is being marketed and exported to every part of the human world

We rolled this snowball down the hill, and now that it’s only some of the way down, we’re going to try and stop it with EVs and Smart Homes? Please. More consumption isn’t going to save anyone.

If anything, the economics of turning things green is that we’re just going to pay a lot more for the same fungible commodity, i.e. cheap electricity will be replaced with very expensive electricity, and companies like Tesla will get rich off the hype. (I saved the planet with my EV, woot woot).

### Lastly, Why It Doesn’t Matter Anyway

So hopefully I’ve convinced you that we’re not going to “fix” any of this, so maybe we should put our focus elsewhere.

Humans are highly adaptable but incredibly stubborn. In one word, we’re *reactionary.*

People won’t leave NYC until it’s underwater, and not a moment before, despite any imminent sea level rise.

People aren’t going to fix AGW in the same way that people don’t get their oil change until the light comes on: yeah so it’s hot, what am *I* supposed to do about it?

And government efforts to try and handle this will largely fail I believe.

We have an incredible capacity to create new and great technology when a need arises, but not before, and we’re talking about *preventing* something that is not in any way imminent. The US federal government is also way more incapable than it was 70 years ago (it went from putting people on the moon to at best being banks and car company’s bailout buddy).

Remember, humans caused this crisis by finding solutions to hard problems, like “how do we feed people consistently” or “how do I launch an aluminum tube at 500 mph through the air”? The climate crisis itself isn’t a problem so much as a fact. I think it might be better to term it *the new climate,* or as I said earlier, our Brave New World.

### What We Might Think About Instead

I have a short list that I think is as good or better than any efforts to decarbonize:

- Don’t eat what industrial agriculture produces, find alternatives (something you can control AND stops supporting a sucky industry)
- Making sure the education of women is *better* or the same as for men universally (educated women have fewer children, that’ll help us with our snowball problem quite a bit, but also maybe those women will help us invent our way out of this problem)
- [Reconquering the deserts](https://www.treeaid.org/blogs-updates/great-green-wall/#:~:text=The%20Great%20Green%20Wall%20is%20an%20epic%20international%20effort%20to,is%20already%20being%20felt%20hard.) and making them into forests (trees love carbon, and keep our planet cooler)
- Preserving/ improving natural barriers to storms, like wetlands/[mangroves](https://www.nature.org/media/oceansandcoasts/mangroves-for-coastal-defence.pdf)
- Ditching our cars and sprawling suburbs for sleek cities with decent public transit (sharing living space helps on the overconsumption, but also not being [fat](https://www.wired.com/2008/12/surprise-people/), or getting [killed by cars](https://injuryfacts.nsc.org/motor-vehicle/historical-fatality-trends/deaths-and-rates/#:~:text=The%20current%20rate%20is%2014.3,vehicles%2C%20a%2095%25%20improvement.), is nice too)

### Religious Folks Might Have the End of Days Right

To circle it back to the beginning of the article, I mentioned the end of days from religious texts. For the New Testament, the end of the days is categorized by a bunch of scary things (demons, fire etc.) but also something pretty important, which is that people are *judged:*

> And I saw the dead, great and small, standing before the throne, and books were opened. Another book was opened, which is the book of life. The dead were judged according to what they had done as recorded in the books. — Rev 20:12

If we spend all our time avoiding putting carbon into the atmosphere, instead of maybe trying to address some of the indirect causes of all our overconsumption, maybe our entry in the book of life will be that much more meaningful.

For Ragnarok, it’s the end of the Asgardian world, where many gods will die, but it’s not the end of *existence*. After all the destruction, two humans will come and repopulate the Earth. Ragnarok is the end of the OLD world.

I’ve mentioned the lines from the *Tempest,* a handful of times, and while not religious, it is a play very much about change and new beginnings, also a play about a storm caused by a man (climate change?!).

As Miranda puts it:

> Oh wonder!  
> How many goodly creatures are there here!  
> How beauteous mankind is! Oh brave new world,  
> That has such people in’t

It’s easy to think of an apocalypse as only an end, but forget that they are also a beginning. A “brave new” beginning.
