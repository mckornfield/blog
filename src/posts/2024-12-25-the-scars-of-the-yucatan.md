## The Earth and Water

65 million years ago, as you might know, the epoch of the dinosaurs ended with a catastrophic meeting of two astronomical bodies, an asteroid and our Earth.

What you might not have known is that this catastrophic event left certain footprints, certain *scars* on the region it struck, [the Yucatán peninsula in Southern Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicxulub_crater).

One was iridium. An element that is relatively rare on Earth but pretty common in asteroids. This was found in a very high concentration within the region, and later discovered in small amounts all around the world. Mostly likely spread by the impact and subsequent ejection of material into the atmosphere.

Also within the Yucatán are these beautiful sinkholes with crystal clear water, Cenotes. They form partly because of the limestone structures in the region and underground rivers, but most importantly, they are there because of a massive impact that created the conditions for them to be formed.

![A cenote about an hour south of Mérida, Photo by the Author](/img/the-scars-of-the-yucatan/1_xaFSvTw2eOL5leDyMx_Okw.jpeg.jpg)

*A cenote about an hour south of Mérida, Photo by the Author*

Like a smith hammering out a flaming hot piece of iron, this life ending impact triggered [a set of geologic events that allowed the Cenotes to form](https://www.lacalypsodivecenter.com/post/chicxulub-meteorite-impact-that-created-the-cenotes-in-yucatan-pen).

![Photo of the Yucatán, as well as where the cenotes are, courtesy of NASA](/img/the-scars-of-the-yucatan/0_1Ud_2iw4UMY5QAQr.jpg.jpg)

*Photo of the Yucatán, as well as where the cenotes are, courtesy of NASA*

These scars left behind a geologic wonder, but also a place that was ritualistically important to the people that occupied this region, the Maya. And the Maya bear scars of their own.

## The Culture

In 1492, Columbus set sail got a bit lost and landed on the island of what would become Hispaniola. A few decades later, in 1519 Hernán Cortés would take a small contingent of Spanish troops and topple the Aztec empire centered in Tenochtitlan.

But he landed in the Yucatán, and he along with one of his officers, Francisco de Montejo, would begin the conquest of the Mayan people.

Cortés knocked over some Mayan temples and put up the cross, as they slaughtered natives for [Glory, God and Gold](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1496820). Though the natives resisted, [Guns, Germs and Steel](https://www.amazon.com/Guns-Germs-Steel-Fates-Societies/dp/0393317552) (and Horses and the written word) helped the *conquistadores* to decisive victory over the natives.

Francisco de Montejo is known for having established dominion over the Yucatán in an official capacity, with Mérida as its seat. The city was named after Mérida, Spain, where a set of Roman ruins reminded the invading Spaniards of the Mayan ruins they faced.

![The side of a church in Mérida, with Mayan markings visible in the stone, photo by the author](/img/the-scars-of-the-yucatan/1_I_734S6muTsJdRrO-H2IMQ.jpeg.jpg)

*The side of a church in Mérida, with Mayan markings visible in the stone, photo by the author*

The Spanish built churches and cathedrals, pillaging nearby ruins to do so, or simply erecting their structures on top of the existing ones. These markings serve as “scars” to these Spanish (and now Mexican) structures.

You might think that was the end: Maya lose, Spanish win, game over. But a period of resistance began, one which would have [its last war in the region end by 1901](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste_War_of_Yucat%C3%A1n).

And the Maya are themselves more celebrated than ever, with a [booming tourism industry](https://www.travelweekly.com/Mexico-Travel/Insights/Taking-stock-of-Yucatan-tourism-boom) centered on their temples and structures. The tourism industry also benefits from the beautiful geologic structures mentioned before.

The Maya have persisted but they are not a rich people, living in communities around the ruins or *haciendas* (ranches) and surviving off of the tourism industry. They have also integrated into the Spanish population, with a long history of *mestizo,* the mix between native and immigrating Spaniards.

The beauty and thoughtfulness of their buildings is not to be understated, as well as their impact on the Mexican identity. But the people themselves bear the scars of *la conquista*, and they run deep.

## The Diet

[Corn is the second most produced crop in the world, with sugar number one](http://Corn%20is%20the%20second%20most%20produced%20crop%20in%20the%20world). These two crops are also one of the main reasons we’re so unbelievably unhealthy in a world drowning in processed, sugary foods, built on top of a base of crops that should be used as a *side*, not the main source of calories.

The Yucatán is one of the original locations where the corn crop was grown, meaning that something that was life sustaining has been weaponized into something that produces chronic diseases.

The food in the region is amazing. The best mangoes I’ve ever had in my life were in the Yucatán. The quality of the tortillas is world class. All the meals I had there were excellent (with the exception of one very touristy place).

But I noticed a proliferation of what I can only describe as “American-style Trash Food” lining shelves within stores.

![Chip’s… photo by the author](/img/the-scars-of-the-yucatan/1_OHmyW2nnRu0fn-5-xT-egA.jpeg.jpg)

*Chip’s… photo by the author*

The latest scar of the region is the scars of chronic diseases. With a local food assortment that is amazing and fresh is paired a panoply of terrible food items.

These food items are marked with warnings such as “*exceso azúcar, exceso calorias*”, demonstrating that the food is not good for you. ([In fact, it is terrible for you](https://www.eatthis.com/what-happens-body-potato-chips/)).

The number of local Mexicans I saw drinking Coca-Cola was not necessarily astonishing, but it was *significant.* [Sugar and caffeine](https://nataliesteiner.com/caffeine-the-root-cause-of-your-carbohydrate-sugar-cravings/) are the most addictive uncontrolled substances available, and the US and its industrial food ecosystem have done their best to peddle this drug the world over, which is only to the detriment of the Yucatecans.

Hopefully they can someday reclaim their dominance with corn’s greatest prepared form: the tortilla.

### Summary

The fight continues over the region, now fought within the bellies of its inhabitants. From its people down to its Earth, the Yucatán has scars.
