### Heaven?

If there is a film that captures the zeitgeist of selfish concepts of the afterlife for me, it’s *Being John Malkovich.*

Without spoiling much of the plot, (so still spoilers), there is a mechanism by which people can live forever by entering a portal to be inside another person. And most of those people are super old.

My wife and I hate this movie, I think for more than one reason (all of the characters are awful to each other and selfish, and it’s plain weird).

But I think my hatred is a bit of a knot for this film. It has a lot to do with the selfishness of the characters as much as it does with their desire to live forever, which to me is what’s ruining modern human society.

Let me try untangling the knot.

### Gerontocracy is Hell on Earth

America is a gerontocracy. I’ve ranted in person about this many times, and a [new book](https://www.amazon.com/Gerontocracy-America-Hoarding-Power-Wealth_and/dp/0374607648) does a better job than I will at laying this out.

But to try and put on a different spin: my belief is that the gerontocracy we live in creates the conditions for hell.

As resources (real and virtual) are funneled to the old, the young have to fight over what remains (scarce housing and scarce university positions/jobs) or create new resources that don’t really work (crypto, NFTs, pokemon cards) while being trapped into a phone/screen based life.

Old people’s value systems are completely misaligned with what kids care about. One wants everything to go on, stay the same, while the latter wants to explore and try and make the world their own.

Parents and grandparents (or just old people) have always exercised control over children, as a safety issue and because they are bigger and stronger. Ideally it’s to help shape their kids to be better people. But I’d argue that if the end goal of older people is not to provide the best life for their kids then those older people aren’t saving their kids, they’re destroying them.

### The Prison of Modern Childhood/Adulthood

I spent many, many hours on a screen. I still do unfortunately, but this is the world we have created.

Spending time on a screen has obvious downsides. There’s isolation, lack of physical connection, the inability to explore a physical world. There’s also just the fact that we’re all so fat. And no, I don’t accept it.

![From the CDC](/img/buying-a-stairway-to-heaven/0_KuDPcctnn4dyWp-o.gif)

*[From the CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/hestat/obesity-child-17-18/obesity-child.htm)*

15% of 4 year olds are obese? What the actual fuck.

There’s a Simpsons episode where Homer is sent to hell and they force feed him donuts. [He happily eats them all](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LtEFEdrrXc4).

I’d posit that’s what childhood is, a force fed diet of:

- Virtual Entertainment (movies, tv shows, video games, social media)
- Horrible food (fast food, processed food, overconsumption, dessert for breakfast)
- Fearmongering (be afraid of cities, be afraid of country people, the woods are scary, other countries are scary, the USA is scary)
- Rationalism and/or Theocracy (people who believe in god are dumb, people who don’t believe in god are going to hell)
- Political bullshit (the government will fix all our problems/ the government is an evil monster that must be destroyed)

This diet is manufacturing mentally sick, physically sick, and spiritually sick human beings. We’ve created a real hell.

I see them as adults mindlessly scrolling with a Starbucks in hand, so that they can go to their 9–5 job and continue to mindlessly scroll email and slack messages.

I see them as homeless people who, in states that spends so much on trying to combat homelessness, don’t do anything about the real problem (housing affordability, mental health issues).

I see them as young boys and girls who would rather use a screen to get their social and sexual needs met, than another person.

And I see many of these issues in myself, past and present. And while there are reactions to this [prison to try and be healthier](https://www.derekthompson.org/p/the-cult-of-the-enhanced-self), I think we’re still missing the social aspect of it all, venerating the screens in place of real social connection.

I am not Neo [looking on at all the pods](https://i.redd.it/4ybitang3wlg1.png) (wake up sheeple!). I am looking back to my past, when I was a chubby, unhappy kid who played lots of video games and used porn at the exclusion of interacting with real women.

And as an adult, it’s still so difficult for me to remain bored; grabbing the phone is an automatic reaction like scratching an itch, even in the presence of my daughter.

Speaking of my daughter —

### What the “Hell” is it All For?

Human societies exist to create a better life for themselves AND most importantly, for future humans.

In *Hamilton*, after the Revolutionary War, Hamilton sings/raps to Burr:

> [HAMILTON]  
> Hey  
> What are you waiting for?  
> What do you stall for?

> [BURR]  
> What?

> [HAMILTON]  
> We won the war, what was it all for?

He’s speaking of the constitution in this segment, but the play also reflects on children and kids, as Hamilton and Burr both have children that come up as major aspects of their characters and life stories.

For me the idea of “The Constitution” or “A Firm Foundation” as the play and history echoes, is about the future.

The future is a bunch of people we will never meet, a chain of descendents. Our descendants are our kids. If you don’t have children or choose not to, good for you.

But if you’re not working to make the lives of kids better than your own, what the hell are you working for? To jump into John Malkovich’s head and live forever?

Fuck off.

Ecclesiastes 3:

> 19 Surely the fate of human beings is like that of the animals; the same fate awaits them both: As one dies, so dies the other. All have the same breath; humans have no advantage over animals. Everything is meaningless. 20 All go to the same place; all come from dust, and to dust all return. 21 Who knows if the human spirit rises upward and if the spirit of the animal goes down into the earth?”

The human spirit lives in humans, just as the spirit of animals lives in them. I don’t believe our “spirit rises upward, or that the spirit of the animal goes down.” The scripture itself says “who knows.”

### What Heaven is to Me

It’s what remains when we’re gone. The society that lives after us (or not).

I’ve been going to church and a major, MAJOR (major!) component of christianity is this eternal life/after life. The resurrection and *our* resurrection and ascension to heaven, like Jesus did in the scripture.

I think if we’re speaking metaphorically, that makes a lot of sense (Jesus lives on because he has so many followers, and ideally his followers make the world a better place, not a worse one).

But the twisting of heaven as this “hey, believe in me, and you, *personally,* will have eternal life” is, I think, selfish and gerontocratic.

Heaven to me is [The Giving Tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Giving_Tree). It’s giving pieces of yourself over the course of your life so that those that come after you have nourishment, have shade, and have a place to sit. And at the end, only a stump remains.

### What We Need to Change

The extraction of our children as a resource needs to go bye bye.

We need mandatory retirement (especially in politics).

We need national zoning (so old fuckers can’t keep out young people from buying property).

We need kids to be allowed to be kids: moving around in the real world, protected where necessary but allowed to make mistakes and get hurt.

We need to stop extracting shareholder value from our kids and chuck the phones and all their ilk.

Heaven is a place on Earth, not a place you buy, ride a portal into, or get into because you picked the right god.

We either create heaven, or we create hell. And I know what we’ve picked for our children.
