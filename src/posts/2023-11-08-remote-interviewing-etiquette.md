Think twice before jumping on an interview call unprepared

*(This man already got the job.) Photo by [Windows](https://unsplash.com/@windows?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)*

I’ve given a handful of interviews recently and wanted to give advice to anyone who’s going through interviews over a Zoom call or the like, since I feel like folks aren’t putting their best foot forward.

It can seem silly, given that you’re in your own house, but it’s good to look the part. You don’t want the difference between you and the person that got the job to be “I couldn’t really hear what you were saying” or “you showed up in a pitch black room with a t-shirt on.”

The problem with my brain, as well as other interviewers’, is we get distracted by strange things. If you’re in a normal, well lit room, with a working mic and camera, then you’ll be the main thing I focus on. But if your lighting is weird, your sound keeps cutting out, or you have weird things going on in the background, my attention will start to wander.

I tend to think of myself as someone who’s aware of these biases, but I still fall prey to them. And I think your setup speaks to your level of effort at making a first impression; *if you don’t care about the interview, why should the interviewer care about you*?

Let’s get into some specifics on how you can put your best foot forward.

## Sound Setup

I wear a gaming headset when I’m on video calls, partly because I find them comfy, but also because the microphone is the best thing I can do without having a legitimate podcasting microphone set up. You don’t have to go all out with your sound setup, but you should always*:*

- Test your sound setup before you join the meeting, something basic like <https://mictests.com/> and then checking the sound levels in Zoom/Google Meet, BEFORE the interview starts
- Invest in a semi-decent directed microphone if your computer doesn’t have a microphone or you have a lot of background noise where you’re interviewing
- Buy decent headphones so you can clearly hear what the interviewer is saying

A good sound setup is key to not having the interviewer knitting their brow because they can’t hear what you’re saying.

## Video Setup

First impressions are everything, and unlike an in person interview, you completely control the environment you’re in. Here are some things to consider.

### Lighting

I highly recommend you have a well lit room and light your face as well. This is the [ring light](https://www.amazon.com/Conference-Lighting-Brightness-Broadcasting-Streaming/dp/B08YJPJLFV/ref=sr_1_3?crid=3VUM3WRLNI5NU&keywords=ring+light+computer&qid=1699376709&sprefix=ring+light+compute%2Caps%2C178&sr=8-3) I have, clipped to my monitor, and I have overhead lighting on as well, but the ring light helps me stand out against the room lighting (you are in the spotlight on these calls).

Dark backgrounds for me are kind of strange… almost as though you have something to hide or are calling from a cave. I think you should light the room you’re in as best you can.

Light yourself well, and light the room well too.

### Background

Backgrounds are tough. These days people will use virtual backgrounds or the blur effect. To my earlier point, I think they’re very *distracting*.

You aren’t in the bottom of the ocean or in a coffee shop, and if you move just right your head will disappear. Also I think people’s heads look like they’re floating if their camera just shows their head with a fake background.

I find the blurr effect to also be annoying. It can shift around if the subject moves, and I find it just as distracting as a fake background. (What’s that blurred painting of? Was that a blurry cat I just saw?)

I’m the type of person that wants to see what your “office” looks like; do you have interesting things on the wall? Is it clean and well taken care of? I think a person’s background says a lot about how they want to present themselves.

Ditch the fake backgrounds and the blur. Put something nice on the wall behind your camera.

### Attire

I know you’re in your own house, but a collar (or even a tie?!) goes a long way, even if you’re the best coder in the world. It takes very little time to get dressed, but interviewers have to look at you for 30 minutes or over an hour.

You have nothing to lose by dressing nice; if the interviewer shows up in a t-shirt, that’s on them. Maybe where you’re going to work, everyone wears hoodies and t-shirts. But **you** don’t work there *yet*.

You look your best because you’re the person for the job.

Wear something nice. No hoodies, no t-shirts.

### Camera

If you have a laptop camera and it works for you, then you do you.

I hate using it because I always have to look down (neck pain!) unless you have your laptop on a [fancy stand](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B0B1L6R9NN/ref=ppx_yo_dt_b_search_asin_title?ie=UTF8&psc=1) (which I do while traveling).

I prefer a camera that hooks onto a monitor (since I want to look straight/up and also like monitors).

The Logitech cameras are my favorite (I bought the [C920](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B085TFF7M1?ref=nb_sb_ss_w_as-reorder_k2_1_13&amp=&crid=3HSVQQGB1REAH&amp=&sprefix=logitech+c920) a while ago; it has a solid microphone in it as well if you don’t have a headset).

Once you have a nice camera, you still have work to do. Just like with your headset, test your video out before you join the call (<https://webcamtests.com/>) and join using the software itself (e.g. Zoom/Meet) BEFORE the interview starts.

I’ve done a handful of mock interviews where the candidate couldn’t get their camera to work; you have to believe that if I’m interviewing someone for an IT job and they can’t use IT, it’s going to count against them.

Get a good camera that works.

### Summary

- Get a good microphone and headphones/headset and test them out
- Be well lit from the front, with a decently lit background.
- Don’t use the blur or backgrounds, make your background look nice.
- Wear something nice. No hoodies, no t-shirts.
- Get a good camera and make sure it works (BEFORE the interview starts!!!)

Good luck interviewing!
