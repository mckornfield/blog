### Books > Tweets

Recently I’ve had a lot of discussions of issues around gender identity with my wife, and I’ve struggled to come up with a position that doesn’t leave me disatisfied.

Folks in my family have come out as gender queer, and I’ve had a hard time squaring it with feelings of “fairness” that I’ve thought are important in the world.

The internet doesn’t offer much solace around the issue, mostly people looking for views and clicks on how valid their opinions are. But the short form is not sufficient to get a useful understanding of an issue; our brains are good at *reacting* to short form content, but not at processing/internalizing it.

For that, we need a superior medium.

### A Great Recommendation: The Race to Be Myself

The best discussion of gender identity I’ve come across is a memoir recommended by coworker, about a controversial world racing figure, Caster Semenya (*The Race to Be Myself).*

She deals with issues around race, *racing* and gender. Her view on gender is not necessarily nuanced (she very much considers herself “she” and thinks of other terms for herself as “silly”), but it is very *personal*.

I can peer into her mind over the course of a few hundred pages. This is much more than I can process in a tweet or even in a heated conversation.

A curated, longform piece of text lets me sit with the problems Caster faces. I can read a chapter and really think what “fair” means. When Caster describes having to take medicine to compete, and the toll it took on her, she does it as a person who is completely driven to run, so much so that she will destroy her body. I don’t know if it’s fair, but it certainly sucks.

I might not agree or not have a good opinion for all of the things that Caster does, or that happen to Caster, but what I can say for sure is that she was treated horribly. And the best way to feel empathy for a person is to hear their experience with (excruciating) detail, in their own words.

I’m thankful to my coworker for recommending the book, and hopeful the book will help me better understand what my family member is going through.

### Space for the Short Form?

I think short form content is good for getting “newsworthy” content across, but deeper issues require deeper time investments to wrestle with.

A decently thought out Medium article, or a well structured podcast, is much better than a doomscroll across tweets and Facebook posts, at least in my experience.

A book is a portal into someone’s mind, something that we can all use if we want to have a more empathetic view on an issue.
