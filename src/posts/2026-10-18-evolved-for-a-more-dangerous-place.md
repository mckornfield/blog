### Another Day, Another Shooting

Recently there was [a school shooting](https://www.nytimes.com/live/2026/10/15/us/bend-oregon-shooting-school) in the town my wife used to live in. Politically speaking, you’ll hear both sides say something akin to “MORE GUNS TO DEFEND KIDS” or “CONTROL THESE GUNS.”

Maybe they’re both right (as Mr. Kirk said, we guard our money with guns, why not our kids? Also… are we *really* safer with all these guns?)

But I want to step back from politics for this discussion.

Over my life I’ve heard and seen this graph, or others like it, showing a spike in *school* shootings in the USA.

![](/img/evolved-for-a-more-dangerous-place/0_1cF-TauDohMb3E1D.png.png)

I’ve read and heard about attacker’s motivations, heard the demonization of video games, of boys and men as monsters, and I want to ask: *why the f — k is this happening, outside of politics.*

Here are a few guesses.

### To Feel Powerful

There’s a [solid book](https://www.thriftbooks.com/w/wild-at-heart-discovering-the-secret-of-a-mans-soul-by-john-eldredge/247806/item/53705470/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=pmax_high_vol_scarce_under_$10_17389091670&utm_adgroup=&utm_term=&utm_content=&gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=17389092606&gbraid=0AAAAADwY45hCH_1C7syEtVWUhBg-9-ijF&gclid=Cj0KCQjwlsfWBhD4ARIsACL7p1GrtSXuv5w00cPmjisOzsUTPx1AI2hqTrnV2cqRkd6w4h5KW7po6i4aAsMJEALw_wcB#edition=6132003&idiq=4506568) on being a boy/man that, while it has a religious component (and maybe that’s relevant), focuses mostly on the fact that men and boys want to be explorers and warriors.

Increasingly the opportunities for boys to be *good* and act in that “wild at heart” capacity is shrinking.

Video games are not sufficient to scratch this itch. Sure, they are addicting and can make you feel like you “won,” but they are not *embodied.*

Being allowed to play outside, in dangerous situations, is increasingly relegated to adults only, or honestly, no one at all.

The few times in my life I’ve gone shooting at a range, I’ve felt *visceral* *power* behind the weaponry.

There’s no number of Call of Duty matches that can match, pound for pound, the smell of gunpowder and the kickback of even a small firearm. And I think boys/men can get high on that power.

When I was at work years ago, there was a shooter event so we all got kicked out of the building (scary).

Later we were talking about it over lunch the next day, and discussed how there’s a male power fantasy of being the guy to take out the shooter and save the day.

I paused and said “Well, there are *two* fantasies, aren’t there?”

The obvious hero one, and the not-so-obvious villain one. I want to unpack this a bit, because if anyone who’s female is reading this, they might not have any understanding why men have these two types of fantasies.

### Witness ME

The companion book to *Wild at Heart* is *Captivating,* a decent read on its own, but it can be summarized as “women bring beauty into the world.”

There’s the literal “women make more people/children” (bringing beauty) and then there’s the more metaphorical, women looking pretty in dresses, women doing their hair and makeup, women drawing attention as an act of “look, but don’t touch.”

Men are very obviously marked the other way: displays of high risk, impressive behavior. “[Witness Me](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x2CeDY9Ywhs)” is probably the opposite of “notice me”, which is the subtle, socially complex way that women interact with the world.

Why do men do this? I think men who succeed in high risk, impressive behavior do well biologically speaking. They have more children, or at the very least more opportunities to have children, since women are looking for impressive , well resourced men.

Don’t believe me? Let’s line up two “categories of men:

- Beer gut, bad breath, no job, no remarkable hobbies
- Six pack abs, pristine grooming, multi-millionaire, surfing champ

The first group are invisible to women, [the second group are receiving all the matches on dating apps](https://www.teamblind.com/post/15-of-men-get-90-of-all-matches-on-online-dating-apps-pdf8fjyp).

The obvious ways to achieve “witness me” are with highly risky, dangerous things.

There are the two male fantasies I mentioned earlier, the shooter and the saver.

Let’s take a divergence to something I’ve been reading recently.

### Reading Twilight

(I’m a bit late to this aren’t I?)

I was grossed out (had the ick?) by this book series when I was a teenager, but reading it now, it’s got some decent bits in it.

The overly sugary detail of Bella’s narration is not what draws me in, but rather their dynamic, that Edward is an *incredibly dangerous person,* and how they navigate that as a couple*.*

There is a scene where Bella confides in how safe she feels around Edward, and he proceeds to show off his freakish strength and speed, hug tackling her and scaring her for sure.

(Witness Me?)

The book has these two characters that are highly exaggerated archetypes of the two genders.

But you don’t need a vampire and a teenage girl to express a major power disparity, just equip a boy with a rifle or shotgun, and you’ve made a person *incredibly dangerous,* within the realm of reality.

What makes Edward the hero and what makes the story compelling is that in spite of how dangerous he is, he does not eat Bella within the first few pages of the book.

What makes a man worthy is that, despite his abilities with his hands, weapons or a gun, he does not use them to dispatch those around him, but aims how dangerous he is elsewhere, on enemies and on the hunt.

### Our Current Environment Does Not Fit Men

The world of 10,000 years ago was full of danger. Yes, a lot of it was other tribes of men, pillaging and raping, but there were also wild beasts, pests and diseases we failed to understand. Men’s skills were necessary to keep the tribe alive, fighting and killing those who would fight and kill them.

Men today in many parts of a “peaceful”, western democracy inhabit a world that is dangerous to them in much different ways.

A world in which their power is caged as best we can, with screens and systems that keep men from tearing at each other’s throats.

In this system, they present a greater threat to their internal communities than to external ones.

There are very dangerous parts of the world right now, and… maybe this is stupid to say, but I suspect men are doing what they were evolved to. do there. They are raping and pillaging, as they were designed to do. “Boys will be boys.” (Men will be murderers?)

But not all wars are the same. Iran and the US are trading drone strikes and ordinance, as are Russia and Ukraine. Our highly technological warfare does not produce in men the same visceral feel as pulling the trigger in person or swinging the sword.

So much of modern warfare is boredom, punctuated by technological feats of precise (or accidentally imprecise) killing, at the hands of flying death machines.

If I move down the societal stack from warfare, I see more opportunities for men to be alienated from their biological cores, of a penis wrapped in a heap of muscles:

- School systems where they are not allowed to fight/spar with each other or act in an embodied manner (sit *STILL* laddy)
- Workplaces where men are captivated by beautiful women, but ill equiped to navigate a complex social/relational dynamic without being predatory (#metoo)
- Societies that are increasingly walled off and virtual, so that men spend their time in their caves, with their screens, alone, tugging themselves instead of trying to make themselves worthy of a mate

And so I ask this: are school shootings a failure of a mental healthcare system, of systems that allow a large number of guns to be owned by citizenry, and broken social dynamics that allow boys to fall into pits of despair out of which they crawl with guns in hand?

Is the solution to all this shooting simply one of politics and managing men?

Maybe? Probably?

But I’ll ask another set of questions:

Are we building and living in a world where, increasingly, being male is bad? Where the high risk, dangerous and *deadly* behavior of men has no place?

And when something is suppressed at all levels within our societies, should we not be surprised when we witness demonic acts by boys and men, because they are so ignored by a “modern” set of institutions?

*witness me*

### I Wish There Was a War

There have always been crazy, dangerous men. But I think in the past men had coping mechanism that were more effective. I believe whatever we are doing now is not effective, and I don’t know that flexing political muscles is the solution either.

I think we might need to start over on some societal fronts, or people will simply turn away from public institutions and places that are becoming stochastic killing fields.

In Lin Manuel Miranda’s *Hamilton*, in the opening song, the character of Hamilton says:

> You’re an orphan/Of course/I’m an orphan/God, **I wish there was a war**/Then we could prove that we’re worth more than anyone bargained for.

We have redefined and rewritten what warfare is in this country, as a volunteer or mercenary act, after the public vitriol around the Vietnam War.

I don’t know that I want these mass shooters overseas shooting people, as much as I want an institution that allows men to exercise (and exorcise) some of their masculinity without suppressing it, and then eventually bringing it down as a hammer on elementary school children. I think the military is great for helping men be men, but it’s volunteer, and represents a small sliver of US citizenry.

Maybe I’m totally off base, maybe these are crazy people who, in *any* scenario, would still be mass shooters.

But I keep looking at the above graph and think “over the course of my life there’s been a f\*ckton of shootings going up, and over the course of my life, I’ve felt worse and worse about the violent and rapacious aspects of being male.”

I think war and the training running up to it are what men are made for, but increasingly cut off from.

### Prophecy

We are living in a self fulfilling prophecy. We want our streets and schools and society to be safer, so we control best we can our very dangerous boys and men, by medicating them, therapizing them, babysitting them with a screen, and separating them away from being Wild at Heart, because it makes us “safer.”

I will end with this quote from Luke 11:24–26, that I don’t fully understand, but I think maps to what is happening:

> 24 When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh through dry places, seeking rest; and finding none, he saith, I will return unto my house whence I came out.

> 25 And when he cometh, he findeth it swept and garnished.

> 26 Then goeth he, and taketh to him seven other spirits more wicked than himself; and they enter in, and dwell there: and the last state of that man is worse than the first.

We can attempt to [squeeze these boys out and fill them with ourselves](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/674161-we-shall-squeeze-you-empty-and-then-we-shall-fill), to push them into a modern mold where they are good, obedient worker bees, but in some of them, something more wicked will come to settle in. And that wicked thing will take women and children with them.

We’ve gotta do something, I wish I knew what. But it’s not this. We have to stop trying to remake men without understanding what makes them in the first place.
