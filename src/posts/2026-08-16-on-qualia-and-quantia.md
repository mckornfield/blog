### In Shadow

I recently watched (and rewatched) this very well done short animated film: [In Shadow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j800SVeiS5I). I took away a lot from it, and wanted to put my own spin on it in written form. If you haven’t seen it, please take a look (with the warning that it has some disturbing imagery and is not fit for little ones to see).

The piece mainly runs through all the ills of western culture, and what crystallized for me were a couple concepts in there.

- Acceleration of Death
- Masking
- Quantia over Qualia

### Acceleration of Death

The piece shifts to multiple scenes of war/soldiers, with coffins revealed as being cash filled. My not so long life (33 years), I’ve witnessed a wave of neoconservatism, whose core is the spread of weapons, war and warriors.

Also appearing in the film was overconsumption of meat/food and abuse of pharmaceuticals. Both of these things produce worse health outcomes for all of us.

We’re all going to die, But some deaths will come on faster than others.

Modern society, while blessing us with futuristic medical care (and insane amounts of debt for it) simultaneously curses us with [the industrial food-medical-pharma complex](https://medium.com/@profgalloway/the-weight-6501fdbada15) (as Scott Galloway puts it). I like to think of this as rolling up, along with the military industrial complex, into one unholy system, *the acceleration of death.*

To accelerate death, you need both to kill more people (weapons/soldiers), as well as create more creatures to slaughter on an accelerated timescale (i.e., veal, young cattle), that way you can make more people unhealthy (so they will die sooner) and as they limp along, make sure they’re as medicated as possible so you can extract value out of them and their families.

This acceleration of death is a corrolary to how we used to live, where our medicines were not nearly as powerful, but also the powers that controlled the world had less of a direct stranglehood on our lifestyles (unless you happened to be in the path of Genghis Khan or his kind). Human existance has always had pitfalls and problems, but death’s acceleration for the sake of profit is a modern concept, especially with all the mechanisms we have at our disposal to delay it, but which only enable the acceleration further.

### Masking

A good chunk of the piece covers people using their smartphones/devices to communicate, and while doing so, wearing these smiling masks.

I found an [interview by Derek Thompson](https://www.derekthompson.org/p/a-philosophers-one-word-theory-to) very interesting, which cenetered on the “uni-context” that philosopher Agnes Callard coined.

Our online personas are immersed in a context that’s shared by all other online users, and so the “[face to meet the faces that you meet](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poetrymagazine/poems/44212/the-love-song-of-j-alfred-prufrock)” is one that serves you the best in this context, while often not being your true self.

(There is also the opposite idea, where people given anonymity are awful online, and maybe that *is* their true self, and the real life persona is their mask).

The core of masking is that we are hyper aware of our presentation to the world, and so we develop a version of ourselves that we believe others want to experience. I personally find it difficult to believe that we are better off in a uni-context, mask wearing culture, and instead believe it hides our worst fears and worst sins.

People who exist online as monsters but hide in real life (trolls/child porn disseminators), or seem pleasant and inconsequential online but are monsters in real life (e.g., Epstein and co), are trying to exist as monsters while divorcing themselves from the consequences of their actions.

Masking can also be nefarious in the form of appearing sympathetic, while simultaneously doing whatever you can to entrench yourself and your power.

Liberals who cry afoul about affordability, then make sure their neighborhood is zoned so no one young/of a different color can afford to live there.

Or conservatives who lambast immigrants as thieves and criminals, while comfy in homes bought with blood only generations before.

The mask again serves its purpose: to pretend to be something that you are not. A hero for the poor, or a paragon of property rights.

While we mask for many reasons, I fear they all boil down to what all masks do: protect yourself from the consequences of your actions. Own who you are and you don’t need the mask.

### Quantia over Qualia

In a world of masked personas and accelerated death, we find that on both ends there is a preference for things that are measurable (clicks, likes, reposts, money, deaths, units shipped).

This is our modern, *quantia* obsessed world. We tap our screens on and the things that are brought to our eyeballs are the ones that drive the most engagement, bring in the most virality, and therefore the most (ad) revenue. There’s also a latest and greatest technology, whose only use seems to be to push out any qualia and replace it with benchmark achieving quantia.

A bulleted list of quantia over qualia might look like:

- Money/ net worth over favors, volunteering and effort for its own sake.
- Over consumption over quality food.
- Drugs and coping instead of dealing with issues head on.
- Clicks, likes and retweets over thoughtful conversation.

Part of this is Western (American) centric, but we’ve been exporting these values globally. I can remember in my life the experiences that sang to my soul were not getting likes or receiving money, but witnessing a unique piece of this Earth that my mind couldn’t line up with some number or fact, like staring out into the Grand Canyon or riding the *Shinkansen.*

Seeing my daughter born, holding her tiny hand, seeing her first steps, seeing my sibling’s first steps, these are precious moments to me, that I could give a f — k about sharing in an ad ridden, pornographic cesspool that is our quantia ridden world.

## In the Light

How do we combat the acceleration of death, the mask and the insistence of quantia as the thing to attain?

By reaching out of the shadow and into the light, swimming away from the path society lays out for us and one where we focus on the moments and details, and not our account balance and “enragement.”

Do something that helps someone because it makes you feel whole.

Be honest in virtual and physical settings.

Look for the things that are worth fighting for.
