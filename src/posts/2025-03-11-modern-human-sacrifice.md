### Human Sacrifice in the Americas, pre-Conquest

*A tan skinned man, in fierce regalia, holds a holy black dagger in his hand. The murmuring crowd is silent as he raises his empty hand, open and commanding.*

*A high priest.*

*In front of him, a man with fear in his eyes kneels before his end. He murmurs to himself in* Nahuatl*.*

*With a practiced thrust and pull, the priest tears the man’s chest open, and pulls his heart, glistening and dripping with blood. The crowd cheers.*

Human sacrifice.

When I visited the Yucatán in December, our guide for Uxmal (Mayan ruins near Mérida) talked about how the Mayans sacrificed people to the gods. These sacrifices would be deposited in specific sites, notably [beautiful clear water](https://mckornfield.medium.com/the-scars-of-the-yucat%C3%A1n-1ca1968a0ea8) sinkholes named Cenotes.

When I was in school, I learned of the other major empire in Central America, *los aztecas*. The Aztecs were the great empire spanning southern Mexico that Cortés toppled in the 1520’s. In class we talked about how the Aztecs would sacrifice people using an obsidian blade, [pulling the beating heart out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sacrifice_in_Aztec_culture#:~:text=When%20the%20Aztecs%20sacrificed%20people,honor%20to%20the%20Sun%2DGod.).

So gruesome, such barbarity!

But also, so *personal*.

Man to man sacrifice.

### Human Sacrifice in the US Healthcare System

In *Sicko,* Michael Moore tells plenty of terrible anecdotes about people being mistreated/mangled/slain by the US healthcare system.

The one that stuck with me was [the death of an 18 month old](https://arc.net/l/quote/oravmelo).

> On May 6, 1993, my 18-month-old daughter Mychelle became very ill. She was vomiting, had diarrhea and was having trouble breathing and a very high temperature. I called an ambulance, which took her to the nearest emergency room at Martin Luther King Jr. Medical Center Hospital in Los Angeles. The doctors believed she probably had a bacterial infection, which could be treated with antibiotics. But he did not conduct a simple blood culture or treat her with antibiotics, because our health plan, Kaiser, told him not to. You see, Martin Luther King Hospital was not a Kaiser facility. Kaiser said the simple test and treatment had to be done in a Kaiser hospital. But Mychelle became sicker and sicker. She became lethargic and unresponsive. I pleaded for them to treat — I pleaded for her treatment. And no one would give her antibiotics. Over two hours later, Mychelle had a seizure. Finally, an hour after that, Mychelle was transferred by ambulance to Kaiser. Within 15 minutes of arriving, she died.

I have a three year old. If I went to a hospital and she was denied treatment and died, I wouldn’t see any difference between the hospital and an Aztec priest, except that Kaiser isn’t driving a literal knife into her.

There are many, many, many stories of folks who are denied care, even after they themselves performed a great sacrifice like responding to the [9/11 terrorist attacks](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/12/23/us/politics/sept-11-health-care-spending-bill.html) or [fighting in our military](https://standtogether.org/stories/foreign-policy/problems-with-the-va-healthcare-system-george-rices-story).

In Aztec and Mayan society, the sacrificers swung the metaphorical (or physical) blade, but in modern society, a labyrinth of paperwork, legal regulations and billing codes are the instruments of human sacrifice.

The killing of the UHC CEO scared a lot of board members and CEOs, but there was a [lack of sympathy from the sacrificed population](https://www.cnn.com/2024/12/06/business/insurance-claim-denials-unitedhealthcare-ceo/index.html).

And there’s plenty of blame to go around between public and private enterprise; don’t forget that the 9/11 first responders and military veterans are covered by *public* institutions that have failed them.

### Why Do We Sacrifice Humans?

When we sacrifice ourselves, it’s an act of true giving. The Christian religion was founded on one man/God’s sacrifice.

But when a human is sacrificed, who is the recipient of this gift?

Well… God or the Gods.

When you slaughter an animal, the reason you have to burn it as part of a sacrifice is so that it will “ascend” to a plane where the god can partake of it.

When men and women are killed in a ritual, it is to please the gods. For a better harvest, for society to propser.

When men, women and (unfortunately) children are sacrificed, it’s in the service of a god.

### Modern Sacrifice is Less Brutal, but Ultimately Much Worse

In *A Game of Thrones*, the series begins with Ned Stark executing a deserter. He notably is the one to pronounce the death sentence, and swing the sword himself. This theme of being the one to “swing the sword” lends an era of responsibility and connection to the act of execution.

We think, because such “brutal” acts are not the standard today, that we live in a culture without human sacrifice.

We’re wrong.

We’re surrounded by systems and institutions that demand sacrifice. And they do so in a way that is confusing, *blameless* and not at all personal. A cold, bureaucratic sacrifice.

Let’s just take 18 month old Mychelle again as an example. Why did she have to die? If we unroll it:

1. Her insurance was Kaiser, which means she won’t be treated at other institutions
2. Kaiser’s policy policy is that Kaiser only lets you use its limited set of doctors, hospitals, etc. when it comes to insurance
3. Kaiser’s limited set of providers exist to increase its profitability
4. Kaiser exists to increase its profit, while minimizing its culpability in anything that might happen to people on its plan

We can rage about how “Kaiser is murdering people” or “for-profit healthcare is the devil,” but raging against it won’t solve the problem.

Let’s go through the alternative: what if Mychelle and others were simply treated when they needed it? And people weren’t pushed into eye-watering amounts of debt afterwards?

Then these systems would shrivel up; for-profit companies that operate on sacrifice don’t do very well when there’s no sacrifice. If the patient is breaking even (or better yet, *gaining* value from an insurance company), the company will eventually close.

These people’s sacrifice isn’t a casualty or a mistake.

Kaiser doesn’t work if it doesn’t have the ability to decide for its patients what healthcare they can and can’t receive.

In the very best case, its margins get squeezed and it actually has to hustle to deliver real value to its patients.

In the worst case, it goes bye bye.

What makes this type of sacrifice worse is its *lack of clarity*. It’s not clear to Mychelle’s mother, or to anyone who is sacrificed by the healthcare system and its insurance companies, that they are going to suffer or die so that the system can continue.

The confusion is part of the arithmetic, remember:

- Increase profits (take in more money, don’t spend it)
- Decrease accountability (don’t get sued)

Not saving a child from death, and not getting in trouble for it later is the ultimate win. Profit made off of a human sacrifice.

### Summary

Humans are still sacrificed today, just not in as visceral a manner. In a way that matches to our “modern human sensibilities.”

At least the Mesoamericans had the guts to swing the sword.

I’d rather die a visceral sacrifice than be [pushed around a system that could care less about me as a patient](https://mckornfield.medium.com/maybe-we-shouldnt-aa8b3db840fe).
