### I Cannot Be Anything

In college I learned about Aristotle’s view on potential. He said that you can have potential, or you can realize it, but you can’t have both. The analogy that our prof kept bringing up was a log.

A log can’t be on fire and not on fire at the same time. When the log is not on fire, it has the *potential* to provide warmth, but doesn’t do so. Once it’s on fire, it no longer has the potential, but instead provides the warmth while it’s slowly consumed.

The myth that slowly fades into the background of every person’s life is that their potential is infinite, that they can be anything: astronaut, Olympic athlete, president, millionaire, etc.

It turns out that, no you can’t be anything. You can only be a small set of things. Let me explain.

### Being Really Good at Something Means Being Bad at Other Things

Expertise is an exercise in *effort.* Hours and hours must be spent on a skill, or a set of skills, to become an expert. There’s the mythical [10,000 hours](https://www.6seconds.org/2022/06/20/10000-hour-rule/), but it can be as short as 728 hours or as long as 16,120 hours in the chess example linked.

Still, 728 hours is 3 months straight, 8 hours a day of playing chess. I would consider that 728 hours to be significant. It means that the time spent over a chess table was not spent on any other endeavor; not listening to an audiobook or performing tai chi.

While you grow your chess muscle, or maybe instead go to the gym and grow your chest muscle, your other skills/muscles will atrophy. The necessity of devoting your time to a small set of skills for many hours is that you are necessarily bad at everything else. Another philosophy class I took in college brought up this necessity as “[the inevitable ignorance of everybody](https://www.6seconds.org/2022/06/20/10000-hour-rule/)”.

While we might think we can swing the axe or drive the truck, we most likely not only can’t but we *won’t*.

### Circumstance is a Measure of Proximity

Piled onto the fact that you have a limited set of time to promote an even more limited set of skills, being a rich kid means your chances of being a rich adult are [significantly increased](https://www.6seconds.org/2022/06/20/10000-hour-rule/), both because you grow up in a rich community, but also because you are connected via elite universities and friend/family networks.

I think in this measure, I am blessed (#blessed). My mother is very wealthy and was able to send me to a decent private college. It got me connected up with a friend/alum who helped get me my first job, making decent money, and putting me on a track to being a software developer (I studied physics, whose material didn’t end up being all that useful career-wise, but was great for connecting with that friend).

But… there’s an odd level of sameness to this rich kid, money chasing existence. When I hear the phase “the world is your oyster,” but see all my peers going to mid-size or big tech companies who are both paid well and tasked with soul-crushingly boring work, I can’t help but think of something better.

### A Land of Missed Opportunity

Big technology companies have many positives for the world. They have an army of smart minds and capital to help create some of the greatest technology, physical and virtual, the world has ever seen.

But I feel the creative, inventive spirit of individuals in America has been twisted into pursuing unicorn startup status or billion dollar tech ideas. The frontiers of the physical world (building houses, innovating on energy production) have been closed off to young dollars and talent, and so us “oyster pursuers” are all funneled into the same set of companies with the same set of goals: get our attention and sell us s\*\*t.

If we’re necessarily ignorant and constrained in what we can learn, and also constrained by who our parents are, and then within the last little bit of freedom, we’re squeezed by companies who treat us like shiny collectibles (or governments who, at best, ignore us, or at worst, kick us out). I can’t help but feel that the world ain’t our oyster.

### Focusing on What Matters

Given the constraints on my life, I’ve found that the small degree of freedom I have that matters is, how I express myself and how I interact with my loved ones.

These economic and political woes may be far outside my sphere of influence, but to those near and dear to me, I can be the difference in their lives, a *positive* difference in their lives.

And that difference might be my only oyster.
