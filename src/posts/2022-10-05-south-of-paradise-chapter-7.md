*7-* *Cleansing*

> *Come like the wind, go like the lightning.*

*-The Art of War, Weaknesses and Strengths*

![Photo by Casey Allen on Unsplash](/img/south-of-paradise-chapter-7/0_GsrQMAFwBXElnuOq.jpg)

*Photo by [Casey Allen](https://unsplash.com/@westbeach013?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)*

Our new master, Lao Peng, was very different. She spoke very little, and instead had a slight frown around the corner of her mouth. She didn’t seem to expect anything great of us, but rather was disappointed by almost anything we did in the next few days of training. The Master was like the first few stages of smithing a sword: white hot metal poured into a mold, then beaten into shape with a hammer. Lao Peng was the last stage of smithing, she was the frigid water to help us keep our sleek shape.

She called us to a meeting and said we would have to really prove ourselves now that our trials and induction were complete. We were going to clear out a town that had fallen recently to Carrion. And by the end of that day we set off.

\*\*\*

The town of Jing was cold; not in the sense of temperature, but in the lack of bodily warmth, the lack of heat emanating from human flesh. The trees swayed lazily, bobbing their heads in silent chatter. The chimneys were silent, the fires had long since gone out in every hearth, and the true cold lay in the eyes of the walking corpses.

Now the wind blew through an empty skeleton of a town, and the shutters banged their open palms against slanted houses, each house given a slanted perspective by the creeping dawn. From the outskirts our Wake crept in, performing a physical check on all our gear. Hands went to spades, to knives attached to volatile baubles, to protective coverings, and to the long sabers worn on our hips.

Everything was there, as we’d done this a thousand times before on the walk over, but the last check is always the most important. We fanned out silently, spreading ourselves out in a line extended shoulder to shoulder. We gazed upon our upcoming targets without malice, but with iron focus.

A few “children” were moving near one building, preoccupied with some scraps of feline flesh. We approached and assumed a double “V” formation of five each to swallow them up. The two on the front of each “V” swiftly grabbed the children’s limbs and held fast, while the middle Vulture removed his saber and took the respective child’s head off with one swift swing. The front vultures not holding the bodies caught the head. All made sure to keep as silent as possible. Blood barely dribbled up over the severed neck as the corpses slumped in the arms of the front Vultures. We dragged their bodies towards a nearby building and deposited the remains in the shrinking shadow.

A single man was wandering in the middle of the street, barely shuffling his feet. We swept around the building and tried to spot any additional bodies, but sensed the rest must be wandering inside. One Vulture signaled mentally to the others to wait, and detached his bauble from his knife. He reached back, and with hawk-eye precision landed the blade in the man’s forehead. The Carrion gave a horrifying shriek and crumbled, convulsing. The town exploded with energy: men, women and children Carrion spilling out from their homes and descending on the body. The Vultures counted quickly and came out with 28, fifteen fewer than the known population of the town, excluding the two children and the man that had been felled. The Carrion did not catch sight of us holding to the shadows, nor did they smell us as the wind wasn’t blowing from our approach.

They had attempted to feed, but finding only the sustenance of one of their own, the walking corpses started to shuffle away, and seemed unsure of where they had come from. We circled back to where we had left the children’s bodies, and crept back out of town to discuss. Our voices were hard to distinguish, as the voices of our thoughts had become more real than those of our vocal cords.

“We need to account for all the townspeople, where could the other fifteen be?” One Vulture spoke aloud, in a soft voice. It helped break the tension that came from constant mental communication and stalking.

“They could have already been consumed, or are prowling around in the nearby forest; some of them may have survived.” Responded another.

“None of them survived, you saw the size of some of them. Even if they had started running, they would have been caught.” Came a third.

“And why were they still in their homes? What a strange sight.”

“I’ve heard this happen before, if there are newborns, infants or pets, they go back for them.”

“I’ve heard that too, what a horrifying thought.”

“Enough! We need to plan out our next course of action; we should sweep the forests before we cleanse the town.”

“Agreed, let’s be off then.”

And without another word, we split into two groups of five and circled around the town through the forest. We listened as a being with ten sets of ears and ten sets of eyes. As soon as we identified a Carrion, five would encircle it, four grabbing the creature’s limbs, and the fifth taking the head off with the spade or saber.

A full sweep of the nearby forest took us past noon-time. We settled down to eat a bit before reuniting on the opposite side of Jing from where we had come in. The sun hung just past the zenith; our shadows were tiny under our crossed legs and the green trees moved lazily back and forth in the wind.

“We’ve felled five, meaning the rest were either eaten or escaped.”

“Most likely yes, we can do a proper survey once we clean the town. Thoughts on how to do so?”

“Burning it works.”

“Yes but then we can’t get a good body count to identify corpses.”

“Luring them one by one then?”

“Somewhat dangerous, we risk one attracting others or being taken unawares.”

“I think we need to funnel them.”

“How?”

“Find two buildings that are close together, but far enough for a swing of the spade, then attract them into the front. Let them bowl each other over trying to get to us. If we get overwhelmed we’ll slip out the back, as long as we keep that exit covered.”

“I’m assuming we’ll use the barricade method as well, correct?”

*That should work well.*

*Agreed.*

The rest of us gave silent or mental assent.

We moved back into the town.

*Do you see any pallets or barrels?* Wanda’s metal voice floated through.

*I saw some near the two girls, around the western side.* Shane returned.

We moved swiftly and silently to the side of the building, carefully lifting up some barrels and pieces of wood. The dead were wandering a bit more, their hunger pulling them out into the open in search of food. The sun sat in the mid-afternoon position, and elongating shadows licked the corners of the brown buildings.

*Any good alleys?* Ian’s commanding voice.

We did a visual sweep, careful to avoid detection, and moved slowly due to the encumbrance of our newly acquired pieces of wood.

*Here.* Bailey shared her mental viewpoint, a one-story building set about 10 feet from another one. We moved silently to her location, avoiding any patrols of Carrion. Upon arriving we placed our pieces of wood and barrels on the side facing towards the inner part of the town. Once the wood was appropriately placed, six of us lodged ourselves behind the makeshift pillbox, reinforcing the debris with our bodies. Without sandbags or something truly heavy, the position was incredibly makeshift, but with our body weight behind the materials, we could make a solid stand. Four of us got ready for decapitation. I found myself among the four, with Shane at my left, Wanda at my near right and Jane on the far right. We positioned ourselves just behind the barricade, and Wanda and I made sure to keep an eye out on the rear escape.

All that remained was for us to start luring them in. Shane, Wanda and I turned to Jane and nodded. She tapped the end of her spade against the wall, a forceful but muffled *CLACK*. The house next to her exploded with movement, as Carrion scrambled for the front door. We touched the top of our spades to our partners’ and to the walls, to make sure we didn’t hit the wall or take off one another’s limbs. We adjusted our grip to the indicated by touching the spades together and made ready for the first group. Carrion in the streets started moving towards the ruckus.

Before the first Carrion had rounded the corner, before the first rotten head had fallen to the earth, our wake took a long, simultaneous breath. We listened and counted the number of footsteps, felt the size and sound of each runner, the angle of attack they were coming from, the birds chirping off in the forest, the wind pushing against the sides of each building.

*Wooooosh*.

We moved in flurries. Each cut clean and body center. Rotten flesh spewed over our blackened leather, body parts attached to bone and muscle thudded against the barricade and spilled over it. Shane dispensed three of them with three strong slashes; Jane made a flurry of cuts for each, while Wanda and I took a couple of swings on each. Not a finger was laid on us; the creatures crashed into the barricades and the one monster that flew over intact was eviscerated by Jane from her braced position. We counted ten felled, with more coming at the noise.

There was a brief break in the action; We were breathing fast.

*SWITCH* Ian commanded. *Samson, Sid, Erin and myself!*

We leapt back, letting the four hop up from their place among the pile of wood. We slid past them and replaced their locations in the woodwork while they positioned themselves and measured their distance apart with the spades. The four new fighters pushed the body fragments over the edge towards the town, and crushed any skulls that were still moving under foot or spade.

Then we saw it, either through Ian’s mind’s eye or with our own eyes. A large, incredibly fast moving Carrion. It had charred skin and a demonic grin: it was a Precursor. A demon from the north.

We had been warned this might be an issue, though it had kept itself to the middle of the pack when we made our count, so we weren’t sure if there was one. The fact that Vultures had already died trying to save the town was our only indication that something powerful was within their midst.

It charged ahead of the approaching mob, flinging itself at Erin. Erin dropped to the floor, and the creature landed expertly, turning itself around to hit our rear. Erin and Ian pushed away from the barricade, stabbing and slashing to send the creature back. They didn’t have to kill it; they just had to occupy it while we dealt with the rest. They pushed it farther towards the edge of the town, dancing away from its supernaturally fast movements.

Without a word or a mental message, Kyle and Bailey moved out from behind the barricade and took up Erin and Ian’s former position. The four of us against the barricade shifted to accommodate their vacancies, as the next group of Carrion smashed over the sides. Pieces of wood started to buckle and splinter as eighteen more creatures crashed into the barricade or flipped over the side, dispensed by the swift cuts of Kyle, Bailey, Samson and Sid.

Erin and Ian were glistening with sweat, breathing hard as they performed the death dance with the Precursor. It let loose a horrible cry, momentarily stopping the approaching mob of Carrion. The creatures continued with a new fervor, but we slew each of them with only a few scratches. One closed its jaw on Sid’s right hand. Samson kicked it so hard that its teeth stayed attached but the rest of the creature crashed into the wall. After a few moments, we had dispensed them all, including a straggler that had come from the opposite direction. We were all fatigued from holding back the onslaught of undead, but Ian and Erin were still under attack.

We sprinted over to them, as Ian was bleeding from a forehead wound. Part of his ear had been bitten off, while Jane had a deep scratch running up her leg.

They were barely keeping up when the rest of us made our way over. The Precursor dodged their slowing attacks and got dangerously close to rending the life from Ian, but the creature saw his impending doom and made for the tree line.

Ian and Erin were gasping for breath, but Ian mentally commanded *SEMI CIRCLE* and we all formed up around the two of them.

*KNIVES.* We all drew our throwing knives, with the baubles of oil attached. The creature was almost out of reach now.

*THROW.*

Each knife came from a slightly different angle, balanced a bit differently in its owner’s hand, but the trajectories were perfect. None of the knives collided but they created an arc that the creature could not avoid. Just a slight amount of spin, ten shiny pieces of sharp metal with small packets along their side, let loose in the afternoon sun.

It didn’t hear the silent command of the throw, and even though it was moving erratically, the Precursor did not expect the two knives that entered its back and its leg. The baubles snapped taut as the knives impacted, bursting open and splattering over the creature, as the attached ignitor struck and threw sparks. The missing knives hit the ground and threw down splashes of flame across the dirt.

The creature screamed from the initial impact; its scream intensified as flames licked its back. It kept running for a while, but eventually dropped to try and roll out the flames. We slowly, carefully stalked after it. Ian and Erin caught their breath, as did the rest of us. We recovered the knives that had impacted the ground with our spades, careful of the flames the knives had spread and the heat coming off of them. The creature was cursing us in an unintelligible language, as the blackened flesh on its back wore away to bone.

We fanned out in a circle around the creature, Wanda, myself, Shane and Jane, impaled the creature’s hands and feet, resting our feet on the hilts so it could not move. It squirmed and screamed, writhing in absolute agony. Ian hefted his saber in his hand, and with a forceful exhale, brought the creature’s screaming to an end.

\*\*\*

Our Wake piled up the corpses, dragging the Precursor back towards our alleyway. We identified the bodies by age, size, shape, keeping track with the list Bailey kept on her person. All in all, missing just a part of an ear, we had survived quite well.

The bodies almost all accounted for, we piled them up in the center of town, and poured some oil we had with us over them. We said their last rites, as Ian sparked a torch and laid it on a child’s head. He beat his chest with a clenched fist: “LIFE IS FOREVER, DEATH AN INSTANT.” He bellowed. We responded in kind.

The sun set slowly as the glow of an orange pyre rose to rival it in the town of Jing.
