### What brought you here?

You might be a part of a homosexual relationship, in which case you’re going to have to at least use IVF (if female) or surrogacy (if male). You might be with a woman that has fertility issues, so you need to put your life planning in another woman’s hands. Either way, you have a very interesting (and expensive) road ahead.

My wife isn’t able to have children anymore, but she fortunately decided to freeze and pay for her eggs to stay frozen (I’ll get into some of those costs). If she hadn’t frozen her eggs, we probably never would have been able to have biological children together, so I think it worked out as best it could.

Adoption is great, but you want to have someone of your own flesh and blood running around. That’s what’s brought you to this decision.

### Some terminology first

- Surrogate -> The woman carrying your baby
- Intended Parents -> People who will become the parents of the baby, usually one or both are the biological parents
- Transfer -> The process of implanting the embryo into the surrogate

### The Good

Having a child that’s your own flesh and blood is something billions of people have done. A child is a door to another time; a young version of yourself and whoever else’s genes she shares.

There’s nothing quite like looking into your daughter’s eyes and seeing your eyes looking back, or hearing your laugh come out of a tiny frame. The crazy energy my wife has is reflected in our super high energy, go-getter little girl. I see a young version of us running around, learning and growing in a way that wouldn’t be the same if she weren’t a small version of ourselves.

If you like yourself (I like *certain things* but not everything about myself), then congrats, you’ll have a mini-me running around.

Also pregnancy and child birth are an absolutely wild processes. We had an amazing surrogate and she made this process as easy as it could be.

My eyebrows probably still have not returned to the same spot they started in before I saw the baby join us for the first time in the outside world. Women have it rough. Speaking of rough, let’s talk about…

### The Bad

$$$$$$$$$$$$$

You know on Google Maps when it uses dollars signs to convey how much things cost at a restaurant? That number of dollar signs above sums up how much surrogacy will cost you. Certain pieces of it are optional but some are mandatory costs. These include:

- Gametes/ Embryo costs -> $1000–10,000 (also per year of around $500 to store)
- Egg freezing/ harvesting -> $1000–5000
- Surrogacy finding services (surrogacy centers) -> $20,000
- Payment to the surrogate (assuming you’re paying her)-> $30,000–50,000
- Covering the surrogate’s insurance and medical fees -> $5000–15,000
- The “transfer” -> $5000–10,000
- Covering all the surrogates lost wages, legal fees, etc. -> $1000–5000
- Legal fees to adopt your own child, etc. -> $1000–5000
- Your travel costs to and from the hospital -> $1000–2000 (assuming you drive and stay in a hotel)

So if you add the top ends of all these ranges up you get around $120,000. This is a pretty astronomical sum for most people (including me).

One way that we had planned to finance this was selling our place and renting somewhere. Medical loans were also an option we considered.

Fortunately my mom helped cover the majority of it. I realize I am extremely fortunate to have her be able to support me that way. She clearly has dibs on the granddaughter 😄.

But this is also the cost when *everything basically went right*. There were a few things that we had to have corrected and of course some scares (it was 2020–2021, so you know COVID and all that). Things can go wrong, which gets me to…

### The Ugly

It varies from clinic to clinic, but our fertility clinic said ~70%. That was the rate of success for the transfer. And we’re talking just of a few weeks old embryo trying to attach to your surrogate. The egg unfreezing can go wrong, the embryos can have problems (we got ours tested, which of course is extra $), and typical bad pregnancy turns can happen as well. From all the eggs we started with we got down to 4 viable embryos.

And the things you go over in the legal paperwork are truly ugly. Life of the mother, life of the child, things that can go *really* wrong, what to do when those things go wrong. If you’re an easily stressed person, surrogacy is going to push you quite a bit.

And you end up with a small human that won’t let you sleep afterward! So the wild ride will only continue (*foreverrrrrr)*.

### Was it worth it?

Hell yes. If I had to take out loans instead of receiving the blessing from Mommy Warbucks, I would have done it.

I love my wife and wanted to have a child with her; if this miracle of science and benevolence with a major price tag was the only way, then it’s the only way and I would have walked down this path again (though don’t think I didn’t complain a ton about the process hahaha).

When I look at my daughter sleeping, I see her gentle breaths and know that the anxiety, the time and the money (yes I did pay for some things) were all worth it.

They were all worth it.
