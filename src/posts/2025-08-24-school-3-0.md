### Medicine 1.0 -> 3.0

In *Outlive,* Peter Attia walks through all the ways you can increase your *healthspan*, i.e., the part of your life that you are capable of really “being alive”, not being stuck immobile or brain dead.

I think the idea that we’re living in an era of *personalized* medicine, or as he terms it, Medicine 3.0, is directly applicable to the new era of schools.

Let me explain

### School 0.0 — No School!

This is the school of the world. Or no school. This is kids being taught by their parents or a tribe member.

The importance of school 0.0 is that, humans are remarkable learners. Given a chaotic environment we manage to extract patterns.

We adapted to the world over time to find patterns *too* well, seeing faces where they aren’t and creating rich mythologies to explain the natural world.

But over time, our systems and ideas became sufficiently complicated that just picking them up from family and the tribe was not sufficient. Societies formed around people who became experts by moving away from being generalists to specialists. This lead us to —

### School 1.0 — Individualized Education

This is the first sets of schools. Instead of large lecture halls and mass market education (we’ll get to those), this is basically the apprentice/master relationship.

It mirrors the way you might teach your children, in an individualized way, but the person could be someone you’re not related to, instead someone you go to seek out, based on an interest you have.

Think of Socrates -> Aristotle -> Plato, or a blacksmith teaching an apprentice, or someone being shown any trade or profession, or getting feedback and guidance on how to do their work better. For athletes, this is a personal trainer or a coach, who brings the best out of their pupils.

School 1.0 is the ideal school: there isn’t a “class” as much as a student/teacher relationship that is highly individualized. Maybe there are groups but they are small and still result in high amounts of 1on1 time.

School 1.0 is a *craftsman’s school*.And what happened to the craftsmen?

### School 2.0 — Mass Market Education

The industrial revolution blew a hole in the side of the guilds and craftsmen’s world. We have Marx to decry people being alienated from their labor, but I think the alienation within school was of people from their interests.

In School 1.0, you seek the teacher: you have interests and you fuel them by finding someone who shares your interests and can help you become more skilled.

In School 2.0, humans are set up to become cogs in an industrial system. This isn’t just X in factories, this is also bureaucrats, lawyers, doctors… you name the profession, it’s got an industrial bent.

School 2.0’s focus is not skills, but *rules*. Knowing the rules (be it the rules of the subject or the rules of the teacher) and *following* them is what makes you successful in School 2.0.

Creators and craftsmen still exist in this system, but they have to wade through the muck of a general education system that prioritizes conformity and an industrial system. Eventually they might find someone who *properly* educates them, via a strong 1on1/coaching interaction, but then we’re just channeling School 1.0.

Part of the issue with School 2.0 is that our information necessary to function in society has *increased* (knowing rules/laws/norms/language), while the ratio of student to teacher has also *increased (*i.e., tens or hundreds of students to a single teacher).

### School 3.0 — Mass Market Individualized Education

*AI*

I know it’s the buzzword of the decade. But I’m pretty sure it has already broken education, and the new education system will reform around it. And the answer is pretty simple: it’s a mass market verison of School 1.0.

For a few years teachers were (mostly) unaware that students were cheating using ChatGPT to create content. This model of education is broken: students just use LLM outputs to pass their classes and don’t spend any time learning or thinking. Add onto that the amount of anxiety kids already felt at the advent of the [phone based childhood](https://www.amazon.com/Anxious-Generation-Rewiring-Childhood-Epidemic/dp/0593655036), and you have a recipe for destruction.

One could argue that we’re currently in a weird intermediate state, something akin to “School 2.5 — AI and Phones’s destruction of School 2.0.”

When it comes to cheating with LLMs, I think a small fraction might use it as an advantage, to get one up on their fellow classmates. But I suspect the majority do it because *they don’t want to do the work*.

That is where School 2.0 failed: creating children who are forced to metaphorically “eat their vegetables” for 12+ years, spend it waiting for it that education to end, only to have a job that they may or may not like, which AI or offshoring may or may not take. Blegh.

What School 3.0 probably looks like is [Alpha School](https://alpha.school/), but doesn’t even have to be that.

People’s interests can be explored through the use of a personalized, intelligent, and, most importantly, *cheap* teacher; some variant of a multimodal foundation model, whose emphasis is on instruction and growth instead of “giving me an answer to copy paste.”

Kids don’t *need* AI to learn, but they do need individuals who are willing to spend the time and effort to teach them, and ideally cater to their specific learning styles.

Alpha School only performs 2 hours of this sort of individualized AI education per day, which makes sense to me. Given that with mass market education, 6–8 hours is spent dividing attention among 10–20 students per teacher (minimum), an individualized tutor wouldn’t take that much time to be a rough equivalent.

The other side of Alpha school is that, with all of this time freed up from sitting at desks, staring at a person talking, there’s time for pursuing personal interests and physical activity, which is what kids (especially young kids) are really excited about.

It allows kids to work on their skills instead of their rules.

### Summary

School is going through a radical change right now. Kids and teachers are faced with a true technological roller coaster, one that has the chance to remake school as it once was: an individualized system that worked with individuals 1on1.
