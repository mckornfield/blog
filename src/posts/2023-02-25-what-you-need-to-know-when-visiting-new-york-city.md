A guide to the “Big Apple” for those not used to big cities

![Photo by Florian Wehde on Unsplash](/img/what-you-need-to-know-when-visiting-new-york-city/0_Qb2nNq195gRy2zQt.jpg)

*Photo by [Florian Wehde](https://unsplash.com/@florianwehde?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)*

New York City is the largest city by population in the US. It’s got tons of tall buildings, world class expensive restaurants, and one of the [best subway systems](/posts/surviving-the-subway-in-style/) you can use.

That said, it’s also a city full of people that are going to be total jerks to you because they’re tired of all the tourists clogging up the street. So here’s some advice for how to survive in NYC, as someone who lived around it while growing up.

Also people call it “New York”, “NYC” or “the City.”

### 1. Keep Moving

People are constantly rushing to be somewhere in New York. The speed at which a person who lives in the city by themselves walks across the sidewalk is borderline powerwalking.

That being said, taking a moment to do a selfie or take a photo means you’re probably going to get run over. So don’t stop walking unless you get the heck out of people’s way.

### 2. Know Where You’re Going

Part of keeping moving is that you need to know where you’re going. That means asking people “how do I take the subway to Grand Central” labels you as a total tourist. And it also stops your momentum

Learn the subway maps well enough to get around quickly, and learn how to use your phone to help get to the subway.

Stupid example: a friend and I got on the wrong ferry and got stuck on it for an hour, since we had to go all the way to the end and turn around. Don’t get lost, you’ll pay for it with your precious time!

Speaking of which, if you get lost, you might find out that…

### 3. People are Super Rude; Don’t Take Crap from Them

Along with the shoving will come various amounts of `f--k you` or any other sorts of swears. Homeless people will harass you for no reason. Why is everyone so rude? The main reason is just the sheer volume of people.

In the City, this is basically a rite of passage. The answer is usually: fight back. Shove someone who shoves you. Tell someone `f--k you` back. Don’t take crap from people, just because you’re from somewhere where people act like decent human beings.

Now… if someone looks like they’re asking for a fight (you’ll see it in their eyes), by all means, *ignore the hell out of that person,* and see rule number 1: **keep moving**.

### 4. Know Things Are Going to Be Expensive, Unless…

With cost of living and rent astronomical ([1000 dollars for a closet?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kUMQ-bRxOjw)), it’s no wonder things are expensive. If you’re going to a bar or a restaurant of any quality, expect to fork out plenty of cash.

But if you want to visit NYC on a shoestring, fear not!

Visit some breathtaking sites for free, like Battery Park (you can see the Statue of Liberty), Washington Square Park (it’s been in a lot of movies), Central Park (also in lots of movies), walk across the Brooklyn bridge, or take a stroll around Manhattan on the [Greenway](https://edc.nyc/project/manhattan-waterfront-greenway).

For food, hit up some “hole in the wall” restaurants or Halal food carts. They’ll still have awesome food, just maybe not awesome atmosphere. Instead, you’ll have the atmosphere of the streets, which reminds me…

### 5. Be Prepared for How Much Things Smell

Every time I’m in the City, without fail, there’s trash piled high on the street. It smells just awful. That’s the price you pay for shoving millions of people into sky rise apartments in a very tiny set of square miles.

I’ve been to plenty of big cities in my life, and New York is for sure one of the nastier ones. So bring a mask or take lots of cabs/Ubers if you don’t want to deal with the smell.

### 6. Go During the Right Time

The City is magical at certain times of the year. It can get unbearably hot in later summer, and brutally cold in January/February, but during the November and December, when the City is all set up for Christmas, is one of the best to visit, despite the cold and the crowds. Late spring and early fall are other times of year that are good to visit.

**To Summarize:**

Pick the **right time** to visit a **city** that’s **very expensive** and **smells** and is full of **rude people.** Always know **where you’re going** so you can **keep moving**.
