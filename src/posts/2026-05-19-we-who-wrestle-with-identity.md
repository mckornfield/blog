This weekend I got to meet my non-binary sibling’s partner’s family (whew, that was a mouthful). And their family included people on various parts of the LGBTQ spectrum.

What was interesting to me was my sibling’s partners parents. They were good, working class people, incredibly supportive of their children, and it really shone through in all their kids. I hadn’t felt that much love radiating off of a family in a long time.

Their family was in town for my sibling’s graduation, and sure enough, when my sibling went onstage, their partner’s whole family stood up and shouted and cheered while I embarrassingly clapped from a seated position… 😂.

We played fishbowl with them, had interesting chats, made breakfast, and overall it was a pleasure being around them.

But at one point, we were talking about incomes and children. The term DINK (dual income no kids) was raised.

As most of them were LGBTQ, children were either not on the radar, or something to consider among a choice of adoption, or IVF/surrogacy.

Non-traditional, expensive paths to kids ([one of which I’ve walked down](https://mckornfield.medium.com/surrogacy-as-if-childbirth-werent-already-a-wild-ride-416ef73d8e2c)). When talking about their incomes and the enjoyment they could get out of life, I made the passing comment “kids are what it’s all for.” I caught a bit of a nod from the parents, both of whom work in education and had 3 kids.

This is where I feel it bears discussing the wrestling that I’ve seen over my life with gender identity, and the wrestles I’ve had with my own identity, as someone who should (or shouldn’t) procreate.

It’s not that I believe people shouldn’t be able to change their identities, or that gay men, lesbian women, or anyone else on the spectrum is wrong or bad. I just wrestle with the question of kids, which I think this conversation begs.

### My identity struggle

I have fortunately never had to feel like I was in a body I didn’t belong in, or have to worry about liking girls vs. boys (I’ve always liked girls ).

My identity struggle is much more existential, i.e., “should I even exist? why do I exist?” My brother has a very debilitating mental illness, as does my cousin, and the path for me to have kids was always filled with the specter of “will I create another very mentally ill person? Am I a very mentally ill person?” I have other reasons for self-hatred/self-doubt, but mental illness is probably top 5.

I don’t know the answer, but the path I was able to take, to have my daughter via IVF/surrogacy was one where I chose the less risky girl gender instead of the more risky boy gender vis-a-vis autism. I feel my identity struggle is an echo of what LGBTQ people experience, though I know it’s different, but it’s one that results in a very difficult discussion around having children.

### The kid thing

The difficulty of having children as someone who is LGBTQ I think is magnified, as it can be difficult or impossible to have kids in those situations without going outside of the relationship.

For a long time I didn’t want a kid because of my identity struggles, but my daughter is a true light in my life. She is not just a fun person, or a burden, or whatever outside view you might have of a kid if you’ve never had one.

She’s my heart running around outside my body. I want to protect her and care for her not as one protects a friend, but as one protects their own limb. It’s scary but it creates true purpose and hope for me. The world I am working to help make is not the world that I will live in, it is the world that she and (hopefully) her kid(s) will live in.

There is an unbroken chain of humans/other creatures (fish!?) that extends into the past. Our ancestors. These creatures had all sorts of struggles, largely just to eat and survive predation. Having a kid is the act of creating another link in this chain; I believe it is the most “alive” thing a person can do. It makes me see my daughter not as a person, but as a descendent, a branch on a very, very large tree. And it lets me see myself there as well.

There is no substitute for having a kid. My sibling has cats, and while they are cute, they will never grow up and have their own lives outside of the care and comfort of the home created by my sibling and their partner. They are branches on someone else’s tree.

### F — k politics

I see these struggles playing out among groups whose identities don’t match up along societal lines; people of a more extreme conservative view condemn their behavior and lifestyles.

I believe the lives of LGBTQ people WITHOUT discrimination are already hard: society is built for men and women to make children, and deviations from that have all sorts of difficulties.

I remember distinctly receiving a piece of mail showing a gender reassignment surgery to me by a conservative politician. I don’t love the prospect of these surgeries, but making something private into a weapon of hate and trying to steer votes bothers me. Ultimately I think the solution is more grace for the people who have these issues, not more vehemence.

I see the point he was making, but I think it’s made from a place of hate.

Politics has made this issue of identity struggles think much worse than it needs to be. Politics has turned gender identity into a cage match instead of a conversation.

The specific to me vastly outweighs the general or the political. *Qualia* over *quantia*. Interacting with fun, secure and happy LGBTQ people reaffirms my faith in humans, individual determinism, and my lack of faith in politics and demagoguery.

### #What would God really want? (WWGRW?)

I have no idea. But I saw the love that family had for one another and for other people, with all of its divergence from the norm, and couldn’t see a better scenario where these people had to have their identities twisted into a conservative cisgender mold.

I think love and grace are what made these folks so strong and confident, being *supported* by their parents instead of rejected or ignored by them.

The answer to these identity struggles I don’t think can be prescriptive either way. Accepting everything a person wants to do to themselves has to be mediated by loving parents who ensure their children have the brightest futures they can, and don’t close doors like father/motherhood without careful consideration.

But not letting people express their identities seems equally problematic. As people become adults, I think the decision should fall to them.

I was reading Isaiah and in chapters 54 and 56, (pardon the bible-ness of this) “[barren women](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2054&version=NIV)” and “[eunuchs](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2056&version=NIV)” are both praised and lifted up by the prophet, brought into the fold instead of being cast out.

I think this sort of unity and hope is what we need to confer to people who are struggling with their identities, one that empowers them and makes them a part of our world, instead of outcasts outside the kingdom of heaven.

I wish nothing but love and hope for the future of my sibling, their partner, and their partner’s family. Even if that means no kids, because I have to accept that, while having their own kids is most likely something they’ll sacrifice by living with the identities they have chosen, it is a sacrifice that allows them to be themselves.
