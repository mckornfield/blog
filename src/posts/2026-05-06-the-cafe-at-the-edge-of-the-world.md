I recently powered through the short read that is *The Cafe at the Edge of the World.* It reminded me a lot of *Who Moved My Cheese?* But I think it was less business-y, and a bit better of a read. The story asks 3 questions to the main character, and I wanted to talk out what I think about those questions and what my answers currently are (for whatever that’s worth to you haha).

### Why are you here?

The book asks the most existential question. Not “why are you in the cafe”, although the protagonist does think that’s the question at first, but realizes it’s “why are you here, doing what you’re doing, living the life you’re living.”

I know many people, including myself at various points, that don’t have any answer to this. There’s nothing worse than living a life without a clear purpose, but definitely while I was in college, or after school, or back in grade school, I used the chronically online salves of porn and video games to avoid the fact that my answer was: “I don’t f — ing know, don’t ask me.”

My current answer, be it what it is, is:

> “I’m here to try and help people and things that have been passed over.”

This can mean at my job, tools and systems that are falling apart or completely ignored. In my personal life it’s meant helping friends, my partner and taking my daughter out of [cryo-freeze](https://mckornfield.medium.com/surrogacy-as-if-childbirth-werent-already-a-wild-ride-416ef73d8e2c). It’s also meant taking care of myself physically, since I’d passed over myself when I was younger.

I think the world is full of things that get hype and attention. The world doesn’t necessarily need more Taylor Swift fans. But it could use a bit more care and attention to those of us who have been passed over, but still have something to offer.

### Do you fear death?

Yes… sort of.

I fear death because my daughter and wife will probably have a rougher go of it without me (as annoying as I can be to them haha).

I fear not getting to see her grow up. I think a lot of the achievements I could have in my life I’ve already gotten to (lots of traveling, some money) and now I just want to bear witness to my daughter growing up.

For me personally though; I didn’t honestly think I’d live this long. I had suicidal thoughts growing up, sure, but more importantly, I just felt like my future was very foggy. Every day the sun rises on me I might not always appreciate it, but as a teenager I didn’t think I’d see 30.

So… fearing death I don’t think is necessarily a good or bad thing. I think I would have welcomed it much more when I was younger, but I don’t think it was for good reasons. Mostly I think it was because I didn’t have a purpose, but also because I had walled myself off from a lot of my peers, and sat in my own misery.

### Are You Fulfilled?

No.

I think a lot of fulfillment comes from where you work, and these days I find it increasingly difficult to find fulfillment at work. I think it’s more than one thing, such as the state of software development, but I might boil it down to the fact that my “Purpose For Existing” or “PFE” as the book says, doesn’t line up with my job very well.

And that’s fine. I’m paid well and working on cutting edge things. It doesn’t have to be fulfilling.

I think of my job a lot like long distance running. I don’t in the moment find it very fulfilling. But I do think there are brief moments, or certainly at the end, where I feel fulfilled. “I survived,” is usually the statement I come away with.

My job lets me take care of my family and will hopefully set me up for the future. It’d be great if it were also fulfilling, but then instead of filling my financial cup I might be filling something else up.

The example given in the book is the fisherman story, where a businessman tries to convince a fisherman to scale up his life. But ultimately that scaling up would just create conditions so that the fisherman could live the exact life he’s living, albeit with a lot more stress layered on top.

I don’t think I have that many more years left in me of working in the field I’ve been working in; even given infinite money. I think if I want to help people and things that have been passed over, money can help, but time directly spent on that activity is where I’d want to end up.

My relationship with my wife and daughter I think is decent, and I have a few friends, though I don’t live near any of them. I’d say on balance my relationships are pretty good, but they aren’t a major source of fulfillment for me (probably because it’s been long enough that I don’t remember what it’s like without them).

Lastly I’ll say that I am a pessimistic person, so I think even if I had the most fulfilling job, I might still not be fulfilled. It’s worth acknowledging that the problem is me, not the world, and that I can work on trying to be more mindful or thankful, without changing anything about what I’m doing.
