### The Gates to Paradise are Shut

California used to be one of the ends of the trail west.

Besides the Wilamette Valley, the other destination was the *golden hills*.

For a long time in US history, California was a land of opportunity:

- Chinese immigrants working in laundromats and railroads
- Steinbeck’s dustbowl farmers
- The Miner 49-ers
- Hispanic “immigrants” (you know, it used to be Spain/Mexico…)

you can name any and all of the immigrants that came to California to seek opportunity (assuming you are not Native American… you’re an immigrant).

But the gates to the golden hills are shut: lack of affordable housing and high costs of living have pushed affordability in California to all time lows, and kept its population from growing at the same rates as in the 1950s-70s. It is slowing and had people actually fleeing the state during COVID (though they are moving back now).

![](/img/the-mummification-of-california/0_44MIDAi80N3wEdob.png.png)

What happened to this gem of the west coast?

The answer is simple. California is a state for old people.

California has been mummified, preserved as it was in the 1970s.

Efforts to build affordable housing have been derailed to benefit incumbents and punish younger people or new entrants.

There are anywhere from [a few hundred thousand to 4 million housing units](https://calmatters.org/housing/2025/09/california-housing-shortage/) that should have been built, but never were.

The California Environmental Quality Act ([CEQA](https://www.archpaper.com/2025/12/op-ed-california-environmental-quality-act-fix-it/)) and [zoning, zoning, zoning](https://stephnakhleh.substack.com/p/dirty-laundry-the-racist-roots-of) are the wraps that have sealed the state to its fate, as an artifact that will soon be part of a museum.

### Why Incumbents Can Afford to Be in Their Homes

California is notorious for having outrageously high taxes (both at point of sale and targeting income), which helps keep the state going with an overly large budget.

This saps the buying power of individuals in the state, keeping them out of reach for something like a piece of property as an asset, in addition to the obvious scarcity politics that plagues the state.

But to our mummies, these taxes largely don’t matter.

Older folks usually have fixed incomes that are small, but they sit on assets that they pay little property tax on, thanks to [Proposition 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_California_Proposition_13). Prop 13 locks in the rate at which property taxes can grow year to year (2%). This leads to laughably small tax bills for incumbents, while new home buyers have to pay a significantly higher tax bill.

As just one cherry picked example, there’s a house up for sale in San Diego worth about $3,000,000. The house is assessed at $355,258, meaning it has a ~$2,650,000 discount for the taxes the current owners pay.

They (they current owners) are paying $5000 a year in property taxes currently, but if sold, the tax would jump to $36,000.

This tax is levied *only* on new buyers outside of the family, as transfers between family members can maintain the original assessed value.

I don’t think there’s anything you can say about Prop 13 other than what it is: a huge boon to incumbents, and a huge “fuck you” to entrants.

The gates are shut.

It also is a massive tax subsidy that will only affect old people, as younger folks weren’t alive to buy the house in the 70s, 80s or 90s and hold onto them through skyrocketing prices.

As California has adopted the airs of being less racist (while functionally being as racist as it’s ever been) zoning has kept “undesirable races” out of neighborhoods, as the federal government took the zones that local governments drew and extended them to FHA loans.

![](/img/the-mummification-of-california/0_eg_rzDIiH8QjvCtc.jpg.jpg)

“Redlining” is the process of designating areas where banks would not give loans, hence the placement of multi family developments as well as the lack of any sort of ability to build wealth through home ownership.

These lines drawn around race now have moved to *also* be around age. [In San Diego, the average age is 54, only to be exceeded by 55 in Los Angeles](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2025/08/28/san-diegos-homeowners-are-among-nations-oldest/). And it was all by the same mechanism: make housing scarce, make it hard to build, make it hard to *afford.*

Yesterday I walked down the street and 3 people using walkers passed by me. I might as well be in a retirement community living down here. (And in some respects, *I am.)*

I would say “wow it’s so great for these retirees etc.”, but I don’t fucking care. People can also complain about how big tech or entertainment or whatever boogeyman you want to blame has ruined this state, but there’s only one group who has contributed so effectively to its mummification, and it starts with “Boo” and ends with “mer.”

### “Nothing Beside Remains”

California is a beautiful place. It *was* a land of opportunity, but unless you’re pulling down millions/billions in tech, (and hell, even *if you are)* you’ll find housing is scarce. You can rent, but rents themselves are significant, as well as taxes.

I love me some SoCal weather, but I hate me some SoCal costs.

All of this leads to an affordability crisis that is doing what affordability crises always do: [making people get the hell out](https://www.ppic.org/blog/whos-leaving-california-and-whos-moving-in/). [People are moving to Austin because they’re building there](https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/articles/2026/03/18/austins-surge-of-new-housing-construction-drove-down-rents). California should build too. But I still see evidence of how you have to [fight against its politics to do anything](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2026/04/01/special-statute-seeks-to-exempt-midway-rising-from-state-environmental-law/).

I want people to be able to move where they want to live to, not to be stuck by shitty politics, but that doesn’t happen when old people control how the local zoning and state functions (old people vote!).

This state needs to build affordable housing, come hell or high water. And it needs to stop pretending that more taxes are going to fix it.

If the state refuses to change things, it will find that healthcare workers and service workers (that all these old people need to function) are moving to a place they can afford their own house. [And voting there](https://www.reddit.com/r/sanfrancisco/comments/1gu7flv/californias_failure_to_build_enough_homes_is/).

And no one will remain beside them.
