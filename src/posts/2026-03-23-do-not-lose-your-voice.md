### “**We shall squeeze you empty, and then we shall fill you with ourselves”**

The most dangerous thing about any controlling force in the world is its ability to replace how you think with what it *wants* you to think.

The quote is from 1984, but it doesn’t have to be a despotic government. It could be:

- Your teachers or classmates
- Your workplace
- A tool so powerful, you let it do the work

The world is full of people who want to replace you with a version of you that fits their needs. A “cog in a machine,” but more importantly, they want you to be “regurgitative.”

There are *four horsemen of losing your voice*. This isn’t to say that these things are bad, but rather they have interests that are aligned against you speaking with your own voice. And losing your voice means losing almost everything about who you are.

As someone who talks a lot/rambles, I don’t think my voice *in particular* is something that matters. But the fact that *it is mine* (“[I cannot have another in my life!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GsixD1yafk)”) is what gives it its power.

So let’s go through our horsemen. First up —

### The Worst Part of School

There are many different views on what has gone wrong with schooling in the USA, but I think the worst part is its inability to be *captivating*.

With the overemphasis on standardized testing, and stating things exactly as test graders want to see it, I felt free expression was missing from a lot of my time in school. Some teachers did their best to make things more interesting/fun, and some teachers were better at it than others, but I think *the core of the system is broken.*

I had many peers growing up who did super well in school. They would go on to good colleges, graduate programs, and great jobs. They were “book smart”, but more importantly, they could focus, be very diligent and *say what needed to be said, when it needed to be said*.

I also had many peers where school morphed from play time to a soul sucking time-waster, a boring preamble to the rest of their lives; these were people who didn’t do well academically and weren’t good at sitting still and regurgitating facts like my more successful peers.

As an adult, the people that I find most interesting/captivating in the world are writers/actors/comedians/entertainers who largely eschew the school system. People who found their voice and used it to make a difference in their own lives, and after being successful, many other people’s lives.

*In spite of* the modern school system.

The worst part of school is that, if you’re not buying what it’s selling, its job is to crush/ignore your voice as much as possible. (“Keep it down, report to the principal, see me after class, etc.”)

To argue that teachers are at fault, or administrators, for this system is to be mad at gravity for falling. They’re as much at the mercy of the way school is structured as the students are, though they are complicit in trying to quiet your voice.

School is an onramp into society/ the workforce. The working world benefits very strongly from people who don’t speak up, who wait their turn, and who do what they are told.

### “[Welcome to the Machine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh20cMEgvqc)”

The working world, from frontline retail to C-level executives, is full of saying and doing what’s “best for the business.” This comes across as many different levels of fake, but it has the very real threat of being fired/going out of business as an undercurrent.

What many do during this time is find other parts of life to be creative in, and let yourself spin as best you can as a well-oiled cog within the machine. Collect paychecks and survive layoffs/firing.

But what school does to force you to regurgitate, the working world forces you to conform with an added level of fakeness.

In school, you have *the out*, which is that some day “you’ll be an adult”, you’ll get to run the world, etc. etc.

But you’re in the real world now, so the only out is… death? The street?

The web of obligations and things you can/cannot say only grows as you embed yourself more into a larger company/machine. The only way to find your voice once again is to jump ship, which is a thing most people (literally) cannot afford to do, or to speak outside of work.

In “the public realm.”

### The Man Keeping Us Down

Where corporations fail to cover our voices with fakeness, the final and probably most plainly obvious entity to squash our voices is government.

I think in the USA there’s a misconception on which side of the political divide is more “loving of free speech.” The right seems to think it has a monopoly on this recently, as the illiberal left is big into protesting people and shouting folks down.

But free speech isn’t just about saying whatever you want, it’s about speaking with *your voice.* And oftentimes a person’s voice runs contrary to the narrative the government wants people to focus on (like a person of interest with *millions of emails*).

Back to the 1984 opener, the government has the best tools to rob us of free speech, like a super advanced spy apparatus, tenterhooks in every technology and telecom company and just good old, run of the mill methods of force (GET ON THE GROUND!).

The government is not just Washington DC either, it’s everyone down to local bodies (yay NIMBYs) that govern who can live in what neighborhood. There’s no better way to silence a person’s voice than telling them where they can or can’t live, and setting the price as high as possible.

I have found that as freedom loving/touting as the US is, that freedom is largely extended to upper class white men, with every deviation from that demographic incurring more suppression.

I believe over my lifetime, as gay rights and race based rights have made headway, people at the lower end of the income spectrum, regardless of their identities, have much more in common with each other than withpeople at the top, regardless of racial or sexual identity.

A person’s unique voice is the last thing a large, bureaucratic organization wants to deal with; they want to put you into a box (your race, how much you make) and let that be all that represents you.

Our last horseman is the youngest, but maybe the most disruptive of them all.

### Slop Cannons

Welcome to 2026. You probably assume I wrote this with AI.

Maybe I did?

Would it be a big rug pull if an article about not losing your voice was written using completely with AI? Maybe?

I didn’t… but feel free to let that doubt sit in your mind.

AI in its current and future forms has great promise, but the idea of losing your voice by over-relying on AI is becoming more and more of a reality, given all the other things that are already trying to remove our voice.

I don’t think you can get away from the fact that the more you use AI to do work, especially creative work, you are relying on what *it* can produce with *its weights* rather than what you produce with your mind and experience.

The worst offenders are people who think that creating a large volume of generic sounding content are somehow doing themselves a favor (“slop cannons”). I think you can use AI to do many many things, except that derivative, garbage content has always existed (tabloids?!), it’s just easier than ever to create.

I’ve been using [AI recently to make music](/posts/my-suno-journey/) (gasp), but I think that the most important part I bring to the experience is the lyrics I write, which I intentionally just use a rhyming dictionary for.

I don’t pretend like the rest of music aren’t art or representative of a voice, but I also am not a good musician, so I see AI as the supplement to my lack of skill.

Maybe I’ll spend 10K hours, but most likely I’ll just continue letting the professionals do their thing, all while keeping my voice alive in the lyrics. Speaking of which —

### A Poem

I thought I’d end this four horsemen of our voices with something I think reflects my voice, poetry. Here goes:

> *Do not lose your voice*

> *For it is all you will ever have*

> *A whisper in the deepest dark*

> *Against oblivion, the salve*

> *For there will be nothing left of us*

> *Nothing that exists in time*

> *Just atoms floating through a void*

> *And events that maybe rhyme*

> But before you’re gone, to die early

> There’s just one single way

> It’s to forget what it means to speak your voice

> It’s to forget what it means to say

> “I am”

Thanks for reading! And despite people’s best efforts to squeeze you and fill you with themselves, I hope you keep your voice alive. :)
