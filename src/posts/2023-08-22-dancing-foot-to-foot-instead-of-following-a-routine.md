### Context

The other day my wife and I were at our dance lesson. We’ve gotten decent enough where we can start talking about the “next level” of the leading and following dynamic.

The first level is just trying to get the steps, keep a good frame and not step on each other. Then we practice moves and develop a routine, so that we know where we’re going to go from start to finish.

But the next level is not knowing a routine. It’s knowing an ever-growing, ever-improving set of moves, and being able to move seamlessly from one move to another.

Our teacher summarized it by saying “the best dancers dance foot to foot.” When I reflected on it, it made me think of my lack of success with relationships and how that has changed.

### Fear Is the Surest Path to Failure

For a while I was essentially too afraid to dance. I saw every relationship as a huge routine I would have to dance through. How would I move from one date to another, would we go steady, would we get married? In retrospect, I was DEFINITELY overthinking things.

I figured I wouldn’t even get past the first step, and so through high school I didn’t bother to dance. Too afraid to slip up part way through, or just to muck up my entrance.

### Routines vs. Foot to Foot

I had a few very short relationships in college, but at least I was getting over my fear of dancing. In each of these relationships I was unable to move past the first few steps, partly because I had the wrong dance partner, but also because I was figuring out how this whole relationship thing worked.

The first real dance came right after college.

### A Misstep in My First Real Dance

I met my wife while traveling. Some hilarious things happened early on: I deleted all of her photos on day 2 by accident; also on our first run I went WAY too fast, even for myself. We bonded a lot on the trip, and became pretty close.

We bonded a lot on that trip, and held hands once when we walked up the stairs of a hotel during the power outage. It was spontaneous but showed that we had something all good dance partners have: trust.

But I made a couple bad decisions soon after the trip, and ended up not talking to her for a while. I once again got focused on the routine and got scared; there was an age difference between us and we lived on opposite coastlines.

I was once again too afraid to dance. The next relationship I went for the other person wasn’t interested, but fortunately my wife reconnected with me.

### Following the Routine

We took a risk and moved in, then we married, and we had a kid. None of it was necessarily easy, had to go through the divorced family wedding gauntlet (we decided to elope, still the best decision I could have made), went through surrogacy, and gained a beautiful kid out of it.

But in many ways this was following the routine. I loved my wife through it all but it wasn’t really *dancing*. It was executing the steps to the best of my ability.

### Dancing Foot to Foot

These days I think we really shine in our relationship when we have a good day together. A fun day with our daughter, or a moment out dancing, or taking a good trip together.

We already know all the steps, and while there might be a few more routines to follow, the best way to live our lives together will be foot to foot. Because eventually routines become boring and aren’t fun.

But dancing foot to foot keeps things fun; the routines can always be varied and change, putting different moves in different orders, and ignoring any anxiety about a “routine.”

If you mess up a step? You move onto the next one, seamlessly. You step again. And you have fun dancing, so that you keep dancing as long as you can.
